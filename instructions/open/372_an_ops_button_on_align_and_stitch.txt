PART 1 -- WHAT OPS NEEDS THAT spaCR DOES NOT HAVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Optical pooled screening reads the barcode OUT OF THE IMAGES, across several
in-situ sequencing cycles. spaCR's existing barcode module does something
else entirely, and the two must not be confused:

    spacr/sequencing.py     "Decode pooled-screen FASTQ reads into per-well
                            guide counts" -- NGS. Reads gzipped FASTQ, finds
                            row/column/guide barcodes, counts guides by well.
    OPS                     no FASTQ at all. Four channels per cycle, one
                            base per cycle per spot, read off the pixels.

So Map Barcodes is NOT the back half of this and must not be pointed at it.

WHAT EXISTS (the front half):        stitching, cycle alignment, phenotype
                                     alignment, FOV cropping -- spacrops.py
WHAT DOES NOT EXIST (the back half): spot detection over the cycle stack,
                                     per-cycle base calling, assembling a
                                     barcode from one spot's bases, mapping
                                     reads to a guide library, assigning a
                                     read to a segmented cell

The back half is the work. The front half is the wiring.

PART 1-A -- THE DECODE CHAIN IS BUILT, IN `spacr/ops_sbs.py`
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Five of the six things PART 1 lists as "the back half" now exist, written from
brieflow's source (PART 2-A) rather than from its stage names:

    estimate_read_locations   SD across CYCLES, mean across channels
    find_peaks                local maxima, window-suppressed
    extract_bases             per-cycle per-channel intensity at each peak
    compensate_crosstalk      linear unmixing fitted from the data itself
    call_reads                argmax after unmixing, with a per-read quality
    correct_to_library        unique-closest-match only; ties are DROPPED
    assign_reads_to_cells     a cell takes the barcode its reads AGREE on

`spacr/ops_merge.py` is the other half -- matching cells across
magnifications by triangle-hashing their arrangement, since the pixels do not
correspond between a 10x and a 20x acquisition:

    triangle_descriptors      Delaunay + a scale/rotation-invariant shape
    align_by_triangles        the transform, by consensus over shape matches
    match_cells               mutual nearest neighbour, 1:1, ties DROPPED

VALIDATED AGAINST PLANTED TRUTH, which is the only way to know a decoder
works: an in-situ decoder that is wrong still finds spots, still emits
barcodes of the right length, and still matches the library by chance often
enough to look healthy. `tests/test_in_situ_barcodes_are_decoded.py` builds a
320 px field with 120 reads whose barcodes IT CHOSE, and demands them back.

The field is built to punish the three specific mistakes:

  * 15 pieces of CONSTANT-BRIGHT DEBRIS, brighter than any read, in every
    channel and every cycle. A decoder that looks for bright things finds
    these; one that looks for CHANGE does not.
  * CROSS-TALK through a mixing matrix, so the raw argmax is the wrong answer.
  * AN UNEQUAL BASE COMPOSITION -- one base at a third the rate of the others
    -- which is the case a median-based unmixing fit gets wrong and a
    percentile-based one survives.

WHAT THE CROSS-TALK STEP IS WORTH, measured on a field where a dim dye leaks
harder into its neighbour's channel than into its own (0.75 against 0.55,
which mis-calls that base EVERYWHERE and is not noise):

    with compensation     400/400 barcodes,  2400/2400 bases
    raw argmax             85/400 barcodes,  1805/2400 bases

WHAT IS NOT DONE, and should not be claimed:

  * MERGE. Triangle-hashing cell centroids across magnifications (PART 2-A)
    is not written. It is the other genuinely new surface and spaCR has
    nothing equivalent -- `align_image_to_stitch` aligns IMAGES by ORB
    features, which is a different thing for a different purpose.
  * The chain has never seen real data. Synthetic truth proves the method is
    implemented correctly; it does not prove the parameters suit a real
    plate. Peak spacing, the peak threshold and the unmixing percentile all
    want fitting against one real acquisition before anyone trusts a number
    that comes out.
  * THE BUTTON (PART 3). Nothing here is reachable from the GUI or from
    `spacr-run`, and the ~40 OPS settings still have no category, type or
    tooltip. That is the remaining work, and it is now the ONLY thing between
    this and a user.

  * Assignment is written (`assign_reads_to_cells`) but has only ever seen a
    synthetic label image. It wants Mask's real output before it is trusted.

PART 2 -- WHAT TO TAKE FROM BRIEFLOW, AND WHAT NOT TO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Brieflow (Cheeseman lab, Nat Commun 2026, s41467-026-73643-7;
github.com/cheeseman-lab/brieflow) is a Snakemake pipeline with SIX named
stages, and the naming is worth stealing because it separates concerns spaCR
currently mixes:

    preprocess   ND2 to tiled TIFF, plus metadata (cycle, tile, well)
    sbs          find and decode the in-situ barcodes
    phenotype    per-cell morphology and intensity features
    merge        match phenotype features to decoded barcodes
    aggregate    collapse single cells by perturbation or barcode
    cluster      unsupervised structure across perturbations

  MAP THAT ONTO WHAT spaCR ALREADY HAS RATHER THAN ADDING SIX MODULES.
  `phenotype` is Measure. `aggregate` and `cluster` are Regression and Image
  UMAP. `preprocess` is Import plus `stitch_cycle_wells`. So the genuinely
  new surface is **sbs** and **merge**, which is the same conclusion Part 1
  reaches from the other direction -- and two agreeing derivations is the
  reason to trust it.

  DO NOT COPY THE SNAKEMAKE ARCHITECTURE. spaCR runs from a GUI button and a
  `spacr-run` name, and a workflow engine underneath one is a second way to
  describe a pipeline that has to be kept in step with the first.

  I DID NOT READ THE ALGORITHMS. The README and the readthedocs landing page
  describe the stages and not the steps inside `sbs` -- no spot detection, no
  peak calling, no base-calling detail. Read the source before implementing,
  and record here what it actually does; a plausible reconstruction of a
  method from its stage name is exactly the kind of thing that produces a
  pipeline that runs and is wrong. The paper is the other half of that
  reading.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2-A -- WHAT BRIEFLOW'S `sbs` AND `merge` ACTUALLY DO, FROM THE SOURCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PART 2 says: "I DID NOT READ THE ALGORITHMS ... Read the source before
implementing, and record here what it actually does; a plausible
reconstruction of a method from its stage name is exactly the kind of thing
that produces a pipeline that runs and is wrong."

Read on 2026-09-05 from a shallow clone of github.com/cheeseman-lab/brieflow.
LICENCE: MIT (Copyright 2025, Massachusetts Institute of Technology). spaCR is
BSD-3-Clause. MIT code may be incorporated with its notice retained, so the
question here is engineering, not permission -- but the attribution is not
optional and belongs in whatever file borrows.

THE `sbs` STAGE IS ELEVEN RULES, in this order (workflow/rules/sbs.smk):

   1. align_sbs                 rigid alignment, WITHIN a cycle (channel to
                                channel) and BETWEEN cycles. Both, separately:
                                `align_within_cycle` and `align_between_cycles`.
   2. log_filter                Laplacian-of-Gaussian, to sharpen spots.
   3. compute_standard_deviation  THE READ-LOCATION ESTIMATE, and it is
                                cleverer than "find bright things": standard
                                deviation ACROSS CYCLES, then mean across
                                channels. A spot that is bright in every cycle
                                is not a read; a spot whose base CHANGES from
                                cycle to cycle is. With one cycle it falls back
                                to SD across channels.
   4. find_peaks                local maxima, each labelled by its difference
                                to the next-highest neighbouring pixel -- so
                                the label is a measure of how isolated the peak
                                is, which is what the later threshold uses.
                                An alternative path uses Spotiflow, a deep
                                learning spot detector.
   5. max_filter                maximum filter in a window, so a peak's
                                intensity can be read without sub-pixel
                                registration being perfect.
   6. apply_ic_field_sbs        illumination correction.
   7. segment_sbs               cell and nucleus segmentation.
   8. extract_bases             intensity from the max-filtered stack at every
                                peak above threshold -> a long DataFrame of
                                (cycle, channel, intensity) per spot.
   9. call_reads                CHANNEL CROSS-TALK IS COMPENSATED FIRST, and
                                this is the step a naive implementation gets
                                wrong. The four dyes bleed into each other, so
                                the raw argmax is not the base. Brieflow fits a
                                linear transform from the data itself -- either
                                `transform_medians` (median of the maximum
                                points along each dimension) or
                                `transform_percentiles` (points above the 95th
                                percentile in each dimension define new axes) --
                                applies it, and THEN takes the highest
                                corrected intensity per cycle. Quality per
                                cycle comes out alongside.
  10. call_cells                assign reads to segmented cells, with error
                                correction AGAINST THE GUIDE LIBRARY: a read is
                                corrected only when there is a UNIQUE closest
                                reference barcode within `max_distance`.
                                Ambiguity is dropped, not guessed. Also handles
                                multi-barcode protocols, UMIs, count-vs-peak
                                sorting and recombination detection.
  11. eval_mapping             QC on the mapping rate.

THE `merge` STAGE MATCHES TWO MAGNIFICATIONS BY GEOMETRY, NOT BY IMAGE:

   * hash_cell_locations       Delaunay-triangulate the cell centroids and hash
                               the triangles. The shape of a local triangle of
                               cells is invariant to magnification, rotation
                               and translation, so it survives the thing that
                               defeats pixel correlation between a 10x and a
                               20x acquisition.
   * merge_triangle_hash       match the two hashed sets to get a coarse
                               rotation and translation.
   * merge_sbs_phenotype       fine alignment of one matched (tile, site).
   * match_cells               MUTUAL nearest neighbour with a distance
                               threshold, enforcing 1:1. A cell is matched only
                               when each is the other's nearest, and ambiguous
                               cells are DROPPED rather than assigned. That is
                               the right default for a screen: a wrongly
                               assigned barcode is worse than a missing one.
   * refine_local_warp         local non-rigid warp for residual within-tile
                               distortion a single affine cannot capture --
                               explicitly for the case of two differently
                               configured microscopes.

WHAT THIS CHANGES ABOUT THE PLAN.

  * spaCR's existing stitching is NOT what `merge` does, and the two must not
    be confused. `spacrops.align_image_to_stitch` aligns IMAGES by ORB
    features. `merge` aligns CELL COORDINATE SETS by triangle hashing, because
    across magnifications the pixels do not correspond but the cells do. For
    matching phenotype to genotype, brieflow's approach is the sounder one and
    spaCR does not have it.
  * Three steps carry most of the correctness risk and none of them is
    guessable: the SD-across-cycles read estimate (3), the cross-talk
    compensation (9), and unique-match-only error correction (10). Implement
    those from the source, not from the stage name.
  * Steps 6 and 7 are already spaCR: illumination correction exists, and
    segmentation is Mask. Do not build them twice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MEMORY PROBLEM, WHICH IS THE REAL DESIGN QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

OPS is not one stitch. It is one stitch PER CYCLE, and the cycles then have to
be registered to each other, because a spot's base is read by comparing the
same pixel across cycles. So the working set multiplies by the cycle count,
and that is what makes this different from Align & Stitch.

  THE ARITHMETIC, as an order of magnitude pending the real plate's numbers:
  a well of 5x5 fields at 2048x2048 is a 10240x10240 mosaic, 105 Mpx, 210 MB
  per channel as uint16. Four channels is 840 MB. TWELVE CYCLES IS ABOUT
  10 GB FOR ONE WELL. A naive implementation that holds the cycle stack to
  decode it will not run, and it will fail on a machine where the same code
  handled a single-cycle plate comfortably.

THERE IS ALREADY AN ANSWER IN THE REPOSITORY AND IT SHOULD BE COPIED, NOT
REINVENTED. `spacr/align.py` solves the same shape of problem and states its
contract in one line: "This, not the canvas, is the module's memory
footprint." Its numbers are measured, not asserted --

    a 64 MB band accumulator writes a 10x10 grid of 2048x2048 uint16 tiles
    (a 695 MB canvas) in 2.0 s at 244 MB peak RSS; a 256 MB budget takes
    4.7 s at 513 MB.

  BIGGER BUFFERS ARE SLOWER, not merely heavier: the band stops fitting in
  cache and every tile is re-read anyway. `max_open_tiles=8` bounds how many
  tiles are memory-mapped at once, because leaving them all mapped makes the
  resident set the size of the input folder.

  `spacrops.py` has half of this already: `_DiskFeatureStore` keeps an LRU in
  front of a disk cache (`max_ram_features`), and
  `build_multichannel_mosaic_from_manifest` writes a CYX BigTIFF through a
  disk memmap when `tmp_dir` is set. What it does NOT have is align.py's
  discipline -- a stated, measured ceiling that the canvas size cannot
  influence.

WHAT A STRATEGY HAS TO ANSWER, and these are the questions rather than the
answers:

  * DOES THE PLATE-WIDE MOSAIC NEED TO EXIST AT ALL? The manifest already
    records every tile's transform, and `mosaic_csv_out` without `mosaic_out`
    is already a supported mode. Decoding needs a WINDOW around each spot,
    not a whole mosaic, and a window can be cut from the tiles through the
    transform. If that holds, the largest array ever allocated is one window
    per cycle and the problem disappears rather than being managed. This
    should be checked first, because every other option is more work.
  * IF IT MUST EXIST, IT IS WRITTEN, NOT HELD. BigTIFF through a memmap, one
    band at a time, with the band -- not the canvas -- as the footprint.
  * THE CYCLE AXIS IS THE ONE THAT MULTIPLIES. Register cycles pairwise to a
    reference and store TRANSFORMS, not resampled copies. Twelve resampled
    plate mosaics is the failure mode to design against.
  * ONE WELL AT A TIME, ALWAYS. Nothing about OPS needs two wells resident,
    and a per-well loop is what keeps the ceiling independent of plate size.
  * SAY WHAT IT WILL COST BEFORE IT RUNS. The measured numbers above exist
    because somebody measured them; this should carry the same, and a
    pre-flight that reports the expected peak from the plate's own dimensions
    is worth more than a ceiling nobody can predict.

2026-09-04 -- THE MAINTAINER'S ANSWER, AND IT REFUSES THE QUESTION AS ASKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Asked whether a well that cannot be stitched is a failure of the run or a fact
about the plate, he answered, verbatim:

    "it can be a failure of the run or it can be an image that is genuanly
     not stitchable."

BOTH. The question above was a false binary and the answer is that the two
cases are DIFFERENT EVENTS THAT LOOK THE SAME AT THE POINT OF FAILURE, which
is exactly why the current code cannot handle either well: it has one outcome
for two situations, and whichever outcome is chosen is wrong half the time.

    mosaic=False   silence. A genuinely unstitchable plate looks identical to
                   a broken run, and both produce nothing.
    mosaic=True    a crash. A broken run and an acquisition that was never a
                   mosaic both stop the whole thing three layers down.

SO NEITHER DEFAULT IS THE FIX, and the choice recorded above -- "then True is
safe" -- is not what to build. WHAT TO BUILD IS A REASON.

THE SHAPE THAT FOLLOWS FROM THE ANSWER

Every well that does not produce a mosaic must carry WHY, and the why decides
what happens to the run:

  NOT STITCHABLE -- a fact about the plate. There is one tile, or the tiles
    do not overlap, or the acquisition was never a mosaic. Report it, skip the
    well, CONTINUE. The run is doing its job; this plate is simply not that
    kind of plate. It belongs in the run summary as a count with the wells
    named, not as an error.

  FAILED TO STITCH -- a failure of the run. The tiles are there and should
    have gone together: a tile is missing from a cycle, a file will not read,
    the feature match found too few inliers, RANSAC did not converge. Report
    it AS A FAILURE, name the well and the reason, and do not let the run
    claim success. Whether it stops or carries on to the other wells is a
    second question, and the answer is carry on and fail at the end with the
    list -- one bad well should not cost the other ninety-five.

  The distinction is not always cleanly decidable, and where it is not, SAY
  SO rather than guessing a category. "3 wells not stitchable, 1 failed, 2
  undetermined -- see the log" is a true summary; "6 wells skipped" is not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE STRATEGY, IN THE ORDER IT HAS TO BE BUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. STITCH TO A TRANSFORM TABLE, NEVER TO AN ARRAY. One pass per well per
   cycle produces, for every tile, its position in the well's frame. Nothing
   larger than a tile is ever allocated. `spacrops.py` already supports this
   shape -- `mosaic_csv_out` without `mosaic_out` -- and `spacr/align.py`
   already states the discipline in one line: "This, not the canvas, is the
   module's memory footprint."

   The output is a well-level manifest: tile path, cycle, channel count,
   affine to the well frame, and the overlap it was solved from.

2. REGISTER CYCLES TO ONE REFERENCE, AND STORE TRANSFORMS. Every cycle has
   its own DAPI channel -- channel 0 of the same 5-channel tile -- which is
   what the maintainer's "alligned to the dapi of each round" names. Solve
   cycle-to-cycle on DAPI alone, at reduced resolution, and COMPOSE the
   result with the tile transforms from step 1.

   ELEVEN RESAMPLED WELL MOSAICS IS THE FAILURE MODE TO DESIGN AGAINST. The
   registration output is 11 affines, not 11 arrays.

3. THE WINDOW IS THE UNIT OF WORK, AND IT IS THE WHOLE POINT. Choose a
   window in the well frame -- a tile-sized region is the natural choice,
   since it is what the transforms are expressed against. For that window
   only, and for each of the 11 cycles plus phenotype, cut the pixels out of
   whichever tiles the window overlaps, through the composed transform.

   PEAK MEMORY IS THEN one window x (11 cycles x 5 channels + 4 phenotype
   channels) x 2 bytes, plus the tiles currently mapped. At 1480 x 1480 that
   is about 260 MB, INDEPENDENT OF WELL SIZE AND PLATE SIZE. That is the
   number this part exists to make true, and it should be MEASURED and
   written here the way align.py's numbers were, not asserted.

   Bound the open tiles as align.py does (`max_open_tiles=8`): leaving them
   all memory-mapped makes the resident set the size of the input folder,
   and the input folder is 12 TB.

4. EVERYTHING COMES OUT OF THE WINDOW. Segmentation, base calling across the
   cycle axis, per-cell measurements, and the single-cell crops the
   maintainer asked for -- all computed while the window is resident, and
   written before it is dropped. A cell straddling a window edge is the one
   real complication: either overlap the windows by a cell diameter and
   deduplicate on centroid, or defer edge cells to a second pass. Decide it
   deliberately and write down which.

5. ONE WELL AT A TIME, ALWAYS. Nothing about OPS needs two wells resident,
   and a per-well loop is what keeps the ceiling independent of plate size.

6. SAY WHAT IT WILL COST BEFORE IT RUNS. A pre-flight that reads the plate's
   own dimensions and reports the expected peak, so the user is told rather
   than discovering it when the machine dies.

GPU, WHICH THE MAINTAINER ASKED FOR AND WHICH THIS SHAPE SUITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"if this could be GPU accelerated that would be even better as it would be so
much faster."

  THE HARDWARE HERE: one RTX 3090, 24 GB, and torch.cuda is available. cupy
  is NOT installed. Torch is already a spaCR dependency, so a torch-based
  path adds nothing to the install; cupy would.

  WHY THE WINDOW DESIGN IS THE RIGHT SHAPE FOR IT: a 260 MB working set fits
  a 24 GB card ninety times over, so the window can live on the GPU for its
  whole life -- registration refinement, warping, peak finding, unmixing and
  base calling -- with one host-to-device copy per window and one back. A
  design that needed the well mosaic resident could not use this card at all.

  WHAT TO PUT ON IT, in the order the profile will justify:
    * the per-window affine warp (11 x 5 resamples per window: pure texture
      sampling, which is what the hardware is for);
    * peak finding and the unmixing matrix multiply;
    * cellpose segmentation, which already has a GPU path in spaCR.
  AND WHAT NOT TO: the stitch solve in step 1 is feature matching on tile
  pairs, latency-bound and small; leave it on the CPU until measured.

  IT MUST STAY OPTIONAL. The maintainer runs an AlphaFold screen on a shared
  card, so the GPU path must degrade to CPU rather than fail, and must never
  assume the card is free. spaCR's existing accelerator resolver is the seam.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 6-A -- THE PHASE-CORRELATION PROTOTYPE, AND THREE THINGS IT SETTLED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Written as a scratch prototype, NOT integrated -- the point was to find out
whether the geometry comes out when the matcher is right, before changing
anything in `spacrops.py`. Same well, A1 c1, 333 tiles.

    peak RSS      750 MB      (the ORB path: 2.5 GB)
    elapsed       406 s       (the ORB path: 409 s)
    tiles placed  181 of 333  (the ORB path: 26 of 333)

The footprint is an LRU of 64 DAPI planes, so it is the cache and not the
well: 8.4 MB a plane as float32, and nothing else of any size is held.

1. THE ROW WIDTH IS DETECTABLE, NOT A CONSTANT TO GUESS. Registering the seed
   tile against a range of successors finds the across-neighbour at +11 with a
   peak of 38.3 against a background near 10. So the adjacency can be measured
   from the data in one pass instead of being configured.

2. THE SHIFTS COME BACK WRAPPED, AND LEFT WRAPPED THEY REBUILD THE WELL AT A
   SIXTH OF ITS SIZE. Phase correlation returns the displacement MODULO the
   tile, so a pair one pitch apart reads as the residual: -213 for tiles that
   are really 1480-213 = 1267 px apart. The first run placed 333 of 333 and
   produced a canvas of 5,323 x 2,543 px, which looked like success and was
   not. Unwrapped, the accepted shifts collapse onto exactly three clusters --
   (+1267, 0), (-1267, 0), (0, +1267) -- and the canvas becomes 26,838 x
   15,531, which is the right order for ~21 rows of ~12.

3. A (0,0) MATCH IS THE NO-OVERLAP SIGNATURE AND MUST BE REJECTED. 141 of the
   504 accepted edges sat at zero displacement: two tiles sharing nothing
   correlate best at no shift at all, and they pass a peak-ratio threshold
   while doing it. Kept, they stack tiles on each other -- which is what
   produced the too-small canvas above, and what made "333 of 333 placed" a
   worse result than 181.

  THE LESSON WORTH CARRYING: this prototype reported 333 of 333 tiles placed
  while being completely wrong, and reported 181 of 333 while being right
  about every tile it placed. A placement count is not a measure of a stitch.
  The canvas dimensions and the shift histogram are; both should be printed by
  whatever ships.

WHAT IS STILL WRONG, AND IT IS THE NEXT THING TO BUILD. 152 tiles are in
components disconnected from the seed, and the across-edges are
under-detected -- 301 vertical against 62 horizontal. The cause is the one
PART 6 already named: the well is ROUND, so a serpentine raster over it has
rows of different lengths and the across-neighbour is not at a fixed index
offset. Widening the offset window (width-3 .. width+3) is what took placement
from 27 to 181; it cannot get to 333 because no fixed window is right for
every row.

  THE FIX IS TO STOP SEARCHING BY INDEX. Once a tile is placed its neighbours'
  POSITIONS are known to within a pitch, so the candidate for each empty
  neighbour slot is the unplaced tile whose registration against the placed
  one peaks -- a position-driven search rather than an index-driven one. That
  also removes `max_site_gap` entirely, which PART 6 showed is unsound for
  this acquisition anyway.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 6-C -- THE LAYOUT, READ FROM THE REFERENCE AND THEN MEASURED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PART 6-B said to stop guessing and read `ops/plates.py`. Doing so answers the
model, and one more measurement answers this plate.

WHAT THE REFERENCE SAYS. `plate_coordinate(well, tile, well_spacing,
grid_spacing, grid_shape, snake_remap)` places tile t on a plain ROW-MAJOR
raster:

    delta = 1280            microns between tiles at '10X'
    i += delta * (tile // width)
    j += delta * (tile %  width)

and `remap_snake(site, grid_shape)` exists because Micro-Manager's HCS plugin
writes sites in SNAKE order -- it reverses every other row to get back to row
order. So the layout has exactly two unknowns, the width and whether the index
is snaked, and `delta = 1280 microns` at 10X against a measured pitch of 1267
px puts the scale at ~1.01 px/micron with a 213 px overlap. The numbers agree.

WHAT THIS PLATE ACTUALLY IS. Neighbour offsets measured at seven sites spread
through well A1 (peak >= 20, (0,0) rejected):

    site   0: +1V  +11H
    site  40: -1V  +1V   +4H
    site 100: -11H -1V   +1V
    site 160: -9H  -1V
    site 220: -4H  -1V   +1V
    site 280: -13H -1V   +1V
    site 320: -5H  -1V   +1V

  +/-1 IS ALWAYS VERTICAL. The index runs DOWN a column, not across a row.
  THE HORIZONTAL OFFSET VARIES: +11, +4, -11, -9, -4, -13, -5.

That is a snake down columns of DIFFERENT HEIGHTS, which is exactly what a
round well gives: each column holds as many fields as fit inside the circle at
that x, so the offset to the next column is that column's height and it
changes as you cross the well.

  SO BOTH PROTOTYPES FAILED FOR ONE REASON. v1 assumed a constant horizontal
  offset; v2 assumed a run ends where the +/-1 link fails. Neither is true: the
  offset varies by column, and at a snake TURN the consecutive pair i, i+1 is
  physically adjacent HORIZONTALLY rather than vertically -- so v2 merged
  columns across turns, which is where its runs of 34 and 42 came from.

  AND SITE 160 HAS NO `+1` AT ALL, so the +/-1 chain is broken in places too:
  the snake does not always land the next index against the last one.

WHAT TO BUILD, and it is now a small algorithm rather than a guess:
  1. Register every (i, i+1) and KEEP THE DIRECTION. A V link continues a
     column; an H link is a turn and is still a real edge.
  2. Columns are the maximal runs of V links. Their heights are the data, not
     a parameter.
  3. Join columns by their heights: the horizontal neighbour of the top of
     column c is the top of column c+1, which is at index offset = height(c).
     Verify by registration rather than trusting it.
  4. Fill the few breaks (site 160's missing +1) from the column geometry
     once steps 1-3 have placed their neighbours.

  AND CHECK IT AGAINST `plate_coordinate` RATHER THAN AGAINST A COUNT. With
  the column heights known, the reference gives the expected micron position
  of every tile; the residual against the measured positions is the honest
  score. PART 6-A's warning stands -- a placement count is not a measure of a
  stitch.


  V3 WAS TRIED AND IS ALSO WRONG, recorded so the fourth attempt does not
  repeat it. Implementing steps 1-3 above gives:

      chain: 308 of 332 consecutive pairs register (301 V, 7 H)
      columns: 32, heights 5, 9, 13, 15, 17, 17, 2, 17, 1, 18, 21, 21, ...
      column joins found: 10 of 32
      tiles placed: 27 of 333

  THE CHAIN IS NEARLY COMPLETE -- 308 of 332 -- AND THAT IS THE POINT. The
  24 breaks are not column turns; only 7 links are H, so the snake turns 7
  times, not 31. "Column = maximal run of V" therefore splits at every BREAK
  as well as at every turn, which manufactures columns of height 1 and 2 and
  makes `member + height(c)` a meaningless join offset.

  SO BOTTOM-UP GRAPH RECONSTRUCTION HAS NOW FAILED THREE WAYS, and the common
  cause is that each version infers the grid from the very links that are
  missing. THE NEXT ATTEMPT SHOULD GO TOP-DOWN INSTEAD: hypothesise a grid
  from `plate_coordinate` -- width, height, snake or not -- PREDICT every
  tile's position, and score the hypothesis by how many predicted neighbour
  pairs actually register. A few dozen hypotheses, each scored on a sample,
  and the best one places all 333 by construction rather than by traversal.
  That also uses the reference implementation as it was meant to be used: as
  the model, not as documentation.

  THREE PROTOTYPES, THREE HOURS, AND THE MEASUREMENTS ARE THE YIELD. They are
  listed at the end of PART 6-B and none of them depends on the adjacency
  being solved. The adjacency is the open problem; the memory ceiling, the
  matcher, the wrapping and the (0,0) rejection are settled.

PART 6 -- THE OUTPUT IS COORDINATES, NOT PIXELS. THE MAINTAINER'S DESIGN,
2026-09-09, WITH THE MEASUREMENTS THAT FORCE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MEASURED ON THE REAL SCREEN, /nas_mnt/data/ops/OpticalPooledScreens_data/
screenA/20200202_6W-LaC024A:

    sequencing site   (5, 1480, 1480) uint16    22 MB   11 cycles, c1..c11
    phenotype site    (4, 2960, 2960) uint16    70 MB   23,071 files
    sites in c1       1,998

    ALL 11 CYCLES HELD AT ONCE                  481 GB   <-- the wall

481 GB against a 125 GB machine, on a maintainer's ceiling of 100 GB. The
phenotype is exactly 2x the sequencing edge, which makes the
cross-magnification transform a clean factor of two.

WHY THERE IS NO `merged/` FOLDER FOR OPS, and this is the whole of PART 6.
Measure consumes `merged/`: one `.npy` per FIELD, channels plus mask stacks,
and `_measure_crop_core` iterates one file at a time. The FIELD is the unit.

Once tiles are aligned into a well mosaic there is no field any more. Writing
`merged/` would mean INVENTING pseudo-fields by re-tiling the mosaic on an
arbitrary grid, and every cut lands where the grid says rather than where the
biology is. In the maintainer's words: it "would cut tons of objects in half
at the image borders". A half cell still measures as a cell, so the loss is
silent -- and for OPS it is worse than silent, because a cell cut in half is
a cell whose barcode cannot be trusted.

SO THE ALIGNMENT RUN PERSISTS COORDINATES AND CUTS NOTHING. Measure streams:
given an object, it reads that object's pixels from whichever source tiles
cover it, assembling across a seam when it spans one. Three additions:

  1. A COORDINATE ARTIFACT -- the per-tile transform and the phenotype <->
     sequencing mapping. It is also what makes a re-run cheap, since scoring
     is the expensive half.
  2. A NEW OBJECT IMPORT FOR MEASURE whose unit is the OBJECT, not the
     field, resolving mosaic coordinates back to source tiles on demand.
  3. AN OPS SETTINGS CATEGORY in measure, and the sequencing reads as their
     OWN TABLE in measurements.db. Barcodes are not morphology and do not
     belong in the object feature table.

Everything downstream -- annotate, classify, regression, the crop PNGs --
keeps working unchanged, because all of it reads measurements.db and
per-object crops, which this still produces.

TWO DECISIONS, ANSWERED BY THE MAINTAINER 2026-09-09:

  SEGMENT PER TILE, THEN MERGE ACROSS SEAMS. Not on a streamed mosaic
  window. Memory stays per-tile, which is the only version that fits the
  numbers above.

  AN OBJECT AT A SEAM IS MERGED INTO ONE OBJECT. Its fragments are unioned
  through the alignment transform, measured once, and written as one row.
  Rejected: keeping whichever tile holds the centroid (loses pixels, so area
  and intensity are wrong for exactly the cells that straddle a seam), and
  emitting both fragments with a flag (puts duplicate-looking rows in
  measurements.db that every consumer then has to handle).

  THE MERGE RULE IS THE RISK and is to be written with that in mind: it can
  be wrong in both directions -- fusing two real neighbours across a seam, or
  failing to fuse one cell and counting it twice. Whatever is built must be
  measurable against planted truth before it is believed, the same way PART
  1-A's decode chain was.

GPU ACCELERATION, ASKED FOR THE SAME DAY: "allignment seems like a matrix
multiplication problem". It is. Three hot spots, and they suit it
differently:

    spacrops        ORB detect + Hamming descriptor matching, N x M   excellent
    ops_merge       align_by_triangles: descriptor match + voting     excellent
    ops_merge       match_cells: mutual nearest neighbour             good

  BACKEND CHOSEN BY THE MAINTAINER: PyTorch first, CuPy second, NumPy always.
  Torch is already a dependency (Cellpose), so nothing new is installed, and
  one code path reaches CUDA, ROCm and Apple MPS. Measured here: torch
  2.6.0+cu124 on an RTX 3090; CuPy is NOT installed, so the live chain is
  Torch -> NumPy.

  EVERY ACCELERATED FUNCTION TAKES AND RETURNS NUMPY, so no caller learns
  which backend ran, and every one gets a test that runs BOTH paths on the
  same input and asserts they agree. Not "the GPU version passes its own
  test": the two must agree, or the fallback is not a fallback, it is a
  second implementation nobody compares.

PART 7 -- WHAT IS AVERAGED, AND WHAT MUST NEVER BE. 2026-09-09.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CORRECTING A WARNING THAT WAS TOO BROAD. I raised "do not average across
sequencing cycles -- c1..c11 carry different base calls and averaging
destroys the barcode". The maintainer's answer: "when i say average i only
mean the hoechst channel", and that changes the conclusion rather than
qualifying it.

    channel                     across overlapping tiles   across cycles
    Hoechst / DAPI              average                    AVERAGE
    CY3 / A594 / CY5 / CY7      average within a cycle     NEVER -- stack
    phenotype GFP/A594/AF750    average within acquisition  n/a, one run

AVERAGING HOECHST ACROSS CYCLES IS NOT MERELY SAFE, IT IS THE RIGHT THING.
The nuclei do not move between cycles, so eleven acquisitions of one DAPI
field are eleven samples of ONE signal: averaging them is roughly a sqrt(11),
about 3.3x, improvement in signal-to-noise on the exact channel segmentation
runs on. The warning applies only to the base-call channels, where averaging
would destroy the read.

SO THERE IS ONE CANONICAL OBJECT MAP. Segment once, on Hoechst averaged
across every cycle and every overlapping tile; then sample each cycle's base
channels at those coordinates. Two consequences worth stating because they
are what makes OPS work:

  * segmentation is better than any single cycle could give, on the composite
    rather than on one noisy field;
  * every cycle's reads are attributed to the SAME object identity, which is
    the whole requirement for assembling a barcode.

PHENOTYPE AND SEQUENCING HOECHST ARE ALIGNED BUT NOT AVERAGED TOGETHER.
2960x2960 against 1480x1480: different optics and different sampling, so
averaging across that boundary means resampling one into the other and
blurring whichever is upsampled. Align them, keep them separate. The exact
factor of two is a convenience for the transform, not a licence to merge the
pixels.

THE SETTINGS MUST SAY THIS EXPLICITLY -- which channel averages across what
-- because "average the overlap" is the kind of instruction that gets wired
to every channel by whoever implements it next, and a barcode destroyed by
averaging fails silently: the reads still decode, they just decode to the
wrong thing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 8 -- THE ORDER OF WORK, AND WHERE IT IS WRITTEN. 2026-09-09.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TWO THINGS THE MAINTAINER FIXED IN THE PLAN, and both change the shape of it
rather than adding to it.

1. HOECHST IS PROCESSED FIRST AND ALONE. It is the alignment channel, and it
   is also the channel the object map is built on. NOTHING else is touched
   until the nuclei are segmented and given plate-level object numbers,
   because those numbers and those coordinates are the only thing that links
   a sequencing read to a phenotype measurement. A pipeline that segments
   and calls bases in one pass has nothing to join on.

2. IT ALL LANDS IN measurements.db. An OPS table set in the same database
   every other module reads, so `measure`, the plotting, the ML and the DB
   browser reach it without learning a new format. Parquet (and HDF5 for raw
   arrays) alongside it where the scan speed is worth it -- but the database
   is the authority and the sidecar is a cache, never the other way round.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE A -- GEOMETRY, ON HOECHST ONLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1  LAYOUT FROM THE ACQUISITION, NOT FROM A GUESS. PART 6-C settled the
    model: row-major with a snake remap, columns of varying height because
    the well is round. Site -> (column, row) is read or measured, never
    assumed, and `max_site_gap` goes away.

A2  TILE STITCH, PER WELL PER CYCLE. Phase correlation on Hoechst; unwrap
    the shifts; reject (0,0) as the no-overlap signature; solve the edge
    graph by least squares into one well frame. Output is a TRANSFORM TABLE,
    never a canvas. It prints canvas dimensions and a shift histogram --
    PART 6-A: a placement count is not a measure of a stitch.

A3  CYCLE REGISTRATION. Each cycle's Hoechst against the reference cycle in
    the well frame -> one rigid transform per cycle, composed with A2.
    A cycle that fails to register is DROPPED FROM THE AVERAGE AND RECORDED,
    not allowed to fail the well.

A4  PHENOTYPE TO SBS. 2960 against 1480: scale and rotation are real here, so
    this is the one place feature matching earns its keep. Align on Hoechst,
    store the transform, and do not resample one into the other -- PART 7:
    aligned, not averaged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE B -- THE CANONICAL NUCLEAR MAP. EVERYTHING WAITS ON THIS.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

B1  THE HOECHST COMPOSITE. Averaged across overlapping tiles AND across every
    cycle. Nuclei do not move between cycles, so eleven acquisitions are
    eleven samples of one signal: about a 3.3x gain on the exact channel
    segmentation runs on. HOECHST ONLY -- PART 7.

B2  SEGMENT ONCE, on the composite, window by window.

B3  SEW THE LABELS ACROSS WINDOW SEAMS. An object straddling a boundary is
    one object, matched on its well-frame centroid.

B4  PLATE-LEVEL OBJECT NUMBERS, ASSIGNED IN THE WELL FRAME. Per-window
    numbering would give two windows an object #1 apiece. This id, and the
    centroid beside it, is the join key for every later phase.

B5  WRITTEN AND VERIFIED BEFORE PHASE C STARTS. The gate is explicit: no
    sequencing or phenotype channel is read until `ops_objects` is on disk
    and its row count checked.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE C -- EVERY OTHER CHANNEL, SAMPLED AT THOSE COORDINATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

C1  SEQUENCING CHANNELS, PER CYCLE, at the B4 coordinates through the A2/A3
    transforms. NEVER averaged across cycles -- that is the one operation
    that destroys a barcode while leaving it decodable.

C2  SPOTS AND BASE CALLS per object per cycle, with a quality.

C3  BARCODE ASSEMBLY across cycles BY OBJECT ID, to a read, a quality and a
    mapped guide.

C4  PHENOTYPE CHANNELS at the same object ids through A4, emitting the schema
    `measure` already emits -- which is what makes the OPS output usable by
    everything downstream without a translation layer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE D -- THE STORAGE CONTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

IN measurements.db, authoritative:

    ops_geometry   well, cycle, site, transform, residual, accepted, method
    ops_objects    plate, well, object_id, centroid_x, centroid_y, area,
                   bbox, window, n_cycles_averaged
    ops_reads      object_id, cycle, per-channel intensity, base, quality
    ops_barcodes   object_id, barcode, mean quality, n_cycles, mapped guide

and the ordinary measurement tables keyed on THE SAME object ids, so nothing
downstream needs to know the run was OPS.

BESIDE IT, as a cache: parquet for `ops_reads` and `ops_barcodes`, which are
the wide numeric tables where a columnar scan is worth an order of magnitude;
HDF5 only if raw per-window arrays are ever kept. Written in the same step,
with the row counts asserted equal, so the sidecar cannot silently drift from
the authority. The reader prefers parquet and falls back to sqlite.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE E -- GPU, WITH A FALLBACK THAT IS TESTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase correlation is an FFT and an argmax, so the backend is an indirection
over the array module: CuPy (CUDA) -> torch.fft (CUDA / ROCm / MPS) ->
numpy+scipy. The CPU path is not an error path; both backends run the same
fixture and must agree to within a pixel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THREE HOLES IN THIS ORDER, NAMED BEFORE THEY BITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. The nuclear gate means every cycle's geometry must be solved before B1
   can start. Hence A3's rule: a cycle that will not register is dropped
   from the average and recorded, never fatal to the well.

2. Sampling the sequencing channels at nuclear coordinates assumes the reads
   are nuclear. In this protocol they are -- but the footprint (nucleus,
   dilated nucleus, cytoplasmic ring) must be a SETTING, because it is the
   assumption that would mis-call bases without ever looking wrong.

3. `number_of_objects` is assigned in the well frame (B4). Anywhere it is
   assigned per window, two windows produce the same id and the join silently
   pairs the wrong cells.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 9 -- THE HOLES IN PART 8, CLOSED. 2026-09-09.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PART 8 named three holes and left them named. That is not a specification --
"this could go wrong" is a note to whoever implements it, and whoever
implements it will decide these three by default and not record the decision.
So each one is decided here, with the setting that carries it and the way it
fails. NOTHING BELOW IS BUILT YET; this is the contract implementation is
held to.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOLE 1 -- A CYCLE THAT WILL NOT REGISTER MUST NOT COST THE WELL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The gate in PART 8 makes every cycle a dependency of the object map: no
composite until all eleven are placed. One bad acquisition then costs a whole
well, which is not acceptable for a plate that took a week to run.

DECIDED:

1. EVERY CYCLE REGISTERS AGAINST THE REFERENCE DIRECTLY, never against its
   predecessor. Chaining c1->c2->c3 is cheaper by one registration and wrong:
   a single bad link displaces every cycle after it, and the residual does not
   say which link failed. Direct registration makes each cycle's acceptance
   independent, which is the only reason the rest of this is possible.

2. ACCEPTANCE IS RECORDED, NOT ASSUMED. `ops_geometry` carries the peak ratio
   and the residual for every cycle, and an `accepted` flag decided by
   `ops_min_peak_ratio` (the measured contrast is 38-46 for a true neighbour
   against a background near 10, so the threshold has real room -- PART 6).

3. A REJECTED CYCLE IS DROPPED FROM THE COMPOSITE AND FROM THE READS. Not
   from the composite only: if its geometry is not trustworthy then its
   coordinates are not either, and base calls sampled at wrong coordinates
   decode to a wrong barcode rather than to nothing. Both halves go, and
   `ops_barcodes.n_cycles` records what the read was actually assembled from.

4. THE WELL FAILS LOUDLY BELOW `ops_min_cycles_for_composite` (default 3).
   Segmenting on an average of two noisy fields and calling it the canonical
   object map is worse than stopping.

5. THE COMPOSITE IS A WEIGHTED MEAN AND CARRIES ITS SAMPLE COUNT. Tile
   overlaps and the well's round edge mean the number of contributing
   (tile, cycle) samples varies across the field, and a nucleus segmented
   where one sample landed is not the same evidence as one segmented where
   eleven did. The count map is kept and `ops_objects.n_samples_mean` records
   it per object, so a later filter can ask.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOLE 2 -- "THE READS ARE NUCLEAR" IS AN ASSUMPTION, SO MEASURE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sampling the base channels at nuclear coordinates is right for this protocol
and it is still an assumption. It is also the assumption that fails without
looking wrong: a footprint that is too tight drops reads, one that is too
loose mixes two cells' reads into one barcode, and both produce barcodes that
decode cleanly to the wrong guide.

DECIDED:

1. SPOTS ARE DETECTED FIRST AND ATTRIBUTED SECOND. Detection runs on the SBS
   channels over the whole field, independently of any footprint; only then is
   each spot assigned to an object. THIS ORDER IS THE WHOLE POINT -- code that
   only ever looks inside the footprint can never discover that the reads are
   somewhere else.

2. THE FOOTPRINT IS A SETTING, `ops_read_footprint`, one of `nucleus`,
   `nucleus_dilated`, `cytoplasm_ring`, `cell`. Default `nucleus_dilated`
   with `ops_read_footprint_radius`, whose default is 3 px -- roughly 3
   microns at the scale PART 6-C measured (1267 px against 1280 microns,
   ~1.01 px/micron). A STARTING VALUE, to be answered by the first real
   decode rather than defended.

3. CONTAINMENT IS A REPORTED NUMBER AND A GATE. The fraction of detected
   spots landing inside the chosen footprint is written per well per cycle,
   and a run below `ops_min_spot_containment` (default 0.8) says so loudly.
   It is the one measurement that distinguishes "this protocol is nuclear"
   from "we only looked in the nucleus".

4. A SPOT INSIDE TWO FOOTPRINTS IS ASSIGNED TO NEITHER, and counted. Splitting
   it by distance would invent a read; dropping it silently would hide how
   often the footprint is too loose.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOLE 3 -- OBJECT NUMBERS ARE THE JOIN KEY, SO THEY GET A CONTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Everything downstream joins on the object number. A number assigned per
window collides across windows; a number that is a row index changes when a
filter changes; either way the join silently pairs the wrong cells, and a
wrong pairing between a barcode and a phenotype is the exact failure this
whole module exists to avoid.

DECIDED:

1. ASSIGNED ONCE, IN THE WELL FRAME, AFTER THE SEAMS ARE SEWN. Never per
   window, never per tile.

2. DETERMINISTIC AND REPRODUCIBLE. Objects are ordered by centroid --
   (y, x), rounded to 0.1 px to make the sort stable against float noise --
   and numbered from 1. A re-run on the same input reproduces the same ids,
   which is what makes two OPS runs comparable and a database diffable.

3. NEVER A ROW INDEX. The id is stored, not derived from position in a table.

4. UNIQUENESS IS A CONSTRAINT, NOT A CONVENTION. `UNIQUE(plate, well,
   object_id)` in the schema, so the bug surfaces as a write that fails at
   the moment it happens rather than as a mispairing found months later, if
   at all.

5. SEAM MATCHING IS BY DISTANCE, NOT BY LABEL. An object whose centroid falls
   in a window overlap is matched to its twin within
   `ops_seam_match_distance`, default half the expected nuclear diameter, and
   the two masks are unioned. An object that matches nothing keeps its own id
   -- an unmatched object near a seam is a real object, not an error.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THREE MORE, FOUND WHILE CLOSING THOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HOLE 4 -- THE SEGMENTATION CHANNEL IS NOT A CHOICE. The premise of PART 7 is
that segmentation runs on Hoechst averaged across cycles. If the module
exposes "which channel do I segment", someone points it at CY3 and the object
map silently becomes per-cycle and non-canonical. The setting is WHICH
CHANNEL IS HOECHST; the segmentation input is the composite, and it is not
selectable.

HOLE 5 -- THE DATABASE IS WRITTEN BEFORE THE SIDECAR, ALWAYS. A run that dies
between them then leaves an authority with no cache, which the reader already
handles by falling back to sqlite. The other order leaves a cache newer than
the authority, which nothing detects and every later read believes.

HOLE 6 -- A WELL-FRAME COORDINATE WITH NO STORED ORIGIN IS NOT A COORDINATE.
`ops_geometry` stores the well frame's origin and pitch alongside the per-tile
transforms, so an object centroid can be mapped back to the tile and pixel it
came from. Without it the centroids are internally consistent and externally
meaningless, and no later run can check them against the images.

WHAT IMPLEMENTATION STARTS FROM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PART 8 is the order of work, PART 9 is the contract. The first code is still
PART 6-C's remainder -- the position-driven adjacency that replaces
`max_site_gap` -- and nothing in PART 8 or 9 changes that, because every
phase after A2 is waiting on a stitch that places all 333 tiles.

PART 9-A -- TEST ON A PATCH, NOT ON A PLATE. 2026-09-09.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MAINTAINER, 2026-09-09: "when you are testing the ops data you do not need to
do the entire plate to start with, i think, if there is a way for you to do a
small segment of the plate (e.g. 100 FOV or less) then do that".

RIGHT, AND IT IS WORTH MORE THAN THE TIME IT SAVES. Every measurement in
PART 6 cost 400 seconds because it ran all 333 tiles of well A1, and each of
the three prototypes was a single 7-minute answer to a question that could
have been asked in one. A loop that turns over in under a minute is a
different way of working, not a faster version of the same one.

THE SUBSET MUST BE CONTIGUOUS, AND THIS IS THE PART THAT IS EASY TO GET
WRONG. A random 100 of 333 tiles is not a tenth of the problem, it is a
DIFFERENT problem: the tiles do not touch, so there are no overlaps to
register, and a stitch over them can only fail. The subset is a PATCH -- a
rectangular block of the raster, or a run of whole columns -- so that the
adjacency inside it is the same adjacency the full well has.

  `ops_site_subset`, taking either an explicit site list or a
  `(column_start, column_stop)` range, with the transform table carrying
  which sites went in. A run over a subset is a real run over a smaller
  well, not a special mode.

WHAT A PATCH CAN AND CANNOT ANSWER:

  IT CAN          the matcher, the unwrapping, the (0,0) rejection, the
                  cycle registration, the composite, the segmentation, the
                  object numbering, the schema, the whole of Phase C, and
                  every memory claim -- PART 6 measured peak RSS as flat in
                  tile count, so a patch's footprint is the well's.

  IT CANNOT       the varying column height that broke both prototypes. A
                  patch inside one part of the well sees a constant offset
                  and will make a fixed raster look correct. THE ADJACENCY IS
                  THE ONE THING THAT MUST BE CONFIRMED ON THE FULL WELL
                  before it is believed.

  SO THE RULE     develop on a patch, and re-run the full well only when the
                  adjacency changes or before calling a stage done.

A GOOD FIRST PATCH IS COLUMNS 0-8 OF WELL A1 c1 -- around 90 tiles, spanning
several column boundaries, so it contains at least one place where the
horizontal offset changes. A patch from the middle of a single column would
run fast and prove nothing.

PART 10 -- THERE IS ONLY ONE DAPI. PART 7 AND PART 8 CORRECTED. 2026-09-09.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MEASURED BEFORE WRITING ANY CODE, and it overturns a decided design, so it is
recorded before it is acted on.

    cycle   tif files   DAPI files   partial downloads
    c1          1,998        1,998        0
    c2-c11      7,992            0        0-3 each

  c1 is ONE FIVE-CHANNEL STACK PER SITE:
      10X_c1_A1_DAPI-CY3-A594-CY5-CY7_Site-N.tif
  c2..c11 are FOUR SEPARATE PER-CHANNEL FILES PER SITE:
      10X_c8_A1_A594_Site-N.tif, ..._CY3_..., ..._CY5_..., ..._CY7_...

  7,992 = 6 wells x 333 sites x 4 channels. 1,998 = 6 x 333 x 1 stack.

  THE HOECHST CHANNEL WAS ACQUIRED ONCE, IN CYCLE 1, AND NOWHERE ELSE.

WHAT THIS BREAKS. PART 7 concluded that averaging Hoechst across cycles "is
not merely safe, it is the right thing" and put the gain at roughly sqrt(11),
about 3.3x, on the exact channel segmentation runs on. PART 8 built two steps
on it: A3 registers each cycle's Hoechst against the reference, and B1
averages Hoechst across every cycle. NEITHER CAN RUN. There are not eleven
acquisitions of one DAPI field; there is one.

  The reasoning in PART 7 was sound and the premise was wrong. It is a
  correction rather than a retraction: on an acquisition that DOES image
  Hoechst every cycle, every word of PART 7 holds.

WHAT REPLACES IT, AND IT IS NOT A GUESS -- THE REFERENCE ALREADY SOLVES THIS.
`ops/firesnake.py:_align_SBS` takes `method` as either 'DAPI' or 'SBS_mean',
and the second exists for exactly this case:

    target  = max over the SBS channels, in a centered window
    normed  = normalize_by_percentile(target, q_norm=70), clipped at cutoff
    offsets = phase correlation on that

So cycle-to-cycle registration uses the SBS channels themselves. The spots
are punctate and abundant, which is good material for phase correlation --
better in some ways than DAPI, whose nuclei are smooth. The reference also
handles the channel-count mismatch directly: it keeps only the channels
common to every cycle, which DROPS c1's DAPI from the cycle stack, with
`keep_extras` to propagate it instead.

  CORRECTED A3: register cycles on the SBS_mean target, not on Hoechst.
  Keep the acceptance test (peak ratio, residual, (0,0) rejection) exactly as
  PART 9 hole 1 decided it -- only the channel changes.

  CORRECTED B1: the Hoechst composite averages across OVERLAPPING TILES
  within c1. That is still worth doing and it is still one canonical object
  map. What goes is the cross-cycle term and the 3.3x with it: segmentation
  gets one acquisition's signal-to-noise, not eleven.

  UNCHANGED: everything the maintainer specified about ORDER. Hoechst is
  still processed first and alone, nuclei are still segmented before any
  other channel is read, and the plate-level object numbers still come from
  that map and still link the sequencing and phenotype channels. PART 10
  changes what the alignment is measured ON, not what is done first.

AND PART 9 HOLE 1's MINIMUM LOSES ITS MEANING. `ops_min_cycles_for_composite`
was a floor on how many cycles may be missing from a Hoechst average that no
longer averages cycles. It is replaced by a floor on how many cycles register
successfully at all, which is what `ops_barcodes.n_cycles` already reports --
a read assembled from four of eleven cycles is a short barcode, and that is a
decode question rather than a segmentation one.

SEVENTEEN PARTIAL DOWNLOADS, worth knowing before a run trips on one.
`.lftp-pget-status` files are lftp's record of an interrupted transfer, and
there are 17 across c2..c11 (c3 2, c7 2, c8 3, c9 3, c11 3, one each in
c2/c4/c5/c6, none in c1 or c10). 17 in ~80,000 files, so the mirror is
essentially complete -- but the .tif beside each of those may be truncated,
and a loader that opens one will fail in the middle of a run rather than at
its start. THE PRE-FLIGHT SHOULD REFUSE THEM BY NAME.

  WORTH ASKING THE MAINTAINER: whether the source has Hoechst for c2..c11
  and it simply was not mirrored. If it does, PART 7 comes back intact and
  this whole entry becomes a note about an incomplete copy. The file layout
  argues it does not -- c1's five-channel stack against c2..c11's four
  separate per-channel files is two different acquisition configurations,
  not one configuration with files missing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 11 -- THE ADJACENCY AS TWO OR THREE NUMBERS, NOT A SEARCH. 2026-09-09.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A HYPOTHESIS TO TEST, LABELLED AS ONE. PART 6-B's lesson was that every
attempt so far has been "an assumption about the acquisition path dressed up
as an algorithm", so this is written as the next thing to MEASURE rather than
as the design.

WHAT THE REFERENCE SAYS, READ TODAY. `ops/plates.py:remap_snake(site,
grid_shape)` builds `np.arange(rows*cols).reshape(rows, cols)`, reverses
every other row, and indexes it flat. IT ASSUMES A FULL RECTANGLE. This well
has 333 sites, and 333 factors only as 1x333, 3x111 or 9x37 -- none of which
is a plausible field layout. So the acquisition is NOT a full rectangle, and
`remap_snake` cannot describe it as it stands.

WHAT THAT LEAVES, AND IT FITS EVERY MEASUREMENT IN PART 6-C. A round well
visited by a snake: for each column the stage visits the grid rows that fall
INSIDE THE CIRCLE, then moves to the next column and comes back up. That
gives exactly what was measured -- +/-1 is always vertical, and the offset to
the horizontal neighbour varies (+11, +4, -11, -9, -4, -13, -5) because it is
the height of the column being crossed.

  SO THE LAYOUT IS NOT 333 UNKNOWNS. It is a circle: a centre, a radius and
  a pitch, in grid units. The column heights follow from those, the site
  index of the first tile in column c is the sum of the heights before it,
  and every adjacency falls out. Two or three numbers, fitted, instead of a
  neighbour search over 333 tiles.

  THE FIT IS CHEAP AND IT IS ALREADY HALF DONE. The column heights must sum
  to 333, and PART 6-C's seven measured horizontal offsets are seven
  constraints on them. Solve for the circle that reproduces those offsets,
  then PREDICT the offsets at sites nobody measured and check them by
  registration. A prediction that survives sites it was not fitted on is a
  layout; one that does not is another guess, and it will have cost an hour
  rather than a night.

  AND IT DEGRADES HONESTLY. If the fit fails, the fallback is the
  position-driven search PART 6-A already argued for -- once a tile is
  placed, its neighbours' positions are known to within a pitch. The fit is
  worth trying FIRST because it is O(1) registrations rather than O(N), not
  because the search is wrong.

WHAT NOT TO DO: raise `max_site_gap`. `spacr/spacrops.py:1332`
`_pairs_by_site_window` proposes pairs by site-index distance, and the
default is now 64 (`spacrops.py:3586`), which proposes 128 candidates per
tile to find four. PART 6 showed the index window is unsound for this
acquisition at 3; at 64 it is merely expensive as well.

PART 13-B -- THE GPU, AS THE INSTRUCTION ACTUALLY OUTLINES IT. 2026-09-09.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"372 outlines more use og the GPU!" -- the maintainer, when offered a

WHAT THE INSTRUCTION NAMES, in its own order: the per-window affine warp;

STRIP THE NAMES AWAY AND THAT IS THREE PRIMITIVES. A windowed maximum, a

    depend on how much data there is.
  * `match_cells` RE-MEASURES ITS THRESHOLD IN FULL PRECISION. The search

    exactly on the threshold would be kept or dropped according to which
    backend ran. That decision is which phenotype carries which barcode,

That run used WHOLE-TILE correlation with a DC mask, and whole-tile is

  THE STRIP IS THE FIRST THING SENSITIVE ENOUGH TO NOTICE, and its 150

  tuning anything. That instinct is what stopped a threshold being fitted

That is what A4 is supposed to do with a field it cannot place, and Phase C
has to record it per field rather than dropping it silently -- exactly as
A3 records a refused cycle.

WHAT THE MODULE NEEDS, and it is one line rather than the machinery
PART 14-B proposed: `PAIR_TRIALS` scales with the target point count.
A tile-sized target holds ~600 nuclei and a well-frame window ~3,000, and
the chance of a random pair landing on a true correspondence falls with the
square of that.

PART 14-D -- TWENTY FIELDS OF THE PHENOTYPE ACQUISITION ARE EMPTY DIRECTORIES
NAMED `.tif`. 2026-09-10.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Found by a sweep dying on one of them.

    IsADirectoryError: [Errno 21] Is a directory:
      .../20X_DAPI-GFP-A594-AF750_A1_DAPI-GFP_Site-864.tif

    find ... -maxdepth 1 -type d -name "*.tif"   ->  20

Twenty paths that end in `.tif` are DIRECTORIES, empty, dated Sep 3 2022 --
the day the screen was downloaded. Four of them are in well A1's DAPI-GFP
channel alone: sites 864, 867, 871 and 909. The rest are spread over A2's
A594, AF750 and DAPI-GFP.

They are almost certainly a partial transfer: a sync that creates the
destination as a directory when the file fails is a known failure mode of
`aws s3 sync` and of `rsync` interrupted mid-file. PART 10 already counted
"partial downloads 0-3 each" on the sequencing cycles, so this is the same
defect in the phenotype half and it was not counted there.

WHY IT MATTERS TO THE CODE AND NOT ONLY TO THE DATA:

  * `os.path.exists` SAYS TRUE. Every existence check that is not
    `os.path.isfile` passes on these, and the failure then arrives from
    inside the reader, as an IsADirectoryError from `tifffile.imread`, at
    whatever depth the call stack happens to be.
  * `except FileNotFoundError` DOES NOT CATCH IT. `IsADirectoryError` is a
    sibling under `OSError`, not a subclass of `FileNotFoundError`, so a
    handler written for a missing file lets this one through. That is
    exactly how the sweep died: 81 fields in, having already read 11 GB.
  * AND A FIELD WITH NO IMAGE IS A REAL CASE, not a corruption to refuse
    over. Twenty of 7,686 phenotype fields is a quarter of a percent, and a
    plate is not worth failing for it. It belongs in the same place a
    refused A4 alignment and a refused A3 cycle belong: recorded per field,
    with a reason, and excluded from the denominator rather than from the
    record.

THE RULE FOR THE MODULE: check `os.path.isfile`, catch `OSError` rather than
`FileNotFoundError`, and carry "no image" as a distinct outcome from "no
alignment". A field that was never acquired and a field that could not be
placed are different facts about the plate, and a barcode assembled from
what survived has to be able to tell them apart.

PART 14-F -- 14-E IS RETRACTED, AND THE REAL DEFECT IS THE ROW INDEX.
2026-09-10.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RETRACTION FIRST. PART 14-E says "every field below site 648 aligned -- 81 of
81 -- and 44 of the 79 at or above it refused", and reasons from that to a
snake parity that breaks at the middle column. THAT NUMBER IS WRONG AND SO IS
THE CONCLUSION. It was read off a `tail -45` of the sweep's output, which
showed only the LAST 45 lines -- all of them refusals, all of them from the
second half, because that is where the tail ends. The refusals before site
648 were above the cut and I never saw them.

    what 14-E claimed        81 of 81 below 648, 35 of 79 above
    what the table says      42 of 81 below 648, 35 of 80 above

Forty-two, not eighty-one. There is no break at the middle column; the
refusal rate is essentially the same on both sides. This is the same mistake
this file records twice already -- PART 6-A, PART 13-E -- committed on the
file's own evidence: a truncated output read as a complete one.

    THE RULE THAT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IT: never take a COUNT from a stream
    that was piped through `tail`. Write the whole result to a file and
    count it there. The sweep now writes `a4_transforms.json` and every
    number below is counted from all 161 rows.

WHAT THE 77 ALIGNED FIELDS ACTUALLY SAY, and it is better than 14-E's story.

THE MAGNIFICATION IS SETTLED. Seventy-seven independent fields, each fitted
on its own nuclei:

    scale     median 0.25069   sd 0.00060   range 0.2497 .. 0.2517
    rotation  median +0.010 deg  sd 0.094 deg

A fifth of a percent of spread on the scale and a tenth of a degree on the
rotation, across the whole well. The 0.2543 predicted from the two fitted
radii is 1.4 % high; 0.25069 is the measured number and should replace it.

AND THE RASTER IS SQUARE, once it is fitted on fields that agree rather than
through the ones that do not. Least squares over the better half:

    col_step   (-4.60, 633.79)    |633.8| px
    row_step   (633.51,  4.52)    |633.5| px

Two steps of the same length at right angles, each carrying the same ~4.5 px
of stage skew that PART 13-D found on the sequencing raster. 633.5 px, not
the 644.5 the radius ratio predicted.

THE DEFECT IS THE ROW INDEX AND NOTHING ELSE. Inverting the fitted raster to
solve each field's grid index from its measured centre, against what
`WellLayout.position` said:

    column index error    median 0.00   sd 0.00      -- EXACTLY right, always
    row index error       median 0.00   sd 1.00

    d_col  0, d_row  0    x48
    d_col  0, d_row +2    x9
    d_col  0, d_row -1    x8
    d_col  0, d_row -2    x7
    d_col  0, d_row +1    x5

The column is right for all 77. The row is wrong by one or two for 29 of
them -- and one row is 633 px, which is why those fields' windows missed and
they refused.

THAT IS PART 11-A's MISTAKE, IN THE PHENOTYPE LAYOUT. Its own note: "rows
were indexed from each column's own top. In a round well every column starts
at a different row." The sequencing well was fixed by making
`WellLayout.position` return ABSOLUTE rows. The 41-column phenotype layout
goes through the same code and comes out with the right column and the wrong
row, so the circle that holds exactly 1,281 fields is not the circle the
acquisition used -- the site count matches and the per-column heights do
not.

WHAT TO DO, and it is now a small and well-posed job:

  1. SOLVE THE ROW INDEX RATHER THAN DERIVING IT. The 48 fields whose row is
     already exact fit the raster; every other field's row follows from its
     measured centre. That is A2's own argument applied one level up:
     register what you can, solve, place the rest.
  2. OR FIT THE PHENOTYPE CIRCLE PROPERLY, against the 77 measured
     positions, the way PART 11-A fitted the sequencing one against seven
     offsets. Four parameters, 77 constraints, and the per-column heights
     come out of it.
  3. EITHER WAY THE MAGNIFICATION IS NO LONGER A QUESTION: 0.25069.

AND `PAIR_TRIALS` IS STILL TOO LOW. 77 of 161 at 4,000 single-shot trials
against 11 of 12 when the count was escalated to 12,000. Some of the 84
refusals are the wrong window and some are the search; the row fix separates
them, and until it lands neither number means much on its own.
