--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FINDING THIS EXISTS BECAUSE OF, AND IT MUST BE IN THE TEXT BOX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`ml.py:591`: `gene_fraction` is THE SUM OF THE GENE'S gRNA FRACTIONS. The
formula spaCR has been fitting is

    y ~ fraction:grna + gene_fraction:gene + rowID + columnID

so every `gene_fraction` column is an exact linear combination of that gene's
`fraction:grna` columns. THE TWO BLOCKS ARE PERFECTLY COLLINEAR BY
CONSTRUCTION -- not by accident of data, and no number of wells fixes it.

statsmodels does not refuse; it takes a pseudo-inverse and returns ONE
arbitrary solution out of infinitely many. That is why a single-guide gene
comes back identical to its guide (244480 and 244480_3, both 3.389291 at
2.873149e-13), why the not-identifiable warning fires (827 parameters, 610
wells), and why the volcano drew a gene once per guide plus once for itself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. THE SETTINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the Model and Inference tab:

  * `regression_type` -- DEFAULT BECOMES `mixed`. The maintainer's reason,
    recorded because it is the design rationale: "mixed answers the most
    central question best".
  * `level` -- NEW. `both` (default) | `grna` | `gene`. GREYED OUT when
    `regression_type == 'mixed'`, per 106: disabled and saying why, never
    absent and never inert.
  * a read-only TEXT BOX that changes with the selection and states, for the
    chosen model, THE FORMULA and WHAT IS MODELLED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. WHAT EACH OPTION FITS -- THIS IS THE TEXT BOX CONTENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MIXED (default):

    y ~ gene_fraction:gene + (1 | gene/grna) + rowID + columnID

    The gene effect is a FIXED effect; each guide is a RANDOM effect nested
    inside its gene. This is the only model here that says what a guide
    actually is -- a biological replicate of one intended perturbation, with
    its own efficiency and off-target profile -- rather than a second
    independent variable. Guides disagreeing about a gene widens that gene's
    interval instead of silently averaging out, and a gene resting on one
    noisy guide is shrunk toward zero.

    WHAT IT DOES NOT GIVE YOU, and the box must say so: guide-level results
    come back as BLUPs (shrunken predictions), NOT coefficients with standard
    errors and p-values, so there is no guide-level hit list to correct.
    Choose a non-mixed model with level='grna' if that is what you need.

EVERY OTHER BACKEND (ols, glm, wls, rlm, huber, poisson, quasi_binomial,
beta, logit, probit, quantile, lasso, ridge, elasticnet, hinge, horseshoe) --
fixed effects only, so they CANNOT nest guides in genes. They fit ONE LEVEL
AT A TIME, chosen by `level`:

    level='grna'   y ~ fraction:grna + rowID + columnID
                   One coefficient per guide. The guide is the unit the
                   screen measures; genes are not in this model at all.

    level='gene'   y ~ gene_fraction:gene + rowID + columnID
                   One coefficient per gene, from the summed guide fraction.
                   Guides are not in this model.

    level='both'   BOTH OF THE ABOVE, FITTED SEPARATELY -- two models, two
                   tables. NOT one design containing both, which is the
                   collinear formula above and the bug this replaces.

The box also says what the MODE does -- ols is least squares, glm a chosen
family and link, rlm/huber robust to outlying wells, quantile fits a
conditional quantile rather than the mean, the penalised ones rank by
bootstrap selection frequency and report no p-value, and so on. One paragraph
each, in the register of the run's own console output.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. TWO TABLES, TWO CORRECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"for each do a BH FDR correction."

Each fit is its own multiple-testing family and is corrected on its own.
Pooling them would be wrong twice: the two sets are not independent (same
wells, and the gene regressor is literally the sum of the guide regressors),
and doubling the family size costs power for no protection. spaCR already
states this principle for multiple outcomes -- "corrects each as its OWN
multiple-testing family; pooling them would make two correlated readouts of
the same wells look like twice as many tests".

    results_grna.csv    the guide fit, BH-corrected within itself
    results_gene.csv    the gene fit,  BH-corrected within itself

Both saved every run. The volcano's existing level switch chooses between
them; it currently filters ROWS of one table and becomes a choice of TABLE.

WORTH SAYING IN THE PAPER, and worth a line in the box: a gene called by the
gene fit AND its guides called by the guide fit is two tests of one
hypothesis. The report should say which the claim rests on.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* A fresh run fits `mixed` and says so.
* Choosing any other model enables `level`; choosing `mixed` greys it out
  with a reason.
* The text box states the formula for the current selection and changes when
  it changes.
* `level='both'` writes results_gene.csv AND results_grna.csv from TWO fits,
  each BH-corrected separately -- and no run anywhere fits
  `fraction:grna + gene_fraction:gene` in one design again. A test asserts
  that formula is gone.
* The not-identifiable warning stops firing on this screen: the guide fit
  drops 452 parameters and the gene fit drops 823, against 610 wells.
