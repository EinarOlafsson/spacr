WHAT TO BUILD

  1. THE GROUPS COME FROM THE CELL PICKER. It already decides which cells
     carry which gene -- rank, attributed, assigned, multivariate -- so this
     reads that answer rather than inventing a second one. Several genes at
     once: each is its own group, and everything not picked is "the rest".

  2. THE LEVEL IS CELL, WELL OR PLATE, and it changes what an observation IS.
     A per-cell test on 60,000 cells from 12 wells has 60,000 rows and 12
     independent units; saying so is the difference between a p-value and a
     number. The chosen level must be stated beside the test.

  3. FIVE PLOTS: bar, box, jitter, jitter over box, violin.

  4. THE STATISTICS NAME THEMSELVES. n per group, a variance check and a
     normality check, and the test CHOSEN FROM THOSE ANSWERS rather than
     fixed: two groups normal with equal variance -> Welch is still the safe
     default and t is offered; non-normal -> Mann-Whitney; more than two ->
     ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis, with the pairwise follow-up corrected. Every
     one of those decisions is printed with the number that drove it.

  5. ONE FOLDER ON SAVE, holding the figure as PDF and PNG, the plotted data,
     the settings that produced the regression AND the graph, the statistics,
     and the cell images per well where they exist.

HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED

* The slider is invisible against its container until you use it.
* `normalize_channels='r,g,b'` visibly changes the picture, and so does '0,1,2'.
* Measure's categories are tabs.
* Hovering recolours one ring rather than adding a second, and the picked
  cells keep their blue in show-all.
* A comparison of one gene against the rest, at all three levels, with each
  of the five plots, and a saved folder that holds every item in 5.
