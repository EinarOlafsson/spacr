================================================================================
THE PLATE VIEWER: ONE LOCKED GRID, SQUARE WELLS, AND DRAG TO SELECT
================================================================================

Status:    OPEN. Reported again 2026-08-25, having been asked for once
           before and never built -- "a long time ago i told you that ...
           none of this is true".
Owner:     spacr/qt/widgets/plate_map_picker.py, and every screen that
           offers a plate map (the regression control-well pickers are
           where it was met).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THREE ASKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. EVERY PART OF THE PLATE IS LOCKED TO EVERY OTHER PART.

   "all elements of the plate locked to each other (wells and column and
   row axis labels)."

   The row letters, the column numbers and the wells are one grid, not
   three things that happen to be near each other. Resize the window and
   they move together; scroll and they scroll together; a column label
   sits over its column at every size, and a row letter beside its row.

   The failure this is aimed at is the ordinary one: labels laid out in
   their own strip drift out of register with the wells as soon as the
   two are given different amounts of space.

2. A WELL IS A SQUARE.

   "the wells should be squares not rectangles."

   A plate well is round in life and square on a map. Stretched into a
   rectangle it reads as a grid of something else, and the eye stops
   being able to count across it. The square survives every window size:
   the grid takes the space it needs to keep them square and leaves the
   rest, rather than dividing whatever it is given.

3. CLICK AND DRAG SELECTS A RECTANGLE.

   "the user should be able to click and drag to select not only click."

   Press on a well, move, release: every well the pointer swept is
   selected. Selecting a block of a plate is the common case -- a whole
   column of controls, a quadrant, four wells in a corner -- and clicking
   ninety-six times is not a way to do it.

   NOTE, from the coverage pass: the drag gesture was WRITTEN and is
   dead. `_Well._picker` walks exactly two parents to find the picker,
   and `area.setWidget(self._holder)` reparents the holder into the
   scroll area's VIEWPORT, so the walk lands on the viewport instead.
   Every `hasattr(picker, "begin_drag")` guard is then False and a press
   selects nothing. Pinned by
   `tests/qt/test_cov_w2_6_plate_map_picker.py`. The walk has to find the
   PlateMapPicker ancestor rather than counting steps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A FOURTH THING, FOUND WITH THEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`PlateMapPicker.set_selection` blocks the well's signals around
`setChecked`, so the `toggled -> _paint` connection never fires: a
programmatically chosen well -- which includes the picker's own starting
value and everything kept across a layout change -- is checked and still
painted EMPTY. It has to repaint after setting the state.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Driven, not eyeballed, at more than one window size:

* a column label's centre x equals its column's centre x, and a row
  label's centre y equals its row's centre y, at 700 px wide and at
  1400 px wide;
* every well's width equals its height, at both sizes;
* press on B2, move to D5, release -> exactly the twelve wells of that
  rectangle are selected, and the ones outside it are not;
* `set_selection(["A01"])` leaves A01 painted as selected.
