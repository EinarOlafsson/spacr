--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FOUR SEPARATE QUESTIONS, AND THEY HAVE DIFFERENT ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. What cheap measurements are missing?
  2. What expensive ones are worth adding as OPT-IN?
  3. Can any be computed on the GPU?
  4. Can a CNN/transformer produce per-object feature vectors, stored either
     as measurements or as a separate object, usable for training?

(3) is instruction 70's territory and should be answered there, referenced
here. (4) is a different KIND of thing from (1) and (2) and the biggest risk
of the four -- see the storage note below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE STATE IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`spacr/measure.py` is 3662 lines and already computes more than the question
implies. Verified by reading it, 2026-08-10:

    Zernike moments                 _calculate_zernike, degree 8
    radial intensity distribution   _calculate_radial_distribution, 6 shells
    periphery intensity             _periphery_intensity
    outside intensity               _outside_intensity, distance 5
    skeleton properties             via measure.regionprops on a skeleton
    shannon entropy                 imported and used
    percentile intensity stats      _extended_regionprops_table
    full regionprops morphology     _morphological_measurements

So "granularity and texture are missing" is the accurate summary, and
Zernike and radial distribution -- two of the more expensive families -- are
already in. ANY proposal must be checked against this list first; proposing
something already implemented is the most likely failure mode here.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A CNN penultimate layer is 512-2048 floats PER OBJECT. A plate can hold
hundreds of thousands of objects. That is not a row in a measurements table:

  * it makes the table unreadable for its actual purpose, which is a human
    or a model looking at named features;
  * SQLite has a column limit and a practical width well below 2048;
  * the features are not interpretable individually, so they do not belong
    beside `area` and `mean_intensity`, which are;
  * they are regenerable from the crops and the model, so they are a CACHE,
    not a measurement.

The likely right answer is a SIDECAR keyed by the same object id the
measurements use -- parquet or .npy per plate -- with the measurements
database holding only the key and the provenance (which model, which layer,
which weights hash). Decide this before writing any of it, and record the
decision here.

HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The default measure run is no slower than before; the new cheap features are
in the table with stated defaults and units; each expensive family is behind
one documented switch that is off by default; and if learned features ship,
they are stored somewhere that does not degrade the measurements table and
carry the provenance needed to reproduce them.

2026-08-10 - INVESTIGATED. ALL FOUR SUB-QUESTIONS ANSWERED, WITH NUMBERS.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full findings: `instructions/data/71_measurements_investigation.txt`.

FOUR PREREQUISITES THAT DELETE A NEW FEATURE SILENTLY. Any new column must
clear all four or it will not survive to a model:

  * a name containing the substring `label` is swallowed by
    `utils._check_integrity` -- folded into `label_list` and dropped with no
    warning, and if it sorts before the real label column it BECOMES
    `object_label` and corrupts the merge key;
  * `utils.filter_dataframe_features` does `df.dropna(axis=1)`, so ONE NaN
    anywhere -- a partially re-measured plate, one flat object producing a
    NaN skew -- deletes the column from every model matrix;
  * every `<object>_`-prefixed numeric column auto-enrols in every
    classifier/UMAP/timelapse feature matrix, with nobody opting in;
  * without a `KNOWN_PROPERTIES` + `FEATURE_SCOPE` entry in feature_dict it
    reports as family `unknown` with a null description.

(1) CHEAP ADDITIONS, ranked. The largest genuine gap is NEIGHBOURS / LOCAL
DENSITY -- there is no spatial family in the taxonomy at all, and local
density is the dominant confounder in image screens (crowded cells are
smaller, dimmer, cell-cycle-shifted) with no per-object column to regress it
out. Cost is O(field) once, not O(objects x field): a cKDTree over 400
centroids is 0.437 ms. Then: the GLCM matrix is already built and thrown
away after ONE of skimage's ten properties is read (contrast, dissimilarity,
correlation, energy, entropy are nearly free); radial MeanFrac, which is the
recruitment statistic, is ~20 us against the ~615 us already spent per bin;
Spearman + Manders overlap coefficient are reductions on vectors already
extracted; form_factor and roundness at 0.67 ms per 5000 rows. Note
`aspect_ratio` and `elongation` are exact monotone bijections of eccentricity
which is already stored -- adding them adds nothing.

(2) OPT-IN. True Manders M1/M2 at a single threshold (~0.68 s/field) is a
CORRECTNESS FIX, not a feature: the current `M1_correlation_85` is not
Manders M1 -- both channels are thresholded at their own within-object
percentile and share one overlap mask, so M1 and M2 correlate at r=0.998 and
two pure-noise channels give M1=0.047. Costes auto-threshold ~38 s/field.
REFUSE per-object Costes randomisation: 83-112 s/field, 30-40x the whole
coloc block.

(3) GPU: mostly no -- see instruction 70. The expensive things are
O(N_objects x H x W) full-field passes inside Python loops, and cropping to a
bounding box gives 87x on the CPU, bit-identical.

(4) LEARNED FEATURES: yes, and most of it is already built. Nothing embeds
crops today -- `generate_image_umap` is 100% tabular and the
`resnet_features` branch raises NotImplementedError with a test pinning the
raise. The recommendation is the plate's OWN trained classifier rather than a
fresh ImageNet ResNet, at `model._run_backbone(x)` -- the penultimate pooled
vector, no hook and no extra forward pass, and the honest choice since the
classifier's decision is a linear function of exactly it. maxvit_t (the
shipped default) is 512-d.

  THE STORAGE ANSWER IS A HARD LIMIT, NOT TASTE. This env's sqlite is
  compiled MAX_COLUMN=2000: 2000 columns create, 2001 fails, ALTER TABLE
  hits the same ceiling and Python 3.10 cannot raise it. A 2048-d ResNet-50
  vector CANNOT be stored one-value-per-column in any table at any channel
  count. Even 512-d, which does fit, takes the cell table from 2.04 to 8.08
  KB/object -- a 384-well plate goes ~301 MB to ~1205 MB. And packing it
  into one column is rejected by `schema.validate_object_table_frame`, which
  refuses any non-numeric column in the object namespace (text, str(list)
  and a float32 blob were all tried and all raised).

  So: a SIDECAR, reusing the artifact kind that already exists --
  `ports.EMBEDDING` is already produced into `results/`, already labelled
  "embeddings" in the usage report, already in `DEFAULT_PRUNABLE_KINDS`.
  `<src>/results/embeddings/<model>__penultimate.npy`, float16, with a key
  index carrying OBJECT_KEY_COLUMNS **plus the object type** (untyped keys
  collide when one sidecar covers cell and nucleus in the same field).

  CONSUMPTION IS ALREADY WRITTEN: `build_anndata(db, embeddings=...)`
  aligns BY OBJECT KEY, not positionally, and raises with the first three
  unmatched objects otherwise. The only gap is that `run_anndata_export`
  never passes `embeddings`, so the settings path needs one new key.

A BUG FOUND IN PASSING, retroactive and needing no re-measurement:
`channel_of_interest` DELETES colocalization columns that were computed and
stored. A coloc column contains two channel tokens and the substring test
hits it via the partner channel -- at the UMAP default `filter_by='channel_0'`
ZERO coloc columns survive. Fix by special-casing `channel_<i>_channel_<j>_`
to survive when either member matches.

FOUR THINGS TO DO, in this order:

1. FIX THE MANDERS ERROR. `M1_correlation_85` is not Manders M1. Both
   channels are thresholded at their own within-object percentile and share
   one overlap mask, so M1 and M2 correlate at r=0.998, at t=95 M1 is
   structurally capped at the intensity fraction in the object's own top 5%,
   and two pure-noise channels give M1=0.047. The tooltips at
   settings.py:2791 and :2669 call them "Manders' overlap coefficients",
   which names a third statistic again. The activation-map path has the
   identical pattern with `>=` instead of `>`.
   This is a CORRECTNESS fix and it changes existing columns, so it needs a
   decision recorded here about naming: either fix the statistic under the
   existing column name (old plates then disagree with new ones) or add
   correct columns beside the wrong ones and mark the old as deprecated.
   Prefer the second -- a silently changed meaning is worse than a new
   column -- and say so in the tooltip.

2. ADD THE FEATURES ALREADY BEING COMPUTED AND THROWN AWAY. The GLCM
   co-occurrence matrix is built and then only ONE of skimage's ten
   properties is read off it. contrast, dissimilarity, correlation,
   energy/ASM and entropy are nearly free from the matrix already in hand.
   Two mechanics found by measurement:
     * only angle 0 is used, and there is an 8.1% spread across the four
       angles -- the current homogeneity is rotation-dependent. Pooling four
       angles before reading properties costs 0.68 ms at 64 levels.
     * `graycoprops` re-normalises the whole P array per call, so cost is
       O(levels^2 x distances x angles) PER PROPERTY.
     * `rescale_intensity` is called inside the per-distance loop although it
       does not depend on distance -- 0.105 ms per object/channel wasted.
   Also free from vectors already extracted: radial MeanFrac (the
   recruitment statistic, ~20 us against ~615 us already spent per bin), and
   Spearman + the Manders overlap coefficient in the coloc block.

3. NEIGHBOURS / LOCAL DENSITY / ADJACENCY, scoped by object class.
   Cost is O(field) once, not O(objects x field): a cKDTree over 400
   centroids is 0.437 ms and one field-level `find_boundaries` is 14.1 ms.
   Measure per object: neighbour count within a radius, first and second
   nearest-neighbour distance, percent_touching, and the label ids of
   adjacent objects where that is cheap.

   WHICH CLASSES, and the reasoning the maintainer asked for:
     cell        YES. This is the main case. Local density is the dominant
                 confounder in image screens -- crowded cells are smaller,
                 dimmer and cell-cycle-shifted -- and there is currently no
                 PER-OBJECT column to regress it out (`object_counts` and
                 `seg_qc.foreground_fraction` are per-field).
     nucleus     YES, and the maintainer's instinct is right: a cell can hold
                 more than one nucleus, so nucleus-to-nucleus spacing inside
                 a cell is a real quantity (binucleation, mitotic pairs) and
                 is not derivable from the cell's own neighbours.
     pathogen    YES, same argument and stronger -- pathogen burden per cell
                 and the spacing of pathogens within one cell are exactly
                 what this assay measures.
     organelle   YES for the punctate/vesicular families, where "how many and
                 how clustered" is the phenotype. Probably meaningless for a
                 reticular or cisternal organelle that is one connected
                 object per cell -- so gate it on the organelle type being
                 introduced by instruction 72 rather than shipping it for all.
     cytoplasm   NO, as the maintainer says. Cytoplasm is defined as cell
                 minus nucleus, so it is one object per cell by construction
                 and its "neighbours" are just the cell's, restated.

   TWO MECHANICS, both found by reading the code: `binary_dilation`
   booleanises, so identity-aware touching needs
   `skimage.segmentation.expand_labels(labels, distance=1, spacing=...)`;
   and the KDTree must be built on spacing-scaled centroids or a 50-px
   radius means different physical distances in 2-D and 3-D runs.

4. FIX THE COLOCALIZATION COLUMN DELETION. `channel_of_interest` /
   `filter_by` delete coloc columns that were computed and stored. A coloc
   column carries TWO channel tokens and the substring test matches it via
   the partner channel; at the UMAP default `filter_by='channel_0'` ZERO
   coloc columns survive, and at channel_of_interest 1/2/3 exactly one pair
   survives and the rest are deleted asymmetrically, purely because the
   hard-coded drop list starts at `channel_1`. Fix by special-casing
   `channel_<i>_channel_<j>_` to survive when EITHER member matches. The data
   is already in measurements.db, so this is retroactive and needs no
   re-measurement.

2026-08-10 - THE GLCM "NEARLY FREE" CLAIM IS REFUTED. MEASURED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The entry above says the extra GLCM properties are "nearly free from the
matrix already in hand". THAT IS WRONG, and it was my claim, not the
maintainer's. Benchmarked min-of-7 x 20-30 reps, taskset-pinned, ratios
reproduced across four runs:

    A  CURRENT code verbatim (rescale in loop, 1 prop, 1 angle)   1.206 ms   1.00x
    B  hoist rescale + drop normed=True, IDENTICAL columns        0.959 ms   0.79x
    C  = B + all 7 properties, shared normalisation, angle 0      2.827 ms   2.34x
    D  = B + 4-angle mean, homogeneity ONLY                      10.254 ms   8.50x
    E  = B + 4 angles + all 7 properties                         47.365 ms  39.27x

WHY: at the default 256 levels, reading ONE property off the matrix costs
MORE than building it. graycomatrix over three distances is 0.434 ms; a
single graycoprops call is 2.1-6.7 ms. The matrix build is O(pixels) over a
14,400-pixel patch; the property read is O(levels^2) over 65,536 float64
elements. The "already in hand" intuition is exactly backwards -- the matrix
is the cheap half.

SO THE SHAPE OF THIS ITEM CHANGES:

  * OPTION B IS A FREE WIN AND SHOULD BE TAKEN ON ITS OWN: hoisting
    `rescale_intensity` out of the per-distance loop and dropping
    `normed=True` (graycoprops re-normalises anyway) is **21% FASTER** than
    today with byte-identical output. That is a pure defect fix, not a
    feature, and it does not need a setting.
  * The extra properties are a 2.34x texture block. Real value, but OPT-IN,
    not "free".
  * The rotation fix is 8.5x on its own. Pooling four angles is the correct
    statistic -- angle-0-only homogeneity IS rotation-dependent -- but it is
    the single most expensive change available here.

TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE NUMBERS QUOTED ABOVE:

  The "8.1% spread across the four angles" is ONE TEXTURE's number, not a
  property of the method. Measured over 19 objects x 3 distances: mean 14.09%,
  median 13.26%, max 33.63%. Per property it ranges from 0.3% (entropy) to
  76-1466% for CORRELATION, which crosses zero and so has no stable relative
  spread at all. Do not quote a single figure for this.

  `graycoprops` re-normalising per call is confirmed and worth 1.66x when
  seven properties share one normalisation (21.867 ms -> 13.191 ms on a
  (256,256,3,1) matrix), verified byte-identical for six properties and
  1.1e-16 for correlation.
