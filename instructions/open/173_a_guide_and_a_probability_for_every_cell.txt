================================================================================
A GUIDE AND A PROBABILITY FOR EVERY CELL
================================================================================

Status:    filed 2026-08-19.
Requested: 2026-08-19 -- "can i get a grna annotation for each cell based on
           the fraction of each gran in each well and its classification
           score. however this would only work for grnas that have a
           measureble effect on classefication. luckaly that is what i care
           about." and then, precisely --

           "i want you to try and assigne every cell a guide but at a
           unsertainty threshold. if a cell only has grna attributions that
           are below 0.55 or something then it gets a ambiguous tag and the
           highest probability any gran had for it if it gets a grna above
           0.55 then it gets annotated with that gene and the probability
           score if 2 genes get exactly the same score one is randomly
           chosen."

WHY HEURISTIC 1 IS NOT ENOUGH, in the maintainer's own words: "if i have 2
grnas both in the same direction and want to attribute cells to both my
arithmatic wont work well because both pick the top hits which would be
wrong". Ranking by score and taking the top x per guide attributes the SAME
cells to every guide pointing the same way.

THE MODEL

Per well, for cell i and guide g:

    prior      pi_g          the guide's read fraction, normalised to sum to 1
    likelihood f_g(s_i)      how likely this cell's score is if it carries g,
                             from that guide's fitted effect beta_g
    posterior  r_ig  ∝  pi_g * f_g(s_i)

AND THE CONSTRAINT THAT ANSWERS THE TWO-GUIDE PROBLEM:

    sum_i r_ig  =  N * pi_g          for every guide in the well

enforced by iterative proportional fitting. The TOTAL mass attributed to each
guide is pinned to its read fraction, so two guides pointing the same way
SPLIT the top cells in proportion to their fractions instead of both claiming
them. Plain Bayes does not do this; the constraint is the whole point.

THE DIRECTION PROBLEM DISAPPEARS. beta_g carries its own sign, so a negative
guide's likelihood peaks at low scores with no ranking rule to get right --
which heuristic 1 needs and can get wrong.

THE CALL, exactly as specified

    best = max_g r_ig
    if best >= ambiguity_threshold (default 0.55, settable):
        assign that guide, record the probability, record its gene
    else:
        tag AMBIGUOUS, and record `best` anyway -- the highest any guide had

    An exact tie is broken AT RANDOM, from a SEEDED generator: spaCR runs are
    reproducible (`guide_permutation_seed` is already a setting) and a coin
    flip that lands differently on a re-run would make the same screen give
    two different annotations.

THE LIKELIHOOD

    log-normal FIRST, on the same scale the regression used, so beta_g and
    s_i are commensurate. Beta KEPT AS AN OPTION -- better behaved at 0 and 1,
    where a classification score actually lives, and the maintainer asked for
    it to remain available. See 174 for the regression's own beta transform.

WHAT IS ESTIMATED WHERE

    pi_g                per WELL          it is what sequencing measures
    beta_g              GLOBAL, per guide from the regression, which already
                                          blocks on plate
    location and scale  per PLATE         never per well: centring per well
                                          destroys the between-well signal
                                          that identifies the effect at all

THE LONG TAIL. Measured on the maintainer's screen: a median well holds ~450
cells and 403 DETECTED GUIDES, most at one or two reads. 30 guides cover 82%
of a well's reads; 4 cover 55%. Attributing 400 trace guides is arithmetic on
no evidence, so the guides covering a stated share of the reads are attributed
and the remainder is pooled into one `other` component carrying the rest.

WHICH GUIDES CAN BE ATTRIBUTED AT ALL, reported BEFORE anything is assigned.
A guide whose effect is small against the spread of scores can never reach the
threshold -- not with more cells, not with a better fit; it is arithmetic and
not sample size. One number per guide says whether it can produce a real
assignment or only ever `ambiguous`, so a user knows which hits support
cell-level work before they do any.

WHERE IT GOES

  * `spacr/attribution.py`, headless and testable without Qt.
  * An OPT-IN write of the columns into `png_list`:
        grna_attributed, grna_attributed_p, gene_attributed,
        grna_attribution_entropy, grna_attribution_coverage
    Never automatic. Writing into measurements.db is not something a viewer
    does behind the user, which is the rule the montage already keeps.
  * A CELL-PICKING OPTION beside heuristic 1 -- "this should be one of the
    options the user can choose to pick cells" -- not a replacement for it
    until the validation below passes.

IT IS NOT A GENOTYPE, and the column names say so. The pooled design never
observed which cell carried what; this is an attribution under a model, and a
reader who takes `grna_attributed` for an observation has been misled by the
name alone.

HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED

* PERMUTATION: shuffle guide labels within a plate and re-attribute. Every
  posterior must collapse to its prior and the entropy go maximal. Structure
  surviving a permutation is structure the method invented.
* CALIBRATION: sum_i r_ig / N == pi_g to numerical tolerance, by
  construction -- so the test proves the solver converged.
* AN INDEPENDENT MEASUREMENT, WITHIN THE WELL. Validating TSG101 recruitment
  against an attribution made FROM the recruitment score is circular and the
  maintainer said so. `pathogen_area` is not: attribute on `pred`, then ask
  whether cells attributed to a pathway guide differ in `pathogen_area` from
  cells attributed to controls IN THE SAME WELL. Same well removes plate and
  well batch entirely, and it is a contrast the well-level fraction can never
  make.
* HELD-OUT PLATES: estimate the effects on three plates, attribute the fourth.
* Two guides with identical effects come back at exactly pi_1 : pi_2 for every
  cell -- flat, ambiguous, and correct. The data cannot separate them and the
  method must not pretend otherwise.

2026-08-19 -- OPTION C SHIPPED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`guide_attribution.posterior_multivariate` reads EVERY measurement instead of
one score: each guide carries a vector of effects, a cell's evidence is the
summed log-density across them, and the same iterative proportional fitting
then pins the guide masses to the sequencing. On a single column it is
byte-identical to option A -- a test asserts it, because if the two disagreed
there would be no way to tell which was wrong.

Two things decide whether it is honest rather than confident nonsense.

LOG SPACE. A product of 785 densities underflows to exactly zero in double
precision long before it reaches the end, and every cell then looks equally
impossible -- which the solver answers by handing back the prior. The bug
would present as "option C always says ambiguous", which reads as a property
of the data rather than of the arithmetic.

THE MEASUREMENTS ARE NOT INDEPENDENT. `cell_area` and `cell_perimeter` are one
measurement wearing two names, and multiplying their likelihoods counts the
same evidence twice. The summed log-likelihood is scaled by
`effective_dimension / n_measurements` -- the participation ratio of the
correlation matrix's eigenvalues, the same statistic the sweep uses to count
a guide's effective wells, applied to the other axis. Twelve copies of one
column measure 1.0, not 12. Without the correction the posterior saturates at
0 or 1 for every cell and the 0.55 threshold is decorative.

The correction is REPORTED: n_measurements, effective_dimension and the
scale factor come back with the posterior, so a reader can see how much of
their evidence was discounted.

A CELL-PICKING OPTION beside the other three, as asked. It needs the gene x
measurement sweep's effects grid -- one effect per measurement per guide --
and when there is none it falls back to the single-score attribution AND SAYS
SO in the caption. A montage that quietly changed how it chose its cells
would be the failure this tab is most careful about.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Screen: /mnt/firecuda2/Claude/toxoplasma_projects/tsg101_screen, plates 1-4.
226,467 classified cells, 1,536 wells, 1,006 guides above fraction_threshold.
Effects: ridge at well level with plate centred out. Guarded by
`SPACR_SCREEN=<folder> pytest -m slow
tests/test_the_screen_validates_the_attribution.py` -- 15 checks, all passing.

1. PERMUTATION -- PASSES, and cleanly.

                                    wells   called  entropy  |post - prior|
    real effects                     1350   0.4676   1.5364      0.082391
    all effects equal (null)         1350   0.4504   1.5654      0.000000
    guide labels shuffled (seed 0)   1350   0.4507   1.5651      0.006503
    guide labels shuffled (seed 1)   1350   0.4536   1.5650      0.007836
    guide labels shuffled (seed 2)   1350   0.4516   1.5651      0.008938

   With no guide distinguishable from any other the posterior is the prior
   EXACTLY -- 0.000000, not approximately. Shuffling the labels collapses the
   movement ~10x and raises the entropy toward maximal. Structure does not
   survive the permutation.

   READ `called` WITH CARE: ~45% of cells clear 0.55 even under the null,
   because in a well with few guides the PRIOR alone already exceeds it. A
   called cell is not evidence of attribution power; the movement from the
   prior is. This is worth saying in the UI.

2. THE WITHIN-WELL CONTROL CONTRAST -- CANNOT BE RUN, AND NOT BY ACCIDENT.

   The instruction asks whether cells attributed to a pathway guide differ in
   `pathogen_area` from cells attributed to CONTROLS in the same well. On this
   screen no cell can ever be attributed to a control:

     control guides in the whole screen        30  (TGGT1_000000_*)
     control guide-well pairs above 0.02       75
     wells holding a control                   71 of 1,536
     wells with both classes attributed         1        -- no test at n=1

   Lowering the threshold does not rescue it (333 wells at 0.005, 745 at
   0.002) because the limit is not abundance. A NON-TARGETING CONTROL HAS NO
   EFFECT TO ATTRIBUTE ON. The likelihood is what separates guides, a control's
   is flat by design, so it can never win the posterior. A method that
   confidently attributed cells to a non-targeting control would be inventing
   exactly the structure this module exists to avoid.

   THE VALIDATION AS WRITTEN IS UNRUNNABLE BY CONSTRUCTION. It should be
   replaced in this instruction rather than left as an outstanding item.

3. THE REPLACEMENT CONTRAST -- PASSES, and it is the honest one.

   Per GENE, within well, on `pathogen_area`: cells attributed to that gene
   against cells attributed to the other genes in the SAME well.

     REAL         genes testable  3    all three |t| > 5   max |t| 9.43
     shuffled x5  genes testable  0    -- not "none significant": none even
                                          reaches the minimum cell count

     gene    wells    mean diff (px)      t
     233460     55           -615.4    -9.43
     239740     98          +1128.0    +7.64
     220950     45          -1722.1    -5.94

   All three signs match the sign of the guide's effect on `pred`.

   IT IS NOT CIRCULAR, and this was checked rather than assumed:

4. HELD-OUT PLATES -- PASSES.

A REAL DEFECT THE SCREEN FOUND: `attributable` WAS NOT A BOUND. Fixed.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`attributable` is the pre-flight this instruction asks for -- "one number per
guide says whether it can produce a real assignment or only ever ambiguous".
It documented itself as arithmetic no amount of data could overturn:
"can NEVER reach the threshold: not with more cells, not with a better fit".

ON THESE FOUR PLATES IT DECLARED 230 OF 5,599 GUIDE-WELL PAIRS IMPOSSIBLE AND
THE SHIPPED ATTRIBUTION CALLED THEM ANYWAY. 113 distinct guides. One with a
stated ceiling of 0.0912 reached 0.9595.

Two separate errors:

  * IT EVALUATED THE RATIO AT THE GUIDE'S OWN CENTRE and called that the best
    possible score. It is not. For a positive effect the likelihood ratio
    against a flat competitor keeps growing as the score rises, so the
    plain-Bayes posterior tends to 1 -- there is NO finite ceiling over the
    whole real line. Wrong by 3x even against a competitor with no effect
    (said 0.1118, reached 0.4201).
  * THE COMPETITION IS NOT AT ITS OWN CENTRE. A guide competing against an
    OPPOSITE-signed effect separates from it at twice the rate. Said 0.1118,
    the shipped posterior reached 0.5493.

FIXED: the ceiling is now a maximum over the RANGE of scores a screen
produces (`span`, in units of scale), and takes the actual competition
(`others`, as (effect, prior) pairs). Omitting `others` keeps the old
signature and treats the rest of the well as one competitor with no effect --
which is the WEAKEST competition and so the most generous reading, stated as
such. With the competition passed, zero violations remain on all four plates.

A test asserted the old, false claim -- that effect=0.3 at prior=0.4 could
never be called -- and has been corrected. What is genuinely uncallable is a
ZERO effect, where the ceiling is exactly the prior.
