--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE STATE IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DISTANCE IS SOLVED. `spacr/object_distances.py` is thorough and recently
rewritten: a Euclidean distance transform per object type, computed once,
giving centre-to-surface, surface-to-surface and local-maximum-to-surface
between any two object types, plus where an object sits inside its parent.
It costs O(image pixels) rather than O(pairs). Nothing below should
re-implement any of it.

WHAT IS ABSENT is everything one level up from a distance:

  * NO SPATIAL POINT STATISTICS. Grepped 2026-09-09: `ripley` appears
    nowhere in the package. There is no test of whether objects are
    clustered, dispersed or random against a null -- only how far apart
    particular ones are.
  * NO NEIGHBOURHOOD COMPOSITION. No "of the k cells nearest this one, how
    many are infected".
  * NO BYSTANDER CONCEPT AT ALL. The word `bystander` does not occur
    anywhere in 227 modules. Cells are currently infected or they are not;
    an uninfected cell touching an infected one and an uninfected cell alone
    in a field are the same row.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The measurable unit in spaCR is the cell, and the biology of an intracellular
pathogen is not confined to the cell it is in. An infected cell changes what
its neighbours do -- secreted effectors, cytokine signalling, mechanical and
metabolic effects on the monolayer. Those neighbours are, right now,
statistically indistinguishable from cells on the far side of the well.

TWO CONSEQUENCES, and the second is the one that costs real results:

  1. A genuine bystander phenotype cannot be found, because there is no
     column that separates a bystander from a distant uninfected cell.

  2. IT QUIETLY DILUTES EVERY UNINFECTED CONTROL. If bystanders are
     phenotypically shifted and they are pooled into "uninfected", the
     control population is a mixture, its variance is inflated, and the
     effect size of every infection comparison in the package shrinks
     towards nothing. This is not a missing feature so much as a silent
     confound in results spaCR already produces.

For a lab whose questions are about the parasitophorous vacuole and what it
does to the host, this is close to the centre of the science.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. BYSTANDER CLASSIFICATION FIRST -- it is the cheapest and by far the most
   useful. A per-cell column: uninfected cells within a threshold distance
   of an infected one are bystanders, everything else is distal. The
   distance transform in `spacr/object_distances.py` already supplies the
   number; this is a threshold and a label, and it lands as a column family
   through `spacr/columns.py` so regression and hit calling pick it up with
   no changes.

   THE THRESHOLD MUST BE A SETTING WITH AN HONEST NAME AND A DEFAULT
   DERIVED FROM THE DATA -- a multiple of median cell diameter
   (`spacr/diameter.py` already measures it), not a hard-coded micron
   count. Instruction 364's standard applies.

2. NEIGHBOURHOOD COMPOSITION. For each cell, the infection status of its k
   nearest neighbours, and the local infected fraction. A KD-tree over
   centroids, which the package already builds.

3. RIPLEY'S K AND L, per well, against a null that respects the well
   geometry and the segmented mask -- not a rectangle. Edge correction is
   the part that gets skipped and the part that decides the answer; a K
   computed without it reports dispersion at the field boundary that is an
   artefact of the boundary.

4. ONLY THEN the comparison that motivates all of it: infected versus
   bystander versus distal, on any measured feature, as a first-class
   contrast the regression layer can express.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * BYSTANDER CLASSIFICATION: on a plate at low multiplicity, the bystander
    count must scale with the infected count and fall to zero in uninfected
    control wells. A well with no parasites reporting bystanders is a bug in
    the threshold, and it is the first thing to check.

  * RIPLEY'S K: THE TEST IS A SIMULATION, NOT A PLATE. Generate a known
    random point pattern inside a real mask and confirm K sits inside its
    envelope; generate a known clustered one and confirm it leaves. A K
    validated only on real data is a K nobody can debug, because real data
    has no ground truth about its own clustering.

  * THE PAYOFF MEASUREMENT, and the one worth reporting: take an existing
    infected-versus-uninfected comparison from a real screen, split the
    uninfected arm into bystander and distal, and show the effect size
    changes. If it does, every result computed the old way was diluted, and
    that is the finding. If it does not, that is worth knowing too and the
    file should say so.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELIBERATELY NOT DONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

REWRITING `spacr/object_distances.py`. The distance layer is correct and
recent. This work sits on top of it and calls it.

A GRAPH-NEURAL-NETWORK NEIGHBOURHOOD MODEL. Considered and rejected for now:
it would predict well and explain nothing, and the question here is which
neighbours differ and by how much -- a question a threshold and a contrast
answer directly and a learned message-passing model answers only by proxy.

TISSUE-STYLE NEIGHBOURHOOD CLUSTERING (cellular neighbourhoods, niches) as
in spatial transcriptomics. Rejected as premature: those methods assume many
cell types in structured tissue. A monolayer with two states does not need
them, and importing the vocabulary would suggest a richness the assay does
not have.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROGRESS -- 2026-09-10
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1, BYSTANDER CLASSIFICATION: code done, setting not wired.
`spacr/bystanders.py` is new and holds STATUSES ("infected", "bystander",
"distal"), `reach_from_diameter`, `distance_to_infected`, `classify` and
`counts`. The reach is a multiple of the measured cell diameter, as the
instruction requires. WHAT IS LEFT is exposing it as a SETTING with
`expected_types`, `tooltips` and a `categories` entry -- held because the
runtime catalog rebuild that would carry the new tooltip into nine locales
is home's and has not landed. The code takes the reach as an argument
today, so nothing is blocked on it but the GUI.

  ONE CORRECTION WORTH KEEPING. The first draft claimed touching cells sit
  at distance 0, taken from `surface_distance_transform`'s docstring. That
  is false for DISJOINT abutting labels: the nearest infected PIXEL is one
  step away, so touching cells sit at one spacing unit, not zero. A reach
  threshold written as `<= 0` would have classified every bystander as
  distal on exactly the plates the feature is for.

STEP 2, NEIGHBOURHOOD COMPOSITION: DONE. `neighbourhood(cell_mask,
infected_labels, k=6, spacing=None)` returns `label`,
`neighbours_counted`, `neighbours_infected` and `local_infected_fraction`,
over a cKDTree of the centroids the package already builds. It queries
k+1 and drops the first hit, because the first hit is always the cell
itself, and it REPORTS the denominator rather than assuming k -- a field
with fewer than k+1 cells cannot supply k neighbours, and a fraction over
an assumed denominator would be wrong in exactly the sparse fields a
bystander analysis is for.

STEP 3, RIPLEY'S K AND L: DONE, in `spacr/point_patterns.py` -- a new
module beside `object_distances`, not inside it, as this file asks.
`ripley_k`, `csr_envelope`, `ripley_test` and `suggest_radii`.

  EDGE CORRECTION IS THE REDUCED-SAMPLE (BORDER) ONE and it is the
  default. Analytic isotropic corrections assume a window whose geometry
  is known in closed form; a well with a torn monolayer is not one. The
  border correction is exact for ANY mask, and its cost -- points thrown
  away at large r -- is reported in `n_contributing` rather than hidden.
  `tests/test_a_well_is_clustered_or_it_is_not.py` reproduces the artefact
  the instruction warns about: uncorrected, a KNOWN random pattern reports
  dispersion at every radius, and the deficit grows with r.

  THE FRAME EDGE IS A BOUNDARY, and this is the subtle half. The
  distance-to-boundary field runs on a mask padded with one row of
  background, because `distance_transform_edt` measures to the nearest
  zero IN THE ARRAY -- a mask filling the image has no zero to find and
  every pixel comes back as deep interior, so no point is ever dropped
  however large r gets. The pad is one line of code and it is the
  difference between a correction and the appearance of one.

  THE NULL IS SCATTERED IN THE MASK, without replacement. With
  replacement, a repeated point is a pair at distance zero, which puts
  clustering into the null itself and raises the envelope at exactly the
  small radii a focus of infection would show at.

  THE TEST IS A SIMULATION, NOT A PLATE, as required: random stays inside
  its envelope, clustered leaves it upwards, a lattice leaves it
  downwards, and the same three answers come back on a DISC window whose
  area is under 64% of its bounding box.

  ONE RECORDED PROPERTY, so nobody "fixes" it later: a lattice OVERSHOOTS
  at its own spacing. Below the spacing a grid has no neighbours at all
  and K sits under the envelope; at the spacing four neighbours arrive at
  once, and a step function crossing lambda*pi*r^2 must overshoot. K
  oscillates around the null for a regular pattern -- only the small-r
  deficit is a stable read of dispersion. There is a test named for it.

STEP 4, THE THREE-WAY CONTRAST: DONE. `bystanders.compare(frame, feature)`
gives one row per neighbourhood with `n`, `mean`, `std` and `median`, every
requested status present even at n = 0 so a join across plates cannot
silently change meaning. `std` is ddof=1 deliberately: the confound this
item exists to remove is an INFLATED CONTROL VARIANCE, so the spread
reported has to be the sample estimate a reader would compare against.
NO TEST STATISTIC IS CHOSEN HERE -- summarising is safe on any column,
but the test depends on how the wells nest and what the null is, and
belongs where those are known. A test pins the confound directly: the same
six cells pooled as "uninfected" have a wider spread than either
population inside them.
