================================================================================
THE CELL TAB OFFERS THE BEST WAYS TO ANNOTATE, NOT ONLY THE OBVIOUS ONE
================================================================================

Status:    OPEN. Requested 2026-08-25.
Owner:     the Regression module's cell tab, and whatever fits the chosen
           cells afterwards.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WAS ASKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"in regress make sure that the cell tabs setting for annotating include all
the best techneques to annotate these cells, if you can thing of any other
methods (e.g. for top scoring cells annotate top scoring cells in chosen
gRNA wells and a random number (same number as top scorring class) then
build XGBoost on those vs rest and apply to rest of cels in screen and gRNA
wells, this is just an example)"

So: the tab should offer a MENU of annotation strategies, the named one
among them, and each should say what it is for.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE THAT WAS NAMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TOP-SCORING VERSUS A MATCHED RANDOM DRAW. In the chosen gRNA wells, take the
top-scoring cells as the positive set; draw the same number of cells at
random as the contrast set; fit XGBoost on the two; apply it to every
remaining cell in the screen and in those wells.

THE TRAP IN IT, which the control below exists for: the score already
encodes the phenotype, so a model trained on high-score against random can
succeed by relearning the score and nothing else. The honest version holds
out the score's own inputs, or reports how much of the fit survives when
they are removed. Offer it, and say so where it is offered.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE OTHERS WORTH OFFERING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. UNCERTAINTY SAMPLING. Annotate the cells a current model is least sure
     of -- nearest the decision boundary. Each label buys the most there,
     and it is the cheapest way to spend a fixed annotation budget.

  2. DIVERSITY SAMPLING. Cluster the measured features (or the embedding)
     and annotate a representative of each cluster. Uncertainty sampling
     alone chases one boundary and never sees a rare phenotype; this is
     what stops a whole morphology going unlabelled.

  3. CONTROL WELLS AS ANCHORS. Where the plate has known positive and
     negative control wells, seed the labels from them -- no manual
     annotation at all, and the labels carry the experiment's own
     definition of the phenotype rather than an annotator's.

  4. POSITIVE-UNLABELLED LEARNING, done properly. The named method is PU
     learning with the unlabelled set treated as negative, which biases the
     fit. A PU estimator that treats the contrast set as UNLABELLED rather
     than negative is the same idea without the bias.

  5. SELF-TRAINING. Fit, take the predictions above a confidence threshold
     as labels, refit, repeat. Cheap, and it drifts: hold a fixed audit set
     out of every round and stop when it stops improving.

  6. DISAGREEMENT BETWEEN TWO VIEWS. Fit two models on different feature
     families -- intensity against shape, say -- and annotate where they
     disagree. Those cells are where a single model is confidently wrong.

  7. STRATIFY ACROSS THE SCORE RANGE. Annotate a fixed number per score
     decile rather than only the top. A model shown only extremes has never
     seen the middle, which is most of the screen.

  8. NEIGHBOUR PROPAGATION. Label a cell and carry the label to its nearest
     neighbours in feature space, with a distance cut. Multiplies an
     annotator's effort; needs the cut shown, because too generous a one
     manufactures agreement.

  9. AN UNBIASED RANDOM SAMPLE, ALWAYS. Whatever else is chosen, a plain
     random draw held aside is the only thing that can measure what the
     clever strategy did to the class balance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO RULES THAT APPLY TO ALL OF THEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

GROUP BY WELL. Cells from one well are not independent. A split that puts
some cells of a well in train and others in test reports a score that the
same model will not reach on a new plate. This is already the subject of
its own instruction and must hold here too.

THE STRATEGY THAT CHOSE THE LABELS CANNOT ALSO SCORE THEM. Every method
above selects cells non-randomly, so an accuracy measured on the cells it
chose is optimistic by construction. Each one reports against the random
hold-out in 9, not against its own selection.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * the cell tab offers the strategies as a named choice, each with a
    sentence saying what it is for and what it costs;
  * the named top-scoring method runs end to end and reports how much of
    the fit survives without the score's own inputs;
  * every method's reported score is measured on the random hold-out, and
    a split never puts one well's cells on both sides;
  * choosing a strategy with no labelled data available says so rather than
    fitting on nothing.
