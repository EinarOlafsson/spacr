================================================================================
THE RESPONSE HISTOGRAM, BEFORE AND AFTER THE TRANSFORM, ON ONE GRAPH
================================================================================

Status:    filed 2026-08-21, not started.
Requested: 2026-08-21 -- "i want to see a histogram of the dependent variable
           distribution after and before transformation (same graph) in the
           graphs clearly stating what type of distribution each is, normal
           bets, etcetera".
Owner:     Regression QC, figures

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT BELONGS IN THE FIGURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE TRANSFORM IS THE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL SETTING NOBODY LOOKS AT. It decides
which model family is even appropriate, and it is chosen from a dropdown
whose effect is invisible until the coefficients come out the other end. A
user who picks the wrong one gets a fit that runs, plots and exports, and is
wrong in a way no error can catch.

One panel showing both distributions makes the choice inspectable in the
moment it matters, and ON ONE GRAPH rather than two, because the question is
what CHANGED and two panels side by side is a comparison the reader has to
do themselves.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE PANEL SHOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * both histograms, before and after, distinguishable and overlaid or
    paired on shared axes;
  * THE NAME OF EACH DISTRIBUTION -- "normal", "beta", "bounded and skewed",
    "binary" -- stated on the panel, not left for the reader to judge by
    eye. That is the substance of the request;
  * the statistic behind the name, so the label can be disagreed with: the
    normality p-value and the skew are already measured by the advisor's
    `Reading` and by `check_distribution`.

`spacr.ml.check_distribution` ALREADY NAMES THE FAMILY and returns one of
'logit', 'quasi_binomial', 'beta', 'ols' or 'glm'. It is the classifier this
panel should label with -- not a second opinion written for the plot, which
would let the picture and the fitted family disagree about the same data.

A TRANSFORM THAT CHANGED NOTHING SHOULD BE VISIBLE AS SUCH. When 'none' is
selected the panel still draws, with both curves identical and the label
saying so, rather than being absent -- an absent panel reads as a missing
feature rather than as an answer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* One panel carries both distributions on shared axes.
* Each is named, and the name comes from the same function that picks the
  regression family.
* The statistic behind each name is shown.
* Selecting 'none' draws the panel rather than omitting it.
* A skewed response that becomes symmetric is visibly so, checked on
  generated data where the answer is known.
