================================================================================
EVERY GENE AGAINST EVERY MEASUREMENT, IN ONE TABLE
================================================================================

Status:    DONE 2026-08-20. See the closing section.
Requested: 2026-08-19 -- "please bake all of this in to spacr. i would also
           like a button that compares every gene to every measurement and
           gives me a comprehensive table of this and visualises it in an
           intuitive way."

WHAT "ALL OF THIS" IS. Three corrections were found by hand while sweeping one
gene, in about ten minutes, and every one of them would be made again by a
user doing it themselves. They are the reason this is a feature and not a
script.

  1. IDENTIFIERS ARE NOT MEASUREMENTS. `pathogen_object_label` came out at
     p=2.5e-07 for EAF1 and it is a LABEL: the cells picked out sit lower in
     the segmentation's numbering. That is position in a list, not biology.
     `pathogen_pathogen` is the same column under another name -- spearman
     0.9979 against `pathogen_object_label`, identical in 141,626 of 226,467
     rows -- and it slipped past the first filter. Both were nearly reported
     as findings.

  2. 785 TESTS IS NOT ONE TEST. Uncorrected, `pathogen_solidity` at p=1.8e-03
     looked interesting; under Benjamini-Hochberg it is nothing. The scan
     already knows this (`spacr.measurement_scan` corrects within AND across
     measurements) and the sweep must too.

  3. CIRCULARITY IS A COLUMN, NOT A FOOTNOTE. The classification score is
     itself a function of the image, so a measurement it already tracks cannot
     validate anything derived from it. Measured on this screen:
     spearman(pred, pathogen_channel_1_mean_intensity) = -0.389, and the
     "strongest result" for GRA14 was exactly that measurement, in exactly the
     direction the correlation predicts. Every row of the table carries
     |rho(pred, measurement)| so a reader can discount it without being told.

WHAT TO BUILD

A. THE SWEEP, at the WELL level, as one matrix operation. Measured: the full
   guide x measurement grid -- 1,376 guides x 785 measurements over 1,366
   wells -- is ONE matmul and takes 0.1 s. Anything that loops is doing it
   wrong. Blocked on plate, so a plate difference cannot become a gene effect.

B. THE TABLE: gene (and guide), measurement, effect, p, q, circularity, and
   the well count behind it. Sortable, filterable, and exportable, because the
   answer to "every gene against every measurement" is a million rows and
   nobody reads a million rows.

C. THE PICTURE. A million cells is not a heatmap anybody can read, so the
   default view is the SURVIVORS: the genes and measurements that have at
   least one entry surviving the correction, clustered so related measurements
   sit together. Everything else is one click away, never the first thing
   shown.

D. AND THE CELL-LEVEL SWEEP FOR ONE GENE stays available beside it -- the
   assignment from 173, then the same three corrections. That is the slow,
   deep version of the same question and it answers a different one: not
   "does this gene move this measurement" but "do the cells I attributed to it
   differ".

HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED

* The whole grid computes in seconds, not minutes.
* No identifier column appears in the table at all.
* Every row carries its q value and its circularity, and the default sort
  cannot put an uncorrected p at the top.
* A gene with no effect on anything produces no survivors, and the picture
  says so rather than showing a colourful grid of noise.
* The EAF1 result reproduces: its three low-circularity survivors are the
  pathogen channel co-localisation measures, at q ~ 1e-2.

THREE THINGS THE TESTS CAUGHT IN MY OWN CODE, all of which would have been
reported as findings:
  * "path" is a substring of "pathogen", so a substring rule for identifier
    names silently dropped EVERY pathogen measurement -- 354 columns.
  * A guide in 7 of 1,366 wells was given the whole screen's degrees of
    freedom and reported p = 0.0. The participation ratio fixed it and took
    the survivor count from 5,959 to 186.
  * Circularity that was never computed came back as 0.00 and the panel
    displayed it, which reads as "nothing here is circular".

ALSO SHIPPED
  * MOUNTED on the Measurements tab, beside the scan, wired to the merged
    frame, the counts (through the same reader the montage uses) and the
    score CSVs.
  * GENE LEVEL, which is the question actually asked. A gene's fraction in a
    well is the SUM of its guides', the same rule the regression applies, so
    "does this gene move this measurement" is not a different arithmetic from
    the fit that found the gene. `level` takes 'gene' (the panel's default),
    'guide', or 'both' -- and with 'both' a gene and one of its guides are
    different rows that a reader can tell apart.

    The mapping needed its own rule. `hits.gene_of` reads a DESIGN TERM and
    truncates at the first underscore; handed the bare `TGGT1_225160_2` that
    a count table actually carries, it returns `TGGT1` -- the organism, for
    every guide in the screen, pooling the whole library into one "gene".

2026-08-19 -- ALL TEN VIEWS BUILT.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"i want you to implement afew ways of visualizing this data, come up with 10
ways, ill tell you what i like" -- then, after four: "For 175 build all of the
I'll let you know which one I like after looking at all of them!"

WRITTEN DOWN HERE, because the first four were built from a conversation and
the other six were nearly lost with it. Each answers a question the others
cannot; that is the test for whether a view earns its place.

  1. HEATMAP -- what survived, clustered so neighbours are alike.
     `plot_sweep`. The default: it is what "a comprehensive table visualised
     intuitively" asked for. Survivors only; the whole grid is a texture.

  2. CALIBRATION -- is the screen calibrated at all? `plot_calibration`.
     Observed P against the uniform, with the inflation factor lambda named
     on the plot. THE FIRST THING TO READ and the last thing anybody builds:
     it says whether the other nine are worth reading. Not the default,
     because opening on a QQ plot answers a question the user did not ask.

  3. GRID VOLCANO -- every pair, effect against evidence. `plot_grid_volcano`.
     The shape of the WHOLE grid, which #1 cannot show. Coloured by
     circularity where it is known and grey where it is not, with the legend
     saying which -- grey is not "clean".

  4. HITS vs REPRESENTATION -- `plot_effect_against_representation`. The
     maintainer's own question: "220950 is waaay over represented". x is the
     EFFECTIVE WELL COUNT, not `share`; see the function for why the two
     order genes oppositely.

  5. MEASUREMENT FAMILIES -- `plot_measurement_families`. What KIND of thing
     each gene moves, in six coarse families. "moves pathogen intensity and
     nothing else" is a sentence about biology; "41 significant
     measurements" is not.

  6. GENE PROFILE -- `plot_gene_profile`. One gene's fingerprint: every
     measurement it moves, ordered, coloured by family. The per-gene readout
     a row of a heatmap cannot give. A gene with nothing significant SAYS
     SO and shows its strongest effects anyway.

  7. GENE SIMILARITY -- `plot_gene_similarity`. Which genes behave alike,
     by correlating whole effect profiles. The only view that reads two
     genes against each other, and the honest way to ask "is my hit list one
     finding or twelve". Correlated across the WHOLE row, because a shared
     sub-threshold pattern is exactly the evidence that two genes belong
     together.

  8. WHICH MEASUREMENTS DISCRIMINATE -- `plot_measurement_hits`. The grid
     read down its other axis. A measurement moved by half the library is a
     plate effect wearing a measurement's name; it is marked in red.

  9. CIRCULARITY -- `plot_circularity`. Corroboration or restatement, for
     every surviving pair. Drawn rather than filtered: 0.15 is a working bar
     and not a law, and a reader should see where their own hits fall.
     Refuses to draw when circularity was never computed.

 10. GUIDE CONCORDANCE -- `plot_guide_concordance`. Do a gene's own guides
     agree on the sign? The only internal control this design has.

ALL TEN ARE IN THE PANEL'S CHOOSER and all ten save beside the table. Every
one returns None rather than an empty axis when there is nothing to draw: a
picture of nothing reads as "no effect" when it means "no data", and that
misreading is the thing this whole instruction is about.

2026-08-19 (later) -- THE FILTERS AND THE FIGURES.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT TO LEAVE OUT, asked for and built: `drop_measurements`, `drop_guides`
(matched at both levels), `max_wells_fraction` (the over-representation
filter) and `max_share`. Breadth and share are kept apart because a guide can
be in every well at a low fraction or in three wells at 0.8, and 220950 is
extreme on both. What was left out is PRINTED, or a sweep that quietly
dropped a gene would send the reader hunting for a row that was never
computed.

THE TEN FIGURES ARE REBUILT in the apicomplexan-genomics idiom (the
`apicomplexan-figures` skill). Everything grey except the claim; dots and a
mean line where the old code drew a bar for n = 2-4 guides, which that skill
explicitly refuses; a bubble plot for measurement enrichment; legends as
coloured text; no gridlines. Data colour comes from the paper's palette and
INK from the theme, so they read in the application and would still print.

2026-08-20 (later) -- THE PICK. MEASURED, AND THE ANSWER IS ALL TEN.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The maintainer asked for the ten to be measured rather than argued about, and
for the weak ones cut. THEY WERE MEASURED AND NOTHING SHOULD BE CUT -- and
the measurement found something better than a cut.

RUN ON A SCREEN-SHAPED SWEEP -- 12 genes x 3 guides, 40 measurements, 4
plates, per-well scores -- every one of the ten draws, and no two answer the
same question. `WHAT_EACH_ONE_ANSWERS` in the test file records the question
each one is for, so dropping one later is a decision rather than an
archaeology exercise.

The closest pair is `plot_measurement_families` and `plot_measurement_hits`,
and they are the TWO MARGINS of one matrix: what kind of thing a gene moves,
and which measurements are moved most. A reader chasing a hit needs the
first; a reader asking whether one measurement is doing all the work needs
the second. That is not a duplicate.

AND THE MEASUREMENT FOUND THE REAL FAULT, which was in the test rather than
in the figures. The fixture was TWO GUIDES AND THREE COLUMNS, one gene each,
and three of the ten could not draw on it at all:

    plot_guide_concordance   needs several guides of ONE gene -- concordance
                             is a statement about guides, and every guide was
                             its own gene
    plot_gene_similarity     needs enough genes to cluster
    plot_circularity         needs per-well classification scores, which the
                             fixture did not pass

All three returned None and their tests SKIPPED. A SKIP IS NOT A PASS: the
two checks a pooled screen most depends on -- "do a gene's guides agree" and
"is this association just the classification score again" -- were untested
for as long as they have existed, and a panel that CANNOT draw looked exactly
like one with nothing to draw.

A fourth was hidden the same way: `_draw` passed the gene name "A", which was
the old fixture's guide. On a library shaped like a real one it names
nothing, so `plot_gene_profile` skipped too.

The fixture is screen-shaped now and the gene comes from the result. Six
skips became zero.
