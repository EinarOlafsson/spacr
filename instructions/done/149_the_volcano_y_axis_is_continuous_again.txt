--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE OBSERVATION IS CORRECT, AND IT IS NOT A BUG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MEASURED on the maintainer's own runs, 2026-08-18:

    guide_permutation_22   823 q values, 31 DISTINCT (4%)
                           19 tied levels covering 811 of 823 coefficients
    ols_10                1169 q values, 28 DISTINCT (2%)
                           8 tied levels covering 1149

    239740_3 (GRA14)  p = 5.0e-06   q = 4.1150e-03
    225160_2          p = 1.0e-05   q = 4.1150e-03     <- the report, exactly

BH's adjusted p is a CUMULATIVE MINIMUM taken from the largest p downwards:

    q_(i) = min over j >= i of ( n * p_(j) / j )

The `min` is what enforces monotonicity -- an adjusted p must not decrease as
the raw p increases -- and it is also what creates the ties. The moment a
later rank produces a smaller value, EVERY earlier rank is pulled down to it,
so a whole block of coefficients collapses onto one number with nothing
between. That is the procedure working, not binning.

AND THAT IS THE CEILING. q cannot go below min_j (n*p_(j)/j) whatever the
smallest p is. On ols_13 the raw p reaches 2.873e-13 while BH's floor is
1.741e-10, attained at rank j=2 -- so the top TWO sit on the floor, tied.

THE PERMUTATION PATH IS DISCRETE TWICE OVER, which is why it looks worst. A
permutation p is (1 + #{null <= observed}) / (n + 1), so with 1,000
permutations there are only 1,001 possible values and many guides already
share one before BH ever runs. Then the step function collapses them further:
823 coefficients onto 31 levels.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. THE FIX: RAW P ON THE AXIS, q FOR THE CALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Plot -log10(RAW p) on y and let q decide COLOUR and the significance line.

That is what the field does and it is honest rather than a workaround: the
axis shows the evidence per test, which is continuous, and the colour shows
which of them survive multiple-testing control, which is the discrete thing it
actually is. Nothing is hidden and nothing is invented -- the same two numbers
are on the plot, each doing the job it can do.

It also explains the maintainer's own observation that "when log(p) is on the
x axis the graph looks great": that axis is already the raw p.

THE CAPTION MUST SAY WHICH IS WHICH. A volcano whose height is raw p and whose
colour is FDR, with nothing saying so, is a figure a reader will misread in
the direction of over-confidence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. AND OFFER A GENUINELY CONTINUOUS FDR QUANTITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a reader who wants an FDR number ON the axis, the answer is the LOCAL
false discovery rate rather than BH's tail-area q. lfdr is a density ratio --
the posterior probability that a given test is null -- so it is continuous BY
CONSTRUCTION and has no step function and no ceiling.

Offer it beside BH rather than instead of it: they answer different questions,
and `spacr.multiple_testing` already holds thirteen corrections, so this is
one more entry in a table that exists.

Storey's q is NOT the answer here and the instruction says so: it applies a
pi0 scaling to the same cumulative minimum, so it moves the values and keeps
the steps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. MORE PERMUTATIONS, FOR THE PERMUTATION PATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 4%-distinct case is the permutation run, and its raw p is quantised to
1/(n+1) before BH sees it. `guide_permutations` is a setting; raising it is
the direct fix for that half, and the box should say what resolution the
current value buys -- 1,000 permutations cannot express a p below 1e-3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. THE AXIS SPLIT, WHICH WAS ASKED FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"the option to insert an axis split on the y axis".

Worth having and worth being honest about: it fixes the DYNAMIC RANGE -- a
handful of hits at 1e-46 flattening everything else into the floor -- and it
does not fix the discreteness. Build it, and do not sell it as the answer to
the ties.

spaCR already has this for the legacy volcano: `y_lims` accepted
`[[low1, high1], [low2, high2]]` and `spacr.toxo._normalize_y_lims` drew a
broken axis. That behaviour was retired as a SETTING with instruction 135; it
belongs on the interactive plot's right-click menu instead, under 147's Axes
category.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* The volcano's y axis is raw p by default, continuous, with q deciding the
  colour and the line -- and the caption says so.
* A local-FDR option exists and its values are distinct where BH's are tied.
* The permutation box states the finest p its permutation count can express.
* The y axis can be split from the plot's own menu.
* Switching to adjusted p on the axis is still possible, and still shows the
  steps, because that is what BH is.


================================================================================
E. THE CORRECTION IS CHOSEN ON THE GRAPH
================================================================================

Requested: 2026-08-18 -- "id also like to do fdr by right clicking the graph."

`spacr.multiple_testing.METHODS` already holds thirteen corrections and the
run picks one. The PLOT should offer them too, under 147's Data category, and
recompute on the spot -- comparing BH against Bonferroni against Storey on
the screen in front of you is a two-second question that currently costs a
re-run.

TWO THINGS IT MUST NOT DO.

  * IT MUST NOT SILENTLY DISAGREE WITH THE EXPORTED TABLE. results.csv carries
    a `multiple_testing_method` column and its q values came from the run. A
    plot recorrected to something else is showing a different analysis from
    the file beside it, and that is how a figure and its table end up telling
    a reader two things. The plot SAYS which correction it is drawing and
    whether that is the run's: "Bonferroni (the run used fdr_bh)".
  * IT MUST RECORRECT THE RIGHT FAMILY. The correction applies within a level
    -- `spacr.hits.tested_family`, and each of the two fits is its own family
    (instruction 128 R). Recomputing over whatever happens to be on screen
    would pool two families and quietly change every number.

Offer to write the re-corrected table, or say plainly that it is a view only.
Do not leave it ambiguous.

================================================================================
F. EVERY FIELD-ACCEPTABLE WAY OF SHOWING THE ADJUSTED P
================================================================================

Requested: 2026-08-18 -- "id like the user to have access to visualizing
adjusted P in all the ways that are acceptable to the field. showing as color,
showing the descrete P on the axis buy showing the line where the adjusted p
threshold lands, etc."

All of these keep the axis continuous. Offer them; do not pick one silently.

1. RAW P ON THE AXIS, COLOUR BY q <= alpha.
   The most common volcano in the literature. Continuous height, binary
   colour, and the colour is doing what the house style says colour is for --
   carrying the claim.

2. RAW P ON THE AXIS, WITH THE BH CRITICAL LINE. This is the one the request
   describes and it is exact rather than approximate. Under BH at level alpha,
   with n tests,

       q_(i) <= alpha   if and only if   p_(i) <= alpha * i / n

   for the largest i satisfying it. Call that rank k; then EVERY test with
   p <= p_(k) is called and every test above it is not. So a single horizontal
   line at -log10(p_(k)) divides the plot exactly as the FDR does, on a
   continuous axis, with no steps anywhere.

   THE LINE IS NOT alpha. Drawing the line at -log10(0.05) is the mistake this
   replaces: that is the uncorrected threshold and it calls far too much. The
   caption gives both numbers -- "FDR 5% (raw p <= 1.7e-4)" -- because the
   second is the one the reader is measuring against.

   When NOTHING is called, there is no k and there is no line. Say that
   instead of drawing one at alpha: an empty result is a finding.

3. ADJUSTED P ON THE AXIS. Honest, stepped, and kept -- because that is what
   BH is, and a user comparing against a published figure drawn that way needs
   to be able to reproduce it. It is not the default.

4. LOCAL FDR ON THE AXIS. Continuous by construction; see section B.

5. A CONTINUOUS COLOUR RAMP OVER q rather than a binary call. Available, and
   it CONFLICTS with the up/down colouring -- a dot cannot be coloured for its
   direction and for its q at once. Whichever is chosen, the other becomes
   grey, and the legend says which is in force. That is the localisation rule
   again: one sentence per figure.

6. SIZE OR OPACITY BY q, leaving colour for direction. The one encoding that
   composes with the others rather than competing.

WHAT NOT TO OFFER: jitter on the y axis to separate the ties. It moves a point
away from its own value, which is the one thing a plot may never do -- and
this instruction exists because a plot was showing something the data did not
say.

2026-08-18 -- SHIPPED. The axis is continuous and the line is exact.

WHAT SHIPPED

  F.1  RAW P ON THE AXIS BY DEFAULT. `VolcanoPlot.p_axis()` is "raw" and the
       height is -log10(raw p), continuous. q decides the COLOUR (two
       brushes, a two-entry legend "called (n)" / "not called (n)") and the
       LINE. On a screen with no category colouring the colour channel now
       carries the call, which is the field's own volcano.

  F.2  THE BH CRITICAL LINE, EXACT. `multiple_testing.critical_p_value()`.
       Not the F.2 formula transcribed -- computed from the correction's own
       REJECTION CALL, so the line cannot disagree with the colours beside
       it, and so ONE function answers for all thirteen methods instead of
       one. Checked against the textbook identity anyway:

           p_(k) from  q_(i) <= alpha iff p_(i) <= alpha*i/n   =  5.6997e-05
           critical_p_value(p, "fdr_bh", 0.05)                 =  5.6997e-05

       The premise is that every correction's rejection region is a LOWER
       SET. That is asserted, not assumed: a parametrised test walks all
       thirteen and checks no unrejected p sits below the largest rejected
       one. All thirteen pass.

       NOTHING CALLED -> NO LINE, and the caption says "Nothing is called at
       FDR 0.05, so there is no threshold line to draw."

       TWO FAMILIES WITH DIFFERENT CUTS GET A LINE EACH, named. One line is
       exact for one family; drawing one for two would be exact for one of
       them and wrong for the other.

  F.3  ADJUSTED P ON THE AXIS. Still there, still stepped, not the default.

  E    THE CORRECTION ON THE GRAPH. All thirteen from `METHODS` under a
       "Correction" group, recomputed on the spot. It says which is drawn and
       whether it is the run's ("Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (the run's)"), and it
       says "The run used fdr_bh; this is a VIEW and the exported table still
       holds the run's q" when they differ. Not left ambiguous: there is also
       "Write this correction as a table…", which writes q, local_fdr and
       called_at_<alpha> beside the run's own columns.

       THE FAMILY IS RESPECTED. `hits.family_labels()` is the single
       statement of the split; each level is corrected separately, and the
       caption says "Corrected within each level separately (gene, grna)".

       AND THE PLOT CHECKS ITSELF AGAINST THE TABLE. When the correction in
       force IS the run's, the recomputed q is compared with the q_value
       column; a mismatch prints "WARNING: recomputing the run's own method
       does not reproduce the table's q values". IT FIRED FOR REAL while this
       was being written, on a fixture whose q had been corrected over 823
       rows against a plot correcting the 822 that are hypotheses. It is the
       single most useful thing in this change.

  D    THE Y-AXIS SPLIT, under Axes. `set_y_split(low, high)` /
       `clear_y_split()`. A piecewise-linear ruler with the tick labels
       printing the DATA's own values across the break, so no mark is
       anywhere its number is not. The status line says: "A split gives the
       rest of the screen its height back; it does not make a stepped
       adjusted P continuous."

       IT REFUSES A BAND THAT HOLDS POINTS, with the count. A mark inside the
       hidden band has no number left on the ruler to sit at, and drawing it
       in the break is the jitter this instruction forbids by another route.

  B    LOCAL FDR ON THE AXIS -- done, and the estimator was CHANGED once on a
       measurement. The first build used the Grenander estimator (the
       decreasing density's nonparametric MLE, what fdrtool uses). It is
       assumption-free and it is a STEP FUNCTION, so it reproduced on this
       axis the exact discreteness the option exists to escape:

           823 tests   BH q: 48 distinct   Grenander lfdr: 8   raw p: 823

       Replaced with the beta-uniform mixture (Pounds and Morris), fitted by
       closed-form EM -- no bandwidth, no bin count, no optimiser tolerance:

           the same 823 tests                 lfdr: 823 distinct

       The shape assumption (alternatives ~ Beta(a,1)) is what buys the
       continuity and it is stated in the docstring and in the caption.
       Below `LOCAL_FDR_MIN_TESTS` (20) it returns 1 everywhere and the menu
       entry greys out saying "a family of 8 is too small to read a density
       off" rather than drawing a curve fitted to a dozen numbers.

  C    HALF DONE, AND THE HALF THAT IS DONE IS THE PLOT'S. The volcano now
       detects a quantised raw p and says so: "The RAW p is itself quantised
       -- 562 distinct values among 823, the smallest 0.000999 -- so a
       permutation p can be no finer than 1/(permutations + 1)". THE
       SETTINGS BOX IS NOT MINE (spacr/settings.py) -- see the handoff.

NOT DONE, DELIBERATELY

  * F.5 (a continuous colour ramp over q) and F.6 (size/opacity by q) are not
    built. F.5 conflicts with the up/down colouring by construction and the
    binary call is what F.1 asks for; F.6 composes but nobody asked for it.
  * NO Y-AXIS JITTER, and there is a test named for it: two tied q values
    land on exactly the same height on the adjusted axis.

WHAT COST TIME, SO IT COSTS NOBODY ELSE ANY

  * `round(p, 15)` IS NOT ROUNDING TO 15 SIGNIFICANT DIGITS. It was used to
    dedupe two families' critical values and it sent every p below 1e-15 to
    ZERO -- so a screen whose critical p is 1.7e-20 drew its line at
    -log10(0) and pyqtgraph died with "cannot convert float NaN to integer",
    a traceback naming nothing. Never round a P value to decimal places.
  * THE LOCAL FDR MUST BE LAZY. Computed on every redraw it was 25 ms of a
    40 ms redraw on the real screen's 1,215 coefficients -- more than drawing
    the plot -- and `test_colouring_does_not_cost_a_brush_per_point` caught
    it at 32 ms against its 30 ms budget. Computed on demand: 8.9 ms.
  * `hits.family_labels` had to be VECTORISED for the same reason: 2.1 ms per
    redraw as a Python loop over `guide_of`.
  * THE HOST STILL DRIVES THE AXIS THROUGH `p_column`, and honouring that had
    to be one-way-until-overruled. `RegressionResultsPanel` redraws on every
    level, baseline and compartment change; honouring it unconditionally made
    any one of them silently undo a choice the user had made on the menu.

WHAT SHIPPED, all of it in spacr/qt/widgets/fast_plots.py::VolcanoPlot.

  F.5  A CONTINUOUS COLOUR RAMP OVER q. `set_q_colour("ramp")` / `"call"`,
       under a new "Show the FDR as" group beside the Correction.

       IT TAKES THE COLOUR CHANNEL AND NAMES WHAT IT TOOK IT FROM. A dot

       colouring that would otherwise be in force is not drawn and the

         "Colour is a continuous ramp over q, brightest at the smallest q;
          the condition colouring is off while it is."

       Silently dropping it is how a reader ends up reading a q ramp as a
       condition, which is this instruction's own failure mode.

       THE RAMP IS OVER -log10(q), NOT OVER q. q values pile up near 1 and
       the interesting ones are decades apart, so a linear ramp over q spends
       nearly all its colour on the tests nobody is looking at. viridis, from
       the five perceptually uniform maps this module already allows on a
       continuous quantity.

       ONE q FOR THE WHOLE SCREEN GREYS THE ENTRY, saying "every q on this
       plot is the same number, so a ramp would be one colour". That is the
       staircase at its limit and it is a real state on a permutation run.

  F.6  SIZE OR OPACITY BY q. `set_q_mark("size" | "opacity" | "none")`.

       THE ONE ENCODING THAT COMPOSES, and the test that proves it drives a
       volcano coloured by condition, turns size on, and asserts the brush
       set is UNCHANGED while the diameters spread. Opacity replaces only the
       alpha of each point's own brush, so both conditions are still on the
       plot as themselves.

       A FADED POINT IS NEVER INVISIBLE. `Q_ALPHA_RANGE` floors at 55: a
       point faded to nothing is a point removed from the plot, and this

`add_scatter` grew `size_list`. A float array goes straight into pyqtgraph's

2026-08-18 -- C IS DONE. THE BOX STATES THE FLOOR.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`guide_permutations` said "More permutations improve tail resolution but take
longer", which reads as a quality-against-time trade-off. It is not one, it is
a HARD FLOOR, and the box says so now:

    P = (exceedances + 1) / (permutations + 1)   [spacr/guide_permutation.py:312]

so the smallest P that can ever be reported is 1 / (permutations + 1) and every
guide at least that extreme is given exactly it.

      1,000 permutations  ->  cannot express P < 1e-3   (1/1001 = 9.99e-4)
     10,000 permutations  ->  cannot express P < 1e-4
    200,000 (the default) ->  resolves the tail to 5e-6

The box also names the SYMPTOM, because a reader who has one needs to
recognise it rather than compute it: a stack of guides sharing one identical P
at the floor, which BH cannot separate and the volcano draws as a flat row of
dots. That is the 4%-distinct case this section is about.

Two tests: one on the sentence, one pinning the formula against
`spacr.guide_permutation` itself, so a tooltip that drifted from the code
fails rather than misinforms. A third drives the real regression panel and
asserts the sentence reaches the control.
