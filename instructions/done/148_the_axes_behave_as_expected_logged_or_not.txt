================================================================================
THE X AND Y AXES BEHAVE AS EXPECTED, LOGGED OR NOT
================================================================================

Status:    filed 2026-08-18. Retitled 2026-08-18 at the maintainer's request
           -- the scope is BOTH AXES BEHAVING CORRECTLY in either mode, not
           only the log transform. The log half is a CORRECTNESS bug, not a
           nit; the linear half is that a typed limit must hold.

THE WHOLE OF IT, IN ONE LINE: an axis must describe the marks drawn against
it, and a limit the user typed must be the limit that is used -- whether the
axis is logarithmic or not. Everything below is a way that is currently
untrue.
Requested: 2026-08-18 --

  "on the interactive plot the axis limits dont quite work and the log x and
   log y just change the numbers not the possition of the dots. grid log x and
   y can be right click options not under the graph options."

Related: 147 is the menu categories this feeds. 129 is the pyqtgraph plots.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. THE LOG AXES ARE LYING, AND HERE IS WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FOUND 2026-08-18, and it is exact:

    FastPlot._apply_log  ->  self.plot.setLogMode(x, y)

`PlotItem.setLogMode` sets the AXES to log ticks and then walks its items
calling `setLogMode` on each one that has the method. Checked directly:

    pyqtgraph.PlotDataItem.setLogMode      exists
    pyqtgraph.ScatterPlotItem.setLogMode   DOES NOT EXIST

Every point on every one of these plots is a `ScatterPlotItem` -- the volcano,
the Q-Q, the control panel, the guide-agreement plot, the effect ranking. So
ticking "log y":

    the axis relabels itself to a log scale
    the dots do not move

THE PLOT THEN CLAIMS A LOG SCALE AND SHOWS LINEAR DATA. That is not a cosmetic
failure. A reader looking at a volcano with log-y on reads every p-value off a
ruler that does not describe the marks beside it, and there is nothing on
screen to tell them. A wrong figure that runs is worse than a control that
does nothing, and this is the first kind.

THE FIX: transform the DATA and label the axis, rather than asking pyqtgraph
to do it for an item type that cannot. Compute log10 of the coordinates when
building the scatter, keep the untransformed values for the hit-testing and
the tooltips -- a user hovering a point must still see its real p-value, not
its logarithm -- and set the axis ticks to the powers.

WHAT MUST NOT BREAK, because each already works: clicking a point returns its
row (the key round-trip), the selection ring lands on the dot the user sees,
and the exported figure matches the screen. All three read coordinates, so all
three have to read the same ones.

NON-POSITIVE VALUES HAVE NO LOGARITHM, AND THE ANSWER IS TO REFUSE.
Decided 2026-08-18: "refuse log on that axis".

A coefficient of zero or below is real and common on a volcano's x axis. The
control is DISABLED for an axis that holds any, with the reason on it, per
instruction 106 -- "log x: 41 of 1,213 points are at or below zero and have no
logarithm". Never dropped, never silently, and never a plot drawn from a
subset the user did not choose.

That is the conservative half of the same principle the whole instruction is
about: the plot may not show something the data does not say. Hiding 41 points
would make a volcano whose visible point count is a number nobody can account
for.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. THE AXIS LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`set_axis_limits` calls `setXRange` / `setYRange` and disables auto-range on
the pinned axis, which is right as far as it goes. Two things to check against
the report of "don't quite work":

  * WITH LOG MODE ON, pyqtgraph's ranges are in LOG UNITS. A user typing
    `1e-6, 1` gets a view from 1e-6 to 1 of the log values, which is not what
    they asked for. Once (A) is fixed the transform is ours, so the limits are
    typed in DATA units and converted -- and the dialog says which it wants.
  * A REDRAW MUST NOT SPRING THEM BACK. The docstring says auto-range is
    disabled for exactly this reason; verify it survives `set_results`, a
    recolour, and a level change, because those rebuild the items.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. GRID AND LOG BELONG ON THE RIGHT-CLICK MENU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"grid log x and y can be right click options not under the graph options."

They are `QCheckBox`es in a row under the plot today. They move into the
right-click menu as checkable entries, under the categories instruction 147
defines -- log under Axes, grid under Appearance -- and the strip under the
graph loses them.

THE STATE MUST STILL BE VISIBLE. A checkable menu entry shows its own tick, so
that is covered; what is not is that a plot with a log axis should say so
where the axis is, not only in a menu nobody has open. The axis label carries
it: "-log10(p)" is already that idea, and a log-x volcano says so on the x
label.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Ticking log y MOVES the points, checked by reading two dots' screen
  positions before and after.
* Hovering a point still reports its real value, not its logarithm.
* Clicking a point still selects its row, with log on.
* Non-positive values are counted and named, not silently dropped.
* Typed limits are in data units, and survive a recolour and a level change.
* Grid and log are checkable entries on the right-click menu; the strip under
  the graph no longer carries them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-08-18 -- SECTION A SHIPPED. THE TRANSFORM IS OURS NOW.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT SHIPPED

`FastPlot` no longer calls `PlotItem.setLogMode` at all. It transforms the
DATA and labels the axis itself:

  * `_install_axis_hooks` wraps `PlotItem.addItem` and `PlotItem.setLabel`, so
    every scatter, line, curve, bar and selection ring is caught on its way
    onto the plot with the DATA coordinates it was handed. Eighteen call sites
    draw on these plots; a transform written at each one is a transform that
    gets missed on the mark nobody tested.
  * `set_log_axes(x=, y=)` / `log_axes()` / `log_reason(axis)` are the API.
    `set_log_axes` RETURNS the state afterwards, because a request can be
    refused and a caller that assumed otherwise repeats the original mistake.
  * The drawn coordinates are always derived from the stored data, never from
    the previous drawn ones, so log-on-then-off is the identity.
  * The axis label gains " (log scale)" and loses it again.
  * Hover reports the real value: a custom `tip` inverts the transform.
  * `BinnedPlot._on_scene_clicked` converts back to data units before asking
    `bin_at`, which it did not before -- a click on a logged histogram landed
    in the wrong bar.

THE TRAP THAT COST AN HOUR, WRITTEN DOWN SO IT COSTS NOBODY ELSE ANY:
`PlotWidget.__init__` does `setattr(self, m, getattr(self.plotItem, m))` for a
list of methods that INCLUDES `addItem`. It is a bound-method SNAPSHOT taken
at construction, not a `__getattr__` forward. Wrapping only the PlotItem
therefore catches `self.plot.plot(...)` and misses every
`self.plot.addItem(...)` -- which is every scatter and every bar in this
module. Measured that way once: the Q-Q reported "1 of 2 values" on an axis
carrying a hundred points. Both copies are wrapped now.

THE REFUSALS, all with the reason on the control (instruction 106):
  * points at or below zero  -> "log x: 4 of 12 points are at or below zero
    and have no logarithm", the sentence this instruction asked for.
  * a line or bar reaching zero -> named as such, with the value.
  * bars measured from a zero baseline -> "the bars are measured from zero,
    which has no logarithm", which is more use than "50 of 100 values".
  * an axis carrying hand-placed category ticks (the control panel's x, the
    effect ranking's y) -> "this axis names its groups rather than measuring
    a quantity".
  * A REDRAW THAT ADMITS A NON-POSITIVE TAKES A LIVE LOG SCALE OFF and says
    so in the status line, rather than drawing log10 of it as NaN and losing
    the points silently.

Consequence worth knowing: neither shipped histogram can take log x. The
p-value one bins over (0, 1) so its first edge is 0; the effect one draws a
reference line at x=0. Both are correct refusals, not omissions.

TESTS: tests/test_the_axes_behave_as_expected.py, 18 of them. Thirteen fail on
the previous code, and the acceptance one fails with the bug's own signature --
"the dots did not move: [0.0, 0.0] px".

WHAT REMAINS: section B (the axis limits) and section C (grid and log onto the
right-click menu). Section C waits on instruction 147 C, which owns the menu
categories the two entries go under.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-08-18 -- SECTION B SHIPPED. THE LIMITS ARE IN DATA UNITS.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`axis_limits()` and `set_axis_limits()` both speak DATA units now, whatever
the scale, and the conversion is this class's own because the transform is.
The dialog's prompt says so on a logged axis -- "Y from in data units, not
log10:" -- and says nothing extra on a linear one, where there is nothing to
disambiguate.

The pin is REMEMBERED as well as applied (`_pinned`, in data units), which is
what makes a scale change keep the same data window rather than leaving the
view wherever the old units put it. Rounding is avoided by keeping what was
typed rather than re-deriving it from the drawn range.

Three ways out of a pin, all of them needed:
  * "Axis limits: back to automatic" forgets it, so a later scale change does
    not put the old window back.
  * a mouse drag or wheel forgets it -- `sigRangeChangedManually` -- because
    panning by hand is the user saying "not that window any more", and a plot
    that snapped back would be arguing with the person driving it.
  * a limit that cannot survive a scale change (a non-positive bound on a
    newly logged axis) is dropped rather than clamped.

A non-positive limit TYPED on a logged axis is refused with the reason in the
status line. Substituting a bound nobody typed is how a figure comes to show
a range nobody chose.

VERIFIED SURVIVING: `set_results` with a different family, `colour_by_column`,
a redraw with the scale on, and the scale being switched under a live pin.
Tests: the section B block of tests/test_the_axes_behave_as_expected.py, nine
of them. 27 in the file, all green.

WHAT REMAINS in 148: section C -- grid and log out of the QCheckBox strip and
onto the right-click menu -- which waits on instruction 147 C for the
categories they go under.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-08-18 -- SECTION C SHIPPED. 148 IS COMPLETE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"grid log x and y can be right click options not under the graph options."

The strip under the plot has lost all three. Log x and log y are checkable
entries under AXES; grid is a checkable entry under APPEARANCE -- the
categories instruction 147 C landed an hour earlier. The legend checkbox
STAYS in the strip, deliberately: it is the one a reader flicks on and off
while looking at the figure, where the other three are set once and then read
off the axis.

The state is on the plot, not on a widget: `grid_shown()` / `set_grid()`, and
`log_axes()` / `set_log_axes()` from section A. A menu entry is rebuilt on
every right-click and cannot be the thing that remembers.

A GATED LOG ENTRY CARRIES ITS REASON IN THE LABEL, the same as every other
control instruction 106 covers -- "Log x axis  —  log x: 4 of 12 points are
at or below zero and have no logarithm" -- and is disabled. It is readable
inside a submenu because `_group` sets `setToolTipsVisible` on every one.

A TRAP WORTH THE LINE: `menu.addAction(text, callable)` connects `triggered`,
whose bool is DROPPED for a slot that does not ask for one. A checkable entry
wired that way reports the state it had BEFORE the press, so "Log y axis"
ticked itself and turned nothing on. Checkable entries connect `toggled`,
which always carries the new state. `_gated` takes callback=None for them.

THE AXIS STILL SAYS SO: the label carries " (log scale)" from section A, so a
logged plot announces itself where the axis is and not only in a menu nobody
has open.

STILL FAST. The transform is a numpy pass rather than an axis relabel now, so
this was worth measuring: 0.37 ms per toggle on the real 1,215-point,
27-category volcano, against the 4.7 ms the module's header quotes for
pyqtgraph's own log toggle and 115 ms for matplotlib. tests/test_fast_plots.py
::test_it_is_fast drives `set_log_axes` and keeps the 25 ms budget.

TESTS: 32 in tests/test_the_axes_behave_as_expected.py, five of them new here
-- the entries exist and are checkable, they are under the right groups, the
strip no longer carries them, the tick shows the scale in force, and the grid
entry works.

INSTRUCTION 148 IS COMPLETE: A, B and C all shipped 2026-08-18.
