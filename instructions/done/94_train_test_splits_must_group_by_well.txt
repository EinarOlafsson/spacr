WRONG AS A BLANKET: `spacr/active_learning.py` ALREADY does grouped splitting,
and does it well. `_grouped_split` uses `StratifiedGroupKFold` with a
`GroupShuffleSplit` fallback; `_split_columns_for` REFUSES an unrecognised
strategy rather than quietly returning no columns -- because a lookup that
returns nothing turns a grouped split into a per-object random one AND records
it in the model card as "no group appears on both sides", which is the
optimistic number with provenance asserting the opposite. The setting is
`cv_group_by` and it already defaults to `'well'`.

SO THE WORK IS TO EXTEND THAT MECHANISM, NOT TO WRITE A SECOND ONE. A second
splitter is a second set of rules about what a well is, and the two would
disagree first on the plate whose names are unusual.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS, AND WHY WELL RATHER THAN FIELD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cells from one well share illumination, focus, seeding density, edge position,
fixation and batch. Split at random and a test cell's SIBLINGS are in
training, so the model can score well by recognising the WELL rather than the
phenotype. The reported accuracy is optimistic by an unknown amount -- unknown
because nothing in any output shows the leak -- and the feature importances
point at whatever varies between wells.

FIELD is the weaker grouping and the more comfortable one: it keeps more
effective sample size, because a well holds several fields. It also leaves the
leak in place, since two fields of one well share every confound above. WELL
is the level at which the experiment assigns a condition, so it is the level
at which a held-out set is independent. That is the maintainer's call and it
is the right one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. PROMOTE `active_learning._grouped_split` and `_split_columns_for` to a
     shared home both classical ML and the crop-list splitters can import.
     They are private today only because nothing outside that module needed
     them.
  2. Route the five sites through it. The measurement frames are indexed by
     `prcfo` and carry plateID/rowID/columnID, so the well is available; the
     crop lists carry it in the filename.
  3. REFUSE, DO NOT FALL BACK SILENTLY. A design with one well per class
     cannot be split this way, and a random split presented as a grouped one
     is the exact failure being fixed. active_learning's wording is the model:
     it says the accuracy "measures how well the model memorised this one
     well, not whether it transfers".
  4. SAY WHAT IT COST. Whole wells move, so `test_size` becomes approximate --
     20% of the WELLS, and whatever fraction of the cells those wells held.
     The run should report both numbers rather than let a user assume the
     second.
  5. RECORD THE SPLIT. `data_usage` already marks each row train/test/not_used
     in ml.py; it should also record the grouping level, or the column
     documents a leak rather than ruling one out.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

No well appears on both sides of any split in the package; a design that
cannot be grouped is refused with a sentence naming why; the reported test
fraction distinguishes wells from cells; and a model card says which grouping
produced the score it reports.

2026-08-13 (later) - A LADDER THE USER PICKS FROM, NOT A LEVEL IMPOSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Requested: 2026-08-13 - "instead cant we alow the user to choose split level
           with the options of cell, fieldID, and wellID?"

BETTER THAN HARD-CODING, and the vocabulary was already there: `_SPLIT_GROUPS`
in active_learning.py had well / field / plate / none, and `cv_group_by`
already defaulted to `'well'`. So this is exposing a choice that exists rather
than inventing one, and it is FOUR levels, not three -- `plate` comes free and
is stricter than well, for a design where the batch is the plate.

    cell    each object its own group. Siblings from one well on both sides.
    field   narrows the leak; two fields of one well still share illumination,
            seeding, edge position and fixation, so it does not close it.
    well    the level at which the experiment assigns a condition, and so the
            level at which a held-out set is independent.
    plate   for a design whose batch is the plate.

`none` RENAMED TO `cell`, WITH THE OLD NAME KEPT AS AN ALIAS. "None" reads as
the feature being switched OFF; it is in fact the leakiest setting on the
ladder, deliberately chosen. Naming it for its UNIT makes the four readable as
a ladder and stops a model card recording "no strategy" when what happened was
a strategy somebody picked. `none`, `off`, False and None all still resolve to
it, because that is what shipped and a settings file must keep meaning what it
meant.

THE REPORTED NAME FOLLOWS THE CHOICE. `_split_columns_for` returned `'none'`
for a `cell` split, so the card recorded the ABSENCE of a strategy rather than
the deliberate choice of the leakiest one -- and those two read very
differently to whoever reads the card next.

TESTS THAT PINNED THE OLD SHAPE, updated with their INTENT kept rather than
their assertion:

  * `test_filter_and_columns_are_one_section_not_two_tabs` asserted there were
    NO tab widgets at all. That was right when it was written and is wrong
    now: Filter and SEARCH are tabs, by instruction 31's spec. What must not
    happen is Filter and COLUMNS being split from each other, so that is what
    it asserts.
  * `test_the_gate_list_has_its_own_handle` pinned the splitter pane's CLASS
    and failed the moment the panel gained tabs, with nothing about the
    handles having changed. It checks the handle, which is what it is for.
  * `test_the_side_panel_does_not_paint_the_window_colour` checked the first
    scroll area found; there are several now, so it checks them all.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-08-14 - COMPLETE: ONE STRICT SPLITTER, FIVE CALLERS, FOUR LEVELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The split policy now has one shared home in `spacr.classifier_evaluation`:

    normalize_split_level   cell / field / well / plate; none -> cell
    split_columns_for       complete metadata keys or a refusal
    split_group_values      metadata, canonical prcfo, or crop filenames
    grouped_split           indices plus a durable SplitReport

THE DEFAULT IS WELL AND THE FALLBACK IS GONE. Missing metadata, anonymous crop
names, one-group designs, one independent well per class, and any design that
cannot put every class on both sides raise a sentence explaining why a random
cell split would measure memorisation rather than transfer. A deliberately
selected cell split remains available and is recorded as `cell`, never as the
absence of a strategy. Repeated rows/augmentations of the same object remain
together even at that level.

ALL FIVE SITES NOW USE IT:

  * `ml.ml_analysis` groups the ordinary holdout and its optional CV folds;
  * `io.generate_dataset_from_lists` groups the permanent disk dataset;
  * `utils.augment_classes` groups augmentation families globally;
  * `gui_elements.AnnotateApp.train_and_classify` groups the in-GUI model;
  * `surrogate.fit_surrogate` groups the fidelity holdout.

Active learning imports the same implementation instead of carrying its own,
and the ordinary Classify validation loader and hyperparameter folds now share
the same `cell` alias and strict identity refusal. There is no remaining direct
`train_test_split` call outside the shared implementation.

THE COST IS VISIBLE. `SplitReport` records requested fraction, realised group
fraction, realised cell fraction, group/cell counts and the exact rule. The
generated crop tree writes `.spacr_split.json`; classical ML writes the values
onto scored rows, into `model_metrics.csv`, and onto the returned estimator;
the surrogate result carries them; active-learning model cards carry the full
split-rule summary. Console summaries say, for example, "2 of 4 well groups
(50%) and 12 of 24 cells (50%); requested 25%" rather than pretending whole
wells can satisfy every requested decimal.

THE PICKER IS THE FOUR-RUNG LADDER. `cv_group_by` now exposes cell, field, well
and plate in that order, defaults to well, and its tooltip explains the leak at
each rung. Legacy `none`, `off`, False and None still deserialize as cell. The
legacy annotation launcher passes the same setting into its classifier.

TESTED NORMAL AND FAILURE CASES: direct metadata, prcfo with an underscored
plate id, crop filenames, repeated object rows, augmentation families,
deterministic cell splits, whole-well fraction expansion, missing/null keys,
anonymous filenames, one well, one well per class, rare cells, invalid
fractions and mismatched lengths. The routed ML, active-learning, CV, crop,
augmentation, surrogate and GUI suites pass (588 focused integration tests;
117 active-learning/surrogate tests; 433 settings tests; and the three
end-to-end classical-ML tests).
