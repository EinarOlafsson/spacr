--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE IDEA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A well containing a positive-control guide gives TWO independent measures of
the same quantity:

  * SEQUENCING says the guide is a fraction `f` of the well's reads;
  * IMAGING says a fraction `p` of the well's cells look like the positive
    control phenotype.

If the positive control is the only thing in that well producing that
phenotype, the two must agree. Where they systematically disagree, the
disagreement measures the bias in `f` -- which is exactly the quantity
instruction 207 D is arguing about without evidence.

THIS IS A CALIBRATION CURVE MADE OUT OF KNOWN TRUTH, and it is the only
proposal so far that can settle raw-versus-normalised with data rather than
reasoning.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEASURES -- READ THIS BEFORE BUILDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The slope of `p` on `f` is NOT the fraction bias alone. It is

    slope  =  penetrance  x  fraction-bias

and a single positive control cannot separate them. A guide that produces
the phenotype in 70% of the cells it enters gives a slope of 0.7 with
perfectly unbiased fractions, and reading that as "the fractions are 30%
too high" would be wrong.

THE COMPARISON SURVIVES ANYWAY, and this is what makes the method worth
building: penetrance is a property of the guide and does not change when the
fraction definition changes. So fitting the SAME wells twice -- once against
raw fractions, once against normalised -- gives two slopes whose RATIO is
free of penetrance. Which of the two is closer to internally consistent is
then an answer, even though neither slope is interpretable on its own.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SHARPEST FORM: SWEEP THE THRESHOLD, DO NOT REPLACE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The request offers "an alternative method to calculating the threshold, or
something before it". It is better as neither. `fraction_threshold` exists to
drop spurious barcodes -- a different job from fixing the SCALE of the
fractions, which is what this measures.

But it can CHOOSE the threshold, which is stronger than replacing it: for
each candidate `t`, recompute the fractions, refit `p` on `f`, and take the
`t` whose fit is most consistent. The default 0.02 becomes a measured number
for this screen instead of a constant.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CLASSIFIER CORRECTION IS A RESCALING, NOT AN ERROR BAR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"6% baked in" is right to include and wrong in shape. With sensitivity `se`
and specificity `sp`,

    p_observed = se * p_true + (1 - sp) * (1 - p_true)

so the correction is Rogan-Gladen:

    p_true = (p_observed - (1 - sp)) / (se + sp - 1)

At se = sp = 0.94 that is `(p_obs - 0.06) / 0.88`. It MOVES the estimate, it
does not widen it -- and at `p_obs = 0.06` it says the true fraction is
zero, the whole signal being false positives. It also inflates the variance
by `1 / (se + sp - 1)^2` = 1.29.

AND ACCURACY IS THE WRONG NUMBER. `se` and `sp` are two quantities and 94%
accuracy is one. On an imbalanced well -- which is every well, since a
positive control is a minority of the cells -- accuracy is dominated by the
majority class and the sensitivity can be far worse than the accuracy
suggests. THE CONFUSION MATRIX IS REQUIRED, not the accuracy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CO-HABITATION ASSUMPTION IS ONE-SIDED, WHICH HELPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"on average the positive controll does not co-habit wells with screen hits"
is mostly true and fails in one direction only: a hit sharing the well ADDS
positive-looking cells and never removes them. So the contamination is
one-sided, which is precisely the case where least squares is worst and a
QUANTILE fit is right -- the same argument instruction 212 makes for the
regression itself. Fit the median, not the mean.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND IT MUST NOT BE CIRCULAR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If the classifier was trained on cells from these same positive-control
wells, `p` is fitted to them and the agreement is guaranteed by
construction. The calibration wells must be held out from training, or the
method measures nothing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* The fit is reported per screen with its slope, its spread and the number
  of control wells it used -- and refuses, rather than reporting, below a
  stated minimum.
* Raw and normalised fractions are both fitted and both slopes shown; the
  comparison is presented as a ratio, not as two absolute calibrations.
* The classifier correction uses a confusion matrix, and the screen says so
  if only an accuracy is available.
* The fit is a median fit, and the one-sided reasoning is written where it
  is used.
* The calibration wells are held out from classifier training, checked
  rather than assumed.
* On simulated data with a known penetrance and a known fraction bias, the
  method recovers their PRODUCT and says that is what it recovered.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WAS SAID BACK -- AND THE CONFOUND IS SOLVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This file states above that a single positive control cannot separate
PENETRANCE from FRACTION BIAS, because the slope is their product. THAT
STANDS FOR A SINGLE CONTROL AND IS WRONG FOR THE DESIGN THE MAINTAINER
ACTUALLY HAS.

    "can the hold out be the mixed ratio wells, where we dont know the
     identity of each cell but we do know how many cells are PC and how many
     are NC from the sequencing. these were not use for training."

A RATIO SERIES SEPARATES THEM. A well that is a proportion `pi` of PC cells
has feature distribution `pi * F_PC + (1 - pi) * F_NC`, and `F_PC` estimated
from the pure wells ALREADY CONTAINS the non-penetrant cells -- a PC cell
showing no phenotype is still a PC cell and is still in that reference. So
the mixture fit recovers the true CELLULAR proportion with penetrance
absorbed, and comparing it to what sequencing reported isolates the bias.

DEMONSTRATED, NOT ARGUED. `mixed_ratio_calibration` recovers a planted bias
of 1.82 as 1.84, 1.81 and 1.76 at penetrances of 1.0, 0.6 and 0.3. The
penetrance does not move the slope.

AND IT FOUND A CIRCULARITY IN THE FIRST IMPLEMENTATION: the pure wells were
identified BY THEIR REPORTED FRACTION, which is the quantity under test. A
bias large enough to matter pushes a 100%-PC well below the cut-off, so the
fit refused to run on exactly the screens that needed it. The wells are now
named from the plate design and the function warns when they are not.

WHAT IT STILL DOES NOT SHOW: PC-versus-NC is a two-class problem with the
largest phenotype difference in the screen. A method can be perfect on it
and fail at six guides in one well, which is the real task. This validates
calibration and discrimination; the simulation remains the only check of
multi-guide assignment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CONFUSION MATRIX -- ASKED AND ANSWERED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "fou have the computer vision scores, cant this be calculated from them?
     cant you bake the calculation of this into the modula?"

NOT FROM SCORES ALONE. A confusion matrix counts agreements between a call
and a truth; a score column with no labels beside it has no truth to agree
with. What looks like a boundary in a score histogram is a property of the
histogram.

`spacr/classifier_quality.py` implements the three routes there are, in
descending order of trust:

  1. the LABELLED TEST SPLIT -- exact, and the answer whenever those rows
     exist. "i think we do have tables with train and test annotation" --
     they are precisely what is needed;
  2. OUT-OF-FOLD predictions from a re-fit -- exact AND better, because
     every cell gets an honest score instead of only the test cells, which
     gives the prevalence stratification the whole screen to work with;
  3. MIXTURE DECONVOLUTION -- the fallback when nothing is labelled, and
     weak. On a realistic fixture it estimated a prevalence of 0.45 where
     the truth was 0.20. It reports a `separation` health check and marks
     itself untrustworthy below 2 pooled standard deviations, which it did
     on that fixture. The number is not the problem; returning it without
     the warning would be.

"columns one and 2 of each plate were the training wells" is implemented as
`training_wells`, so the held-out set is identifiable from the well name
alone. THIS IS WHAT MAKES THE CALIBRATION NON-CIRCULAR: a classifier scored
on the wells it was fitted to agrees with itself.

RETRAINING IS WORTH IT BUT NOT REQUIRED. Excluding columns 1 and 2 is enough
to validate. Cross-validating is strictly better and is the recommendation,
for the reason in route 2.

`sensitivity_by_prevalence` is the specific number this file asked for, and
it does more than supply a constant: sensitivity should NOT depend on
prevalence, so if it rises with it the classifier is reading context rather
than the cell -- and every fraction it produces is then partly a copy of the
fraction it was given. There is a test that plants exactly that cheat and
catches it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    plate      se        sp     se+sp-1    threshold
      1      0.9640    0.9846    0.9486      0.4699   (computed per-cell)
      2      0.9553    0.9817    0.9370      0.2955

      4      0.9584    0.9700    0.9284      0.8567

BETTER THAN THE 94% THIS FILE ASSUMED, and the specificity is the half that
matters most. Variance inflation is 1.13, not 1.29.

THE CORRECTION IS NEGLIGIBLE FOR A COMMON GUIDE AND DOMINANT FOR A RARE ONE,
which is not visible in the accuracy and is the finding that decides whether
any of this was worth building. At se = 0.9604, sp = 0.9812:

    true 30%  ->  observed 30.1%   -- a no-op
    true 10%  ->  observed 11.3%   -- 1.13x
    true  5%  ->  observed  6.6%   -- 1.32x
    true  2%  ->  observed  3.8%   -- 1.88x
    true  1%  ->  observed  2.8%   -- 2.82x

The false positives are a share of the NEGATIVES, and when a guide is rare
almost every cell in the well is a negative -- so a small false-positive
rate on a large population swamps a large true-positive rate on a small one.
SCREEN HITS ARE RARE, so the correction matters exactly where the science
is.

TWO LIMITS OF THIS TEST SPLIT, both material:

  * PREVALENCE STRATIFICATION CANNOT BE DONE FROM IT. Every test well is
    purely pc or purely nc -- per-well prevalence is 0 or 1 -- and the split
    itself is balanced 584/584. There is only one prevalence in the file, so
    the check that would catch a classifier reading context has nothing to
    vary. It needs out-of-fold predictions on mixed wells, which is the
    argument for the re-fit the maintainer offered.
  * THE THRESHOLDS ARE NOT COMPARABLE ACROSS PLATES. 0.2955 on plate 2
    against 0.8567 on plate 4 is nearly threefold. Measured on plate 1's
    ROC, moving between those two costs three points of sensitivity and
    gains one and a half of specificity. THE CORRECTION IS PER-PLATE OR IT
    IS WRONG.

Confirmed too: every test well sits in column 1 or 2, exactly as stated, so
`training_wells` identifies the held-out set correctly on this screen.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The three settings this needed now exist -- `positive_control_wells`,

come from the plate design rather than from the numbers under test.

Plate 1, contaminant excluded, PC share taken as the sequenced fraction of
`TGGT1_239740_1` against `TGGT1_233460_4`, imaging taken as the mixture

    column 1 (pure NC)      sequenced PC share  0.001
    column 2 (pure PC)                          0.980
    column 3 (the mix)                          0.578
    48 wells

    slope                      0.989
    intercept                  0.023
    median absolute residual   0.023

IMAGING AND SEQUENCING AGREE TO WITHIN ABOUT ONE PER CENT. Three things
follow, and the third is the one to be careful about:

  * the mixture estimator works on real cells, not only on the simulation it
    was built against;
  * the classifier separates PC from NC well enough to measure a proportion,
    which is a stronger statement than its se/sp on a balanced test split;
  * IT DOES NOT MEASURE THE SCREEN-WELL FRACTION BIAS. The quantity fitted
    here is the PC share OF THE TWO CONTROLS -- a renormalised proportion
    inside a two-guide subspace. A screen well has 440 guides competing and
    a threshold that discards most of them, and nothing in these 48 wells
    exercises that. So this validates the METHOD and the CLASSIFIER; the
    normalisation question instruction 207 raises is still answered by the
    control-column background measurement, not by this.

Column 3 is 58% PC by sequencing rather than the 50% a nominal mix would
give, which is itself worth knowing: the design intent and the delivered
composition differ by eight points.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SWEEP: fraction_threshold BECOMES A MEASUREMENT OF THIS SCREEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This file's own sharpest form -- "sweep the threshold, do not replace it" --
is `spacr/fraction_calibration.py`. For each candidate it recomputes the
per-well fractions, refits imaging on sequencing over the control wells
through `mixed_ratio_calibration`, and returns every candidate's fit with the
one it chose and why. 0.02 stops being a constant in a library.

WHAT "MOST CONSISTENT" MEANS, and it is not the residual. The choice is made
on the median absolute DISAGREEMENT between the two measurements, well by
well. Scatter around the FITTED line cannot see a systematic deflation at
all: fractions that are uniformly three per cent low sit exactly on a line of
slope 1.03, and that fit looks perfect. The mixture estimator absorbs
penetrance -- a PC cell showing no phenotype is still in the pure-PC
reference -- so what is left to explain a disagreement is the fraction
itself. The slope, the intercept and the fitted residual are all reported at
every candidate; the choice is made on how far apart the two answers are.

TIES GO TO THE SMALLEST THRESHOLD. Once the spurious barcodes are gone a
higher cut changes nothing except how much real data it destroys, and
`guides_per_well` is reported at every candidate so what a cut costs is
visible.

IT REFUSES RATHER THAN REPORTING below `MINIMUM_CALIBRATION_WELLS` (8): a
median over a handful of wells is decided by which wells happened to be
usable, and the sweep exists to compare fits.

THE CLASSIFIER CORRECTION IS A CONFUSION MATRIX OR NOTHING. `sensitivity` and
`specificity` must both be given or neither; one alone is an accuracy wearing
a confusion matrix's name and is refused, as is a pair summing to no more
than one, which would invert the estimate. Given both, each well's imaging
proportion is Rogan-Gladen corrected before the disagreement is taken, the
slope is rescaled by the same denominator, and the variance inflation is
reported beside it.

THE PURE WELLS ARE NAMED FROM THE PLATE DESIGN, never from the reported
fraction, for the reason this file already gives; and how many of the fitted
wells also trained the classifier is COUNTED and reported, because a
classifier scored on the wells it was fitted to agrees with itself.

WHAT IT DOES NOT MEASURE, restated because the sweep does not escape it: a
control column has two guides competing for its reads and a screen well has
hundreds. The threshold this chooses is the one that makes the CONTROL
columns consistent.

Tests: `tests/test_the_threshold_is_measured_per_screen.py`, 15 passing. On a
control plate with a planted contaminant the sweep recovers the threshold
that removes it, and the run keeping every read is shown fitting a perfect
line while both measurements disagree.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SWITCH IS WIRED, AND IT HAD NEVER ONCE REACHED THE SWEEP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`calibrate_fraction_threshold` is declared, typed, tooltipped, on the panel
and read by `ml._perform_regression`, which is why this looked done. Driven
end to end on a synthetic control plate it returned NOTHING, every time, for
two independent reasons -- and both were invisible because the tests either
side of the seam agreed with themselves rather than with each other.

ONE: THE ANSWER WAS READ OFF THE WRONG KEY. `_calibrated_fraction_threshold`
took `result.get("threshold")`. `sweep_fraction_threshold` returns the
answer as `chosen`; `threshold` is a per-candidate key, one on each row of
`candidates`. So the reader was asking the top level for a name that only
exists a level down, got None every time, and printed "found no cut-off it
preferred" over a sweep that had chosen 0.02. The two tests covering it
supplied `{"threshold": ...}` themselves, so the stand-in and the reader
agreed about a shape the real producer has never had.

TWO: THE WELLS WERE TAKEN FROM THE GUIDE SETTING. `_calibration_inputs`
matched well labels against `positive_control` and `negative_control`. In a
REGRESSION those are gene or gRNA ID substrings -- `positive_control` defaults
to '239740' -- and no well label has ever contained one, so the sweep was
refused with "no well matched" before it read a number. The three control
BLOCK settings this file added for exactly this purpose,
`positive_control_wells` and `negative_control_wells`, were never read. They
are now, and the guide setting is used for the guide, which is what
`positive_guide` needs. A token is matched against a PART of the `prc` label
rather than as a substring of it: 'c2' in 'plate1_r1_c20' is true and says
nothing, and folding column 20 into column 2's reference would shift the
endpoint the whole calibration is anchored on.

There is now a test that drives `_calibrated_fraction_threshold` onto the
REAL sweep over a planted control plate and gets 0.02 back -- the claim
neither stand-in could make.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND THE TWO ANSWERS ARE REPORTED SIDE BY SIDE, AND NAMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`graph_sequencing_stats` still picks its threshold from `target_unique_count`
alone, and that is right: it answers "how many guides per well do I want" from
the counts, and the calibration answers "which cut-off makes imaging and
sequencing agree" from the control wells. Neither replaces the other.

Both are on screen. When a threshold is in force the run draws the sweep
anyway and `_draw_the_threshold_sweep` prints the sweep's own pick beside the
number in force. What was wrong was the attribution: it said "you set 0.0168"
about a number the calibration had measured, which tells the user something
untrue about where their threshold came from -- the one thing that line
exists to say. It now names the source, and says in the same sentence that
the two answer different questions.
