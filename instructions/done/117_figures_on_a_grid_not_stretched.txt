================================================================================
FIGURES ON A GRID, AT THEIR OWN ASPECT, NOT STRETCHED
================================================================================

Status:    not started
Requested: 2026-08-16 — "it would also be great if we could overhaul the
           figure showing mechanism to show the figures on a grid and not
           stretch them . so e.g. teach graph gettts on square box and the
           heatmaps get e.g. 2 or 1 per plate. then the user sees everything
           on their screen or in a field that they can scroll"
Owner:     figures panel

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE STATE IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FigureQueue shows ONE figure at a time, fitted into whatever shape the panel
happens to be. A run produces seventeen figures; seeing the fourth means
clicking to it, and every one is stretched to a container that has nothing to
do with its own proportions. A square volcano and a wide plate heatmap get
the same box.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A plate heatmap distorted to a square is not a heatmap of a plate any more --
the wells stop being square and positional artefacts, the whole reason to
look at it, become impossible to see. And a run's figures are meant to be read
TOGETHER: the fraction histogram explains the volcano, and one-at-a-time
navigation hides that relationship.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A scrollable grid. Each figure keeps its own aspect ratio inside its cell:
square plots get a square cell, a plate heatmap gets a wide one and takes one
or two per row. Cell size follows the panel width, so widening the window
shows more per row rather than stretching what is already there. Clicking a
cell opens that figure full size; the one-at-a-time view stays as that
detail view.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT IS DONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A run's seventeen figures are visible by scrolling one panel, a plate heatmap
is still the shape of a plate, and no figure is drawn at an aspect ratio it
was not created with.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-08-16 - WHAT "GRID" MEANS: TILES THAT CAN BE PRESSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "when i say figure grid im thinking of tiles that can be pressed."

So this is not a static montage and not a scroll of stacked images. Each cell
is a TILE and a tile is a control: it has a hover state, it takes a click,
and clicking it fills the container (the click-to-fill half of this file).

That settles two things that would otherwise be guessed:

  * a tile is the unit. Anything else that belongs in the grid -- the gene
    information panel of instruction 121, a table, a note -- is A TILE TOO,
    not a special case beside the grid. One grid, one interaction, one set of
    tests.
  * the aspect rule in this file applies to the FIGURE INSIDE the tile, not
    to the tile. Tiles are a regular grid; the plot inside one keeps its own
    aspect and is letterboxed rather than stretched.

================================================================================
2026-08-17 - CLOSED
================================================================================

Both halves ship and both are tested:

  * the grid is scrollable, sits above the console, and reflows to the
    container -- `spacr/qt/widgets/figure_grid_view.py`;
  * a click fills the container, the Image UMAP behaviour that was asked to
    be copied -- `figure_activated` -> `FigureQueue.show_index`;
  * a plate heatmap is still the shape of a plate. `cell_span` accepts an
    aspect and deliberately ignores it, with the reason written down: a grid
    whose cells are different sizes is not a grid, so the FIGURE is
    letterboxed inside a regular cell rather than the cell being stretched.
