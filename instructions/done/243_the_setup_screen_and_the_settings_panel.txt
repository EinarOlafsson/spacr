THE SETUP SCREEN, A BUTTON EXPLAINS ITSELF AT THE FOOT OF THE WINDOW

"for button tooltips there should allways be a spece at the bottom of the
window that gets populated with the tooltip for the button not on the
button itself. like it works for the modules on the main screen."

  * `spacr/qt/widgets/hint_bar.py` is the Home screen's mechanism made
    reusable: a `HintBar` at the foot, `explain(widget, text)` to register
    a control, and an event filter that writes the sentence while the
    pointer is on it and restores the resting line when it leaves;
  * the button carries NO tooltip. The sentence would otherwise appear
    over the button -- where the pointer already is, and where the user is
    about to click -- covering the thing it describes;
  * the sentence goes onto the widget's accessible description, so
    removing the tooltip costs no assistive text;
  * a control with nothing to say is not watched, or crossing it would
    blank the sentence belonging to the control beside it;
  * a window with NO bar keeps the tooltip: explaining itself awkwardly
    beats explaining itself nowhere;
  * Preferences has one, and its five cleanup buttons report to it.

STILL OPEN

1. THE MONTAGE CAP -- MEASURED, THEN SET AT 2,000

   "set the cap to 10000 images." The cost of 10,000 was measured before it
   was taken, and shown to the maintainer, who chose 2,000 instead: most of
   the reach, none of the cliff. The number is now 2,000 in
   `cell_montage.MAX_OBJECTS` and in the panel's own default, and
   `montage_cap_cost()` says what any cap costs as it is typed.

   THE MEASUREMENT IS DONE and it is recorded in the code, in
   `picture_settings.MEASURED_PAGE_SIZES`, `MEASURED_CROP_BYTES` and
   `MEASURED_MS_PER_CROP`, with `montage_cap_cost()` turning them into the
   sentence the cap control now carries. What 10,000 costs, against 300:

   ===============  ==================  ==================
   what             cap 300             cap 10,000
   ===============  ==================  ==================
   pages            5 - 13              167 - 417
   crops held       43 MB               1,436 MB
   twelve well tabs 0.5 GB              16.8 GB
   time to cut      0.1 - 3.4 s         4.9 - 114 s
   ===============  ==================  ==================

   Pages are measured with the tab's own geometry -- six columns, 96 px
   thumbnails -- across the viewports the Cells tab is used at: 24 crops a
   page in the narrowest panel the splitter allows, 36 in an ordinary one,
   60 on a full screen. Memory is measured resident, not estimated: a well
   tab holds EVERY crop it was given, because turning a page must not
   re-cut, and 10,000 crops of 224x224x3 is 1,436 MB of them. Cutting time
   is the recorded per-crop cost by route -- the low figure reads exported
   PNGs, the high one cuts from merged arrays spread over many fields.

   TWO CONSEQUENCES THE NUMBER DOES NOT CARRY ON ITS OWN, and both are
   decisions rather than measurements:

     * `MAX_WELL_TABS` is 12 and a tab is only ever closed by hand. Twelve
       tabs at the raised cap is 16.8 GB, which is more than the machines
       spaCR runs on have. The cap and the tab bound multiply, so raising
       one is a decision about the other;
     * the pager is a previous and a next button. At 300 the far end is 13
       clicks away; at 10,000 it is 417. A cap that large wants a page to
       jump to, or the last page is unreachable in practice.

   WHAT IS LEFT IS THE NUMBER ITSELF. It lives in `cell_montage.MAX_OBJECTS`
   and in the Cells-tab toolbar's own spin box, whose range stops at 5,000 --
   so a settings window offering 10,000 would be silently clamped to 5,000 on
   the way to the montage, which is worse than a cap that has not moved.
   Those two, and `picture_settings.OWN_DEFAULTS['cap']`, have to move
   together or not at all.

THE TERMS ARE LONG ENOUGH TO BE TERMS, AND THE ACCEPTANCE IS GATED ON
HAVING READ THEM.

"for the spacr terms there should be much more text, so the user has to
scroll down. when they scroll down to the bottom the tick box stopps
being grayed out and they can check it (the text should also be grayed
out)."

  * THE DOCUMENT IS NINE NUMBERED SECTIONS in `terms.TERMS`, and the
    length is load-bearing rather than padding: the gate below cannot
    mean anything unless reaching the end is an act. It covers the
    licence, what may be done with spaCR, what is not promised, what
    leaves the machine, how what is sent may be used, what is never made
    public, how to refuse each route, and what a rewritten version does;
  * `TERMS_VERSION` is 2.0. A profile that accepted 1.0 accepted a
    document with no data-use clause in it, so the existing machinery
    asks it again;
  * THE ACCEPTANCE IS GATED ON HAVING REACHED THE END. The control is
    disabled, and drawn disabled, until the terms have been scrolled to
    the bottom -- so the enabling IS the evidence that the text went past
    the reader's eyes. A viewport tall enough to show the whole text at
    once counts as read; the gate is "the end is on screen", not "a
    scrollbar moved", or a large monitor becomes a trap;
  * THE TEXT IS GREYED TOO while the gate is closed, not only the tick.
    One state, drawn on both halves, so the page reads as one thing
    waiting rather than as a tick that happens to be dead;
  * THE GREYED SWITCH SAYS WHY, from the moment the slide opens rather
    than only after a press: `terms.SCROLL_HINT` sits above it, and the
    refusal on Next carries it too, because "tick the box above" is not
    actionable advice about a box that will not take a tick;
  * A PAGE THAT IS NOT ON SCREEN HAS NOT BEEN READ. An unshown scroll
    area still answers questions about its range off geometry nothing
    laid out, and that answer is "no scrolling needed" -- the same one a
    fully-read document gives. It is not believed: `terms_were_read`
    measures only while the page is visible, and the gate is drawn shut
    until it can be measured;
  * READING IS LATCHED. Scrolling back up does not un-read what was
    read, or the gate closes behind the reader who has already earned it.

WHAT THE TERMS MUST SAY, in the maintainer's own words:

  * "the developers can use the users data to develope the software
    further";
  * "any image data or models generated and uploaded to shared resourses
    by the user may be used by the developers to generate comunity
    resourses such as object detection models";
  * "the users logs and metadata may be used for making the software
    better, and other uses."

AND THE TERMS MUST MATCH WHAT THE SOFTWARE ACTUALLY DOES. The maintainer's
reading of the report control -- "so if the user dosnt unclick include
logs in report, i can use those logs for whatever i want" -- is what the
terms now have to state plainly, because a permission nobody was told
about is not a permission. Note the change that went with it: a bug
report no longer PUBLISHES the log. The log is saved to the reporter's
own machine and the public issue names the path, because "the github
issue can be public but the users loggs can never be publicly facing,
users whould not like that". So the terms grant the developers use of
logs that are SENT; they do not put them on the tracker.

  * sections 5 and 6 of the document are that pair, written out: what the
    developers may do with what is sent, and then what is never made
    public, so the permission cannot be read as a permission to publish.
    Section 7 is the other half of an honest permission -- every route
    out of the machine, and the switch that closes it;
  * two claims were checked against the code rather than asserted: the
    version check is the only request spaCR makes that is not about the
    user's data, and the report preview shows and can edit the whole
    report but does not re-offer the log setting, so the document says
    what it does instead of what would have read better.

THE ANIMATION QUESTION, UNDER THEME

"in the startup, under theme should be annimation, degault to blobs."

  * a question of its own on the same slide as the theme, immediately
    under it, offering `ambient.ANIMATION_CHOICES` including None;
  * it defaults to Blobs, which is already `ambient.DEFAULT_THEME` and
    what `preferences.get_ambient_animation` falls back to -- so the
    slide shows the default the application already has rather than a
    second opinion about it;
  * it writes through `preferences.set_ambient_animation`, which is the
    one seam that both turns the backdrop on and stores the choice, and
    it writes as the choice is made -- like the theme above it, because
    the only way to know a look took is to see it;
  * it is asked by the SLIDE, not added to `setup_screen.questions()`:
    that screen's apply pass writes each answer through its own setter,
    and a second writer for the backdrop is how a stored None ends up
    drawing. With no ambient module to ask, the row is absent and the
    theme slide is the two questions it was.

THE TERMS OF USE
  * a slide of its own, second to last: after the questions, before the
    screen says it is finished. It carries the terms, the licence's name and
    a link to the whole of it, and the required copyright notice;
  * THE TERMS THEMSELVES ARE NOT TRANSLATED and everything around them is. A
    translated licence summary is not the licence; the title, the sentence
    introducing it, the acceptance and the refusal are the screen talking,
    and they are catalogued in all nine languages through the same
    registration seam an app's name uses;
  * the acceptance is a slider, like every other boolean on that screen;
  * THE NEXT BUTTON IS NOT GREYED. Pressed without the acceptance it stays
    on the slide and says why -- a control that does nothing and says
    nothing leaves the reader to guess which thing on the page is stopping
    them;
  * the acceptance is RECORDED, beside the other setup answers and under the
    same `onboarding/` prefix, with the version of the terms and the time it
    was given. `terms.agreement_record()` is the whole record in one call;
  * A CHANGED VERSION IS A NEW AGREEMENT. `TERMS_VERSION` is compared against
    what was recorded, so rewritten terms ask again instead of inheriting the
    old answer;
  * dismissing the screen records NOTHING -- a dismissal chooses the
    defaults, it does not accept a licence -- and the screen comes back next
    launch. A store that refuses the write is treated as not having agreed,
    which is the safe direction. A launch that declined setup (a server, a
    headless job) is still never asked.

THE SETTINGS WINDOW IN TABS
  * the picture-settings window is five tabs -- Source, Channels, Picture,
    Outline, Which cells -- off one table, `picture_settings.categories()`,
    the same shape the module screens group their settings with;
  * the grouping is BY THE QUESTION EACH SETTING ANSWERS, not by which mode
    uses it: a tab that appeared and vanished with the crop source would hide
    the controls whose greyed reason explains the mode;
  * every offered setting is on exactly one tab, and a setting added later
    with no tab named for it lands on a trailing one rather than vanishing;
  * a tab holding controls this mode cannot use says how many, and stays
    selectable -- behind a tab, a greyed control's reason has to be findable.

PERCENTILES AS TWO FIELDS
  * one label, two numeric fields, low and high. The stored value is still
    the two-element list every settings file holds;
  * the fields constrain each other rather than validating afterwards: the
    high end cannot go below the low one and the low end cannot go above the
    high one, so an inverted window is not a value the panel can be left
    holding;
  * every spelling already on disk is MIGRATED, not refused: `[2, 98]`,
    `(2, 98)`, `2,98`, `2;98`, and the `[1 99]` a user types when they use a
    space. A pair given high-first is put in order; a percentile outside
    0-100 is brought back, because numpy raises on one and the raise
    surfaces as a lost montage with no mention of a setting;
  * THE SHIPPED DEFAULT USED TO BE THROWN AWAY. `percentiles` was read by the
    channel-list parser, which maps the position strings '0', '1' and '2'
    onto the colours r, g and b -- right for `normalize_channels` and
    destructive here. `[2, 98]` became `['b', '98']`, the float conversion
    raised, and the caller fell back to a pair nobody asked for. Every
    window whose low end was 0, 1 or 2 was silently replaced;
  * the same migration at the crop layer: a `normalize` written as text
    reached `CropSpec` as a truthy string that is not a sequence, so the cut
    fell through to a full 0-100 stretch -- the window was configured, the
    crop ignored it, and nothing said so.

THE SETUP SCREEN
  * one rounded translucent surface, with nothing square behind it;
  * Hello moved up a row and fades out over a band the next page leaves
    empty;
  * three spaCR modes, described by what they hold;
  * clicking a provider starts its sign-in, and offers the install page
    when the CLI is absent; brand fill means ready, hover gives the brand
    tint, muted ink means not installed;
  * the GitHub sign-in opens the default browser at the device page and
    shows the one-time code; the LOGO is the button, and it takes the
    theme's own ink when a token is reachable -- white on dark;
  * the language applies as it is chosen and redraws the screen in it;
  * the first slide says segmentation and object classification need an
    NVIDIA GPU, and names the card in green or red. A card that is present
    but unreachable is sent to `spacr-doctor`, because that is a CUDA
    problem and not a hardware one. First slide only;
  * the last slide says "Done" and, under it, "Welcome to spaCR".

THE WINDOWS
  * frameless, with minimise / full screen / close top right, and the MARK
    changes colour rather than a plate behind it: red x, blue square, blue
    minus. The x spans the same box as the square;
  * every popup is cut to the card's shape with a rounded mask, so no
    compositor can leave a square corner;
  * the resting rim is the palette's dark grey and every card lights the
    same fraction of it, measured against the first-run window;
  * the edges resize, handed to the window manager, because dropping the
    frame dropped the grips;
  * the menu bar lights its words in the accent on hover and press, and is
    re-laid on a window-state change so a menu opens under the word that
    was pressed.

THE SETTINGS PANEL
  * every Preferences row carries its tooltip ON THE LABEL, never on the
    field, filled from one table and moved there by one pass over the
    finished dialog -- so a row added later is covered without the author
    having to remember the rule.

THE CELLS TAB AND THE FIGURES
  * three crop sources: load images, stream (array), stream (database).
    The array route reads an object's label out of a mask plane and can
    follow the outline; the database route reads its row in the
    measurements table and can only cut the recorded box, and the panel
    says so rather than offering a shape it cannot cut;
  * the object types cover the four organelle roles, which had no
    coordinate column and so could not be streamed at all;
  * three fields map each colour to an array plane, so planes 1, 2 and 4
    of a five-plane array are a mapping rather than a slice, and a blank
    field means that colour is not drawn;
  * crops fill their row instead of stopping at a hard-coded 96 px;
  * the save dialog's preview is drawn on the page the FILE gets, and the
    dialog offers aspect ratio, line width, an absolute text size and both
    axis titles, all defaulting to "as drawn".

THE REGRESSION
  * the intercept is a choice in four states: fitted, zero (through the
    origin), control (the response centred on the negative controls, so
    the intercept IS the control level), and value (pinned at a number the
    user gives, by shifting the response AND suppressing the term).
    OFFERED IN THE FORM: `intercept` is a dropdown of the four under
    "Regression: Model", `intercept_value` is the number beside it, and
    the number is greyed for the three modes that do not read it, with
    the reason naming the mode actually in force. A settings file written
    before the keys existed still runs, and fits as it did;
  * rank refuses a continuous column as a grouping, by name, and names the
    thin groups once instead of one line per pair.
