================================================================================
THE SETUP SCREEN IS A SEQUENCE OF SLIDES, NOT A FORM
================================================================================

Status:    filed 2026-08-21, not started.
Requested: 2026-08-21 -- "for the spacr start up. i want one question per
           slide with a little explantaion. the blue rim should flow like
           water towards the mouse and every time i press nezt it should
           make one round clockwise and preveous goes counter clockwise. ...
           then a screen that just says Done / the background should be
           stratified with random moovement at 150% speed. / it should feel
           like a smooth transition into spacr".
Owner:     Qt GUI (setup screen)

This SUPERSEDES the grouped-form layout instruction 221 shipped. 221's model
(`spacr/qt/setup_screen.py`) and its persistence are unchanged and stay --
what changes is entirely how the questions are presented.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE QUESTION PER SLIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A form asks everything at once and answers nothing; a slide asks one thing
and has room to say why it matters. EACH SLIDE CARRIES A LITTLE EXPLANATION
-- not the setting's full tooltip, which is reference text, but the sentence
a person needs to choose.

THE ORDER IS THE MAINTAINER'S, and it is not arbitrary: it runs from what
changes the screen the user is looking at, through how spaCR runs, to what
leaves the machine.

  1. LANGUAGE
     And HELLO IN THE CHOSEN LANGUAGE underneath it. That is the request and
     it is also the demonstration -- the only way to know a language choice
     took is to see it, and the greeting is the smallest thing that shows it
     without redrawing the screen.

  2. THEME and COLOUR-BLIND MODE
     Together because they are one question: what this looks like. Both
     should apply as they are chosen, for the same reason the greeting does.

  3. spaCR MODE and the REPRODUCIBILITY HASH
     How it runs.

  4. THE ASSISTANT -- provider, and on at launch
     THE PROVIDER IS A LOGO BUTTON, one each for Claude, GPT and Gemini.
     A dropdown of three names is a dropdown; three logos is a choice
     somebody makes in one glance. A provider whose CLI is not installed
     must say so rather than being offered as though it were ready.

  5. USER FEEDBACK -- file GitHub issues, include logs in the report,
     GitHub login
     Grouped because they are one decision with three parts: what leaves
     this machine, and under whose name.

  6. DONE
     A screen that says only that. It is the transition, not a summary --
     a list of what was chosen here would be a form again, at the end.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EVERY BOOLEAN IS A SLIDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "aslo in the startup all the booleans should be sliders"

Not a checkbox anywhere on this screen. A tick box is a form control and
this is not a form -- and a slider READS AS A STATE rather than as a task,
which is what these settings are: the reproducibility hash, the assistant at
launch, issue filing and log inclusion are all things that are on or off,
not things to be done.

spaCR already has `spacr.qt.widgets.toggle.Toggle`, which is what the rest
of the application uses for exactly this. Use it rather than a second
slider, so the gesture and the look are the ones the user meets everywhere
else.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "the blue rim should flow like water towards the mouse and every time i
     press nezt it should make one round clockwise and preveous goes counter
     clockwise"

TWO BEHAVIOURS ON ONE ELEMENT, and they have to compose rather than fight:

  * AT REST it FLOWS TOWARDS THE POINTER. 221's card already picks the
    nearest corner and this is the continuous version of it -- the accent
    eases along the rim to where the mouse is, rather than jumping between
    four positions.
  * ON NEXT it makes ONE FULL CIRCUIT CLOCKWISE; on PREVIOUS, one
    ANTICLOCKWISE. The direction is what tells the user which way they went,
    which is worth more than the animation.

WHILE A CIRCUIT IS RUNNING THE POINTER DOES NOT STEER IT. A lap that gets
dragged off course by a mouse movement is not a lap, and the user cannot
tell whether it went round. The follow resumes when the circuit finishes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BACKGROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "stratified with random moovement at 150% speed"

Stratified layers, each drifting on its own, at 1.5x the ambient theme's
rate. It is the existing blob/ambient background driven faster and split
into strata -- not a second background system, which would be a second thing
to keep in step with the theme.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND IT MUST STILL BE DISMISSIBLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

221's rules survive intact and are not up for renegotiation by the redesign:

  * every question has a working default, so the screen can be closed at any
    slide without answering;
  * closing it MARKS IT ANSWERED, because a screen that reopens until it is
    filled in cannot be dismissed;
  * INVARIANTS 10 -- if the blur, the rim animation or the strata cannot be
    drawn, the slides still work and still write the same answers. Every one
    of these is decoration, and decoration must never be load-bearing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Six slides in the maintainer's order, one question each, each with its own
  sentence of explanation.
* The greeting under the language slide is in the chosen language and
  changes when the choice does.
* Theme and colour-blind mode take effect on the slide that sets them.
* The provider is chosen by logo, and an uninstalled provider says so.
* Next runs one clockwise circuit and Previous one anticlockwise, asserted
  on the DIRECTION rather than on a frame count.
* A pointer move during a circuit does not steer it.
* Every boolean on the screen is a Toggle; a test asserts no QCheckBox
  survives anywhere in the slides.
* Closing at any slide writes the defaults and does not reopen.
* With the animation disabled the same answers are still written.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND IT IS IN THE HELP MENU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    2026-08-21: "the startup should be in the help menue"

It ran once on the first launch and then never. That is right for the
AUTOMATIC path -- a screen that reappears every launch cannot be dismissed
-- and wrong as the only path, because this screen is the one place several
of these settings are EXPLAINED rather than merely offered. A user who
dismissed it lost the explanation along with the questions.

IN HELP, NOT PREFERENCES. Help is where somebody goes to be told what a
choice means; Preferences is where they go when they already know and want
to change it. Both exist and they answer different questions.

THE MENU ENTRY DOES NOT ASK `should_open`. It would refuse on every launch
after the first, and a menu item that does nothing is the inert control this
codebase keeps meeting -- three of them were reported on the same day this
was added. The automatic path keeps its guard; the menu is the user asking.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DONE 2026-08-22 -- THE SECOND PASS, FROM SEEING IT RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Everything above shipped, and then five things were reported on meeting the
screen itself. All five are fixed and all five are MEASURED, because the
first of them was invisible to a code reading.

THE BLACK BOXES. This palette's `bg` is literally `#000000`, so every plain
QWidget between the translucent card and the drifting backdrop -- the page
stack, each page, the provider strip -- was caught by the blanket `QWidget`
rule and painted a pure black rectangle over the animation the dialog had
just installed. The widget tree gives no sign of this; it took a grab and a
pixel count. 92% of the page stack was `#000000` and it is 0% now. THE
CONTAINERS ONLY: the combos keep their surface, because a control you can
see through is a control you cannot read.

THE PAUSE FOR THE GREETING. "there should be a lag after the first next
click to make time for Hello in the chosen language". The greeting is the
only proof the language choice took and it lives on the page being LEFT, so
without a pause it was on screen for one frame of a fade. The rim starts its
circuit immediately -- the click is answered at once -- and only the page
change waits. Next is disabled for the wait, or a second click would queue a
second advance and skip a slide. It happens ONCE: a pause on every return to
the first slide is a delay the user has already sat through.

The greeting also has its own line now. It was prepended to the explanation,
so choosing a language rewrote the paragraph under the title and the one
word that changed was buried in it.

THE PROVIDERS ARE THEIR MARKS. Claude's radiating burst, OpenAI's six-fold
knot, Gemini's four-pointed spark, each a `QPainterPath` built in
`spacr.qt.widgets.provider_marks`. DRAWN, NOT SHIPPED: a bitmap would be a
vendor asset in the repository with its own licence, its own resolution and
its own two versions for light and dark. The brand colour is used only where
that vendor's CLI is actually installed -- colour is the fastest answer to
"which of these can I use" -- and an absent one is drawn in the theme's
muted ink rather than in a greyed version of its own colour.

THE RIM. Longer, fading, and quicker. It was ONE FLAT STROKE, which has two
hard ends that arrive and leave abruptly; it is twenty-four segments now,
each with its own alpha and its own width, brightest just BEHIND THE HEAD
and falling to nothing at both ends. A highlight that peaks in the middle
reads as a bar; one that peaks at the front reads as something moving. The
lit run went from 90 px to 150, and the ease from 0.18 to 0.34 -- still
travel, not a jump, and asserted not to overshoot.

AND ONE SPEED, NOT TWO. "most notably make the transitions smoother!"
`setCurrentIndex` swapped the page between two frames while the rim took
half a second to travel, so one gesture had two speeds -- which is what read
as unfinished. The pages cross-fade over 260 ms. The animation is HELD on
the dialog or Qt collects it before it runs, and the opacity effect comes
back OFF when the fade ends: it renders its widget into an offscreen pixmap
on every repaint, and six left alive over a drifting backdrop is a cost paid
per frame for an animation that has finished.
