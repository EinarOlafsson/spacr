--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE MODEL IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One model with three heads:

  1. OBJECTS.      Is this pixel foreground. (Cellpose already does this.)
  2. SPACE FLOWS.  The 2D vector field that splits touching objects.
                   (Cellpose already does this.)
  3. TIME FLOWS.   For each pixel of an object in frame t, a vector pointing at
                   where that same object's centre is in frame t+1. Following
                   it gives the object the same label in both frames.

Head 3 is plausible for exactly the reason head 2 works. Cellpose never
predicts labels; it predicts a dense local field and lets the labels fall out
of following it. A field is defined at every pixel, trains with a plain
regression loss, and needs no assignment step inside the loss -- which is what
makes it trainable at all. A division is two frames' worth of vectors pointing
back at one parent: representable, where a one-to-one matcher has to
special-case it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PLAN, IN ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 0 -- SEGMENTATION FIRST, AND IT IS USEFUL ALONE.
    Fine-tune cpsam_v2 on the light-microscopy datasets, then again on the
    timelapse frames. Two stages in that order: the timelapse set is small and
    would overfit on its own. This produces a usable model before any of the
    research below, and it is the backbone everything else is built on.

STEP 1 -- CHECK THE TRAINING DATA BEFORE WRITING MODEL CODE.
    The labels are said to be consistent across frames. Verify it, because
    every later result rests on it:
      - Is a label ever REUSED after its object leaves or dies? A recycled id
        teaches the model to point at an unrelated cell. This is the most
        common defect in tracking annotation and the easiest to miss.
      - Are divisions annotated as two new ids, or does one child inherit the
        parent's? Both conventions exist; the loss has to know which.
      - What is the frame interval, and is it constant within and across
        datasets?
      - Distribution of per-frame displacement in pixels. This sets whether
        the task is easy (displacement much smaller than object size) or hard,
        and it decides the receptive field the head needs.
    Write this as a QC script that produces a table, not as a one-off look.

STEP 2 -- BASELINE AND METRICS, BEFORE THE FORK.
    Build the plain stitcher -- segment each frame, link by mask overlap or
    Hungarian matching on IoU -- and the tracking metrics: TRA/DET from the
    Cell Tracking Challenge, plus a count of identity switches. This number is
    what the fork has to beat. Without it there is no way to tell whether the
    third head earned its complexity, and the temptation will be to declare it
    did.

STEP 3 -- THE FORK.
    The trainer here deliberately avoids owning a fork: it does checked AST
    surgery on train_seg so the installed Cellpose is never edited. A new
    output channel and a new loss term is past what that can carry. So: a real
    fork, pinned to one upstream Cellpose version, with the diff kept as small
    as it can be -- a head, a loss term, a dataloader that yields PAIRS of
    frames, and nothing else touched. Record the upstream commit it forked
    from in the model's provenance, the way run_config.json already records
    versions.

STEP 4 -- WHAT THE TIME HEAD PREDICTS, CONCRETELY.
    Two extra channels of vector, plus a third scalar the plan as given did
    not include and needs:
      - dx, dy: toward the object's centre in frame t+1.
      - HAS SUCCESSOR: a validity channel. Cells die, leave the field of view,
        and the last frame has no t+1 at all. Without this the model is
        trained to invent a successor for every object, and at inference every
        disappearance becomes a wrong link. Supervise the vectors ONLY where
        this channel is true.
    Condition on the frame interval, or train across a range of intervals,
    because a displacement field silently encodes pixels-per-frame and a
    dataset imaged every 30 s and one every 10 min are different problems.

STEP 5 -- TRAINING.
    - The dataloader yields frame PAIRS, and every augmentation must be applied
      IDENTICALLY to both frames of a pair. A flip applied to t and not t+1
      teaches the model a displacement that never happened. This is the single
      easiest way to get a model that trains beautifully and tracks nothing.
    - Curriculum: start from the step 0 weights, freeze the backbone, train the
      new head alone, then unfreeze at a low learning rate. The segmentation
      ability is already paid for and should not be spent re-learning.
    - Sample pairs across the displacement distribution from step 1, not
      uniformly over frames, or the model sees mostly easy, nearly-static
      pairs.

STEP 6 -- THE SCRAMBLE TEST, AS THE TRAINING LOOP'S OWN CHECK.
    Hold out a set. Scramble the object labels in frame t+1. Let the model
    relabel t+1 by following its time flows from t. Score the concordance
    between the recovered labels and the true ones.
    This is a good test and it belongs in the loop, reported per epoch beside
    the losses, the way the segmentation scores already are. Two refinements
    so it does not flatter the model:
      - Report it STRATIFIED by displacement and by local object density. A
        sparse, slow field is relabelled correctly by almost anything, and an
        unstratified average is dominated by those easy cases.
      - It measures LINKING GIVEN PERFECT SEGMENTATION, because the true masks
        are handed to it. Report the end-to-end number too -- the model's own
        segmentation, linked by its own flows, scored with the step 2 tracking
        metrics. The gap between the two says whether the remaining errors are
        tracking errors or segmentation errors, which decides what to work on
        next.
    Add the obvious controls: a scramble test on a model with an untrained
    time head should score at chance, and one where t+1 is a copy of t should
    score near perfect. If either does not, the harness is wrong, not the
    model.

STEP 7 -- INFERENCE AND THE SUBMODULE.
    Link frame to frame, then resolve tracks over the whole movie: divisions
    become parent/child edges, gaps are bridged or not, and the result is
    written to the measurements database with a track id alongside the object
    id, so downstream measurement and regression see tracks as first-class.
    Decide early whether a track id is stable across a re-run -- it must be, or
    no analysis built on it is reproducible.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW IT COULD STILL FAIL, HONESTLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - If per-frame displacement is routinely larger than an object, the vector
    has to point further than the receptive field can see, and the field
    becomes ambiguous where several identical cells are candidates. Step 1
    tells you this before any code is written.
  - If the annotation reuses labels, the model learns to link unrelated cells
    and the scramble test will not catch it -- the scrambled truth is wrong in
    the same way.
  - If the plain stitcher from step 2 is already at the accuracy the biology
    needs, the fork is a maintenance burden for no gain. That is a real
    possible outcome and should be accepted if it happens.

2026-09-21 -- STEPS 3 TO 6 ARE CODE: spacr/timeflows_model.py
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Built on the CPU, with a stand-in backbone in the tests and the real
Cellpose-SAM backbone in a smoke run:

  * STEP 3, THE FORK, without forking. Cellpose-SAM's encoder is replayed up to
    its neck (CellposeSamFeatures: 256 channels at 1/8), shared by both frames,
    and a new head reads the two frames' features together. The installed
    Cellpose is not edited and its segmentation head is untouched. The
    encoder runs in its own bfloat16; features reach the head as float32.
    Checked on the real network: 305 M parameters, 0.45 M of them the head,
    a 256 x 256 pair in and a (2, 256, 256) field plus a successor map out.
  * STEP 4, THE TARGET. Per pixel of frame t, (dy, dx) to the successor's
    centre in t+1, in the object's own diameters, and a successor flag; the
    vector is supervised only where a successor exists, background never.
    Children of a division take new labels (step 1's finding), so a dividing
    parent has no successor, which is what the flag encodes.
  * STEP 5, TRAINING. Pairs augmented identically (one flip/quarter-turn for
    both frames), targets computed AFTER from the augmented labels so vectors
    cannot disagree with the pixels; pairs drawn across the displacement range
    (equal-width bins -- quantile bins collapse when most pairs move alike);
    the curriculum head-only on a frozen backbone, then everything at a low
    learning rate. On a CPU stand-in the loss falls.
  * STEP 6, THE SCRAMBLE TEST. Frame t+1's labels are shuffled and the links
    scored against the truth, beside the IoU stitcher on the same pair; with a
    perfect prediction the model recovers every identity where IoU cannot.
  * DATA. ctc_pairs() builds pairs from a CTC movie: frames, the silver
    segmentation relabelled by the tracking markers (the TRA ground truth is
    markers only) -- full masks carrying track ids.
  * THE GPU JOB IS ONE COMMAND:
        python -m spacr.timeflows_model --movies <ctc movie dirs> \
            --base cpsam --out timeflows_v1.pt
    It waits for the GPU to be idle 20 minutes (the maintainer's rule); then
    step 6 is scored on held-out movies against step 2's stitcher.
  * spaCR's own Cellpose training takes a base_model now (a checkpoint, a zoo
    key or cpsam), so step 0's second stage can continue from the first
    instead of silently restarting from stock weights.
