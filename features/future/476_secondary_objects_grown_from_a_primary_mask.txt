WHAT WAS BUILT ALREADY, UNDER ITEM 473
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The four numbered steps above are done and shipped: Make Masks' Detection
method category offers "Maxima + propagate", which blurs, finds the local
maxima, grows a seeded watershed out of each one and stops by one of four
rules. It runs in both scopes -- the magnifier's box and the whole image --
and records its settings with the mask.

The engine is
:func:`spacr.qt.mask_engine.maxima_propagate_instances`.

WHAT IS LEFT, AND WHY IT IS A SEPARATE ITEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CellProfiler's primary/secondary pair is TWO features and Make Masks now
has one of them:

* PRIMARY objects are found from the image alone -- a nucleus channel, say.
  That is "Maxima + propagate", and it is done.
* SECONDARY objects are grown from an EXISTING mask of another object
  type: cells grown outward from the nuclei already segmented, each cell
  keeping its own nucleus's identity, so cell 7 is the cell around nucleus
  7 and the two tables join on that number.

The second is a different feature because its INPUT is different. It does
not find seeds; it is given them, as a labelled mask of another object
class, and the identity of each seed has to survive into the result -- that
is the whole point of the pair. Make Masks has no notion of "the other
object's mask" today: it edits one mask at a time.

WHAT IT WOULD REUSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The propagation itself, unchanged. :func:`maxima_propagate_instances`
already builds a marker image and grows it with
``skimage.segmentation.watershed(-image, markers, mask=...)``; a secondary
run is that call with the markers taken from the primary MASK instead of
from ``peak_local_max``, and with the resulting labels kept rather than
renumbered. The four stop rules, the hole filling and the minimum-area
filter apply as they stand.

So the work is:

1. Split the marker-building out of :func:`maxima_propagate_instances`, so
   "grow these markers by this rule" is a function of its own and the
   maxima are one way of producing markers.
2. A way for Make Masks to hold a SECOND mask -- the primary objects -- and
   a picker for which object class it is. This is the real design question
   and the reason this is not a half-hour change: the screen, the queue,
   the save path and the curation ledger are all one-mask-at-a-time today.
3. The label identity contract: a secondary object carries its primary's
   id, and a primary with no secondary and a secondary with no primary are
   both reportable states rather than silent ones.
4. Measure it the way item 473 measured the rest, on a set with both a
   nucleus and a cell ground truth.

WHAT TO BE CAREFUL WITH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* CellProfiler's Propagation uses a shortest-path cost mixing local image
  differences and distance. spaCR's negative-intensity watershed is a
  different algorithm, not an equivalent lambda-zero implementation.
  The CellProfiler manual lists Watershed - Image separately from Propagation:
  https://cellprofiler-manual.s3.amazonaws.com/CPmanual/IdentifySecondaryObjects.html
  The baseline here is deliberately intensity watershed. Whether a separate
  distance/intensity growth mode is needed remains a paired-data evaluation
  question, not a claim of CellProfiler equivalence.

ENGINE MILESTONE, 2026-09-22
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
secondary_object_instances takes a primary mask and retains its exact IDs,
including sparse uint64 IDs, by using compact internal markers. It includes
the primary footprint before size filtering and reports missing, orphaned,
incompletely enclosed and unexpanded IDs through primary_secondary_report.
All four stop rules share the existing maxima detector's growth helper.
240 seeded-random old/new comparisons preserve labels, seed counts and
threshold levels exactly for the existing maxima detector.

canonical_labels, save_mask and save_seg_bundle now offer explicit keyword
preserve_ids=True. A lone cell900 or separated pieces of cell900 are saved
as900, not interpreted as binary or split into new IDs. TIFF and Cellpose
bundle save/reload checks verify this. Invalid/oversized IDs are refused
without overwriting the previous mask; the GUI storage format remains uint16.
Default editing/save behavior remains unchanged.

127 focused engine, persistence and CPU-settings tests pass. No GUI selector
is claimed yet. Remaining work: primary source/class selection per field,
ROI requests and accepted-label identity, save/queue/ledger provenance,
editing/filter/lookup operations that otherwise reassign IDs, and measurement
on paired nucleus/cell reference data with its annotation quality stated.
