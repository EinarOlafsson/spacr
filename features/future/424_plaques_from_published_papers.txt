================================================================================

Shape:     The plaque module takes a DOI, a PMID or a PDF. It finds the figures,
           finds the plaque images inside them with the trained YOLO detector,
           segments and measures the plaques, works out WHAT CONDITION each
           plaque image shows, and writes the lot to the database.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. INPUT: a DOI, a PMID, or a PDF the user already has.

  2. FETCH. Given a DOI or PMID, search the European database -- the same one
     that was scraped to build the plaque model's training set, so the
     retrieval path already exists in this project and should be reused rather
     than written again.

  3. FIND THE PLAQUE IMAGES. Run the trained YOLO detector over the downloaded
     files. It was trained to locate wells in plate images; here it is being
     asked to find plaque images inside FIGURES, which is a related but not
     identical problem -- see the open questions.

  4. MEASURE. Segment and measure the plaques in each detected image, and save
     to the database, the same measurements the plaque module already produces
     for images from a microscope.

  5. ATTRIBUTE THE CONDITION. Below. This is the part that makes the output
     worth anything: a plaque area with no condition attached is a number
     nobody can use.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATTRIBUTING THE CONDITION -- TWO STRATEGIES, ALWAYS BOTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both are ALWAYS applied, not one as a fallback for the other failing. Two
independent readings that agree is evidence; one reading is a guess.

  STRATEGY 1 -- TEXT AROUND THE IMAGE.
      Detect the text immediately around each plaque image -- the label above,
      beside or beneath it -- and annotate the image with it. This is the
      direct route: most figures label the panel with the condition.

  STRATEGY 2 -- PANEL LAYOUT PLUS FIGURE LEGEND.
      Detect which panel each plaque image sits in, and where each image sits
      RELATIVE TO THE OTHERS. Then read the figure legend and attribute each
      image from the panel letter, its position, and what the legend says about
      that panel. This is how a human reads a figure whose panels are labelled
      only A, B, C.

  WHEN BOTH FAIL.
      Annotate each plaque by the DOCUMENT it came from and its position
      relative to the other plaques in that document. That is weak, and it
      must be recorded AS weak: a row whose condition came from this route must
      be distinguishable in the database from one that was read off a label,
      or the whole dataset inherits the uncertainty of its worst rows.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTE -- THE MODELS THIS DEPENDS ON ARE ABOUT TO CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Many more images have been curated for a new training round of BOTH the YOLO
detector and the plaque segmentation model, and the plaque model is to move to
a cpsam_v2 backbone. That is expected in the near future.

So this module should not hard-code a checkpoint. It should ask the model zoo
for the current plaque detector and the current plaque segmenter, so the
retrain lands here without a code change -- and so a result can record WHICH
model version produced it, which matters more here than usual, because these
measurements come from other people's published figures.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPEN QUESTIONS -- ANSWER BEFORE BUILDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - The YOLO detector finds WELLS in plate photographs. Finding plaque images
    inside a published figure is a different distribution -- cropped, rescaled,
    JPEG-compressed, often greyscale. Does it transfer, or does it need its own
    training round? Measure this before building on it.
  - Scale. A plaque area in pixels is meaningless across papers with different
    magnifications and no scale bar. Is a scale bar detected, is a well
    diameter used as the ruler the way the plate pipeline does, or are only
    RATIOS within one figure reported?
  - Licensing and redistribution: which of these papers may have their figures
    downloaded, stored and republished as crops in a database?
  - What happens when the two strategies DISAGREE? Recording both with a
    conflict flag is more useful than silently preferring one.
  - PDF text extraction is unreliable on scanned and vector-mixed figures. Is
    OCR in scope, and which engine?
  - Deduplication: the same figure appears in a preprint and the published
    version, and the same plaque should not be counted twice.

2026-09-19 — DOES IT TRANSFER? PARTIAL: ROUND WELLS YES, CROPPED PANELS NO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WAS MEASURED, AND WHAT A REGION MEANS

  Two samples, drawn from Europe PMC's open-access subset, CC-BY only, with a
  fixed seed, and with EVERY PMC ARTICLE IN THE v4 DETECTOR'S PUBLISHED SPLIT
  (670 of them) refused at sampling time -- v4 trained on 939 reviewed PMC
  figures, so a paper it trained on measures nothing.

    arm A   20 papers, any organism, "plaque assay" and its variants.
            129 figures. Mostly virology and phage; that is what the
            open-access plaque literature is.
    arm B    8 papers, Toxoplasma only, none of them in v4's split.
            53 figures. The in-domain arm.

  Every figure of every sampled paper was kept -- blots, graphs, schematics,
  micrographs -- because precision measured only on plaque figures is not
  precision. 158 of the 182 figures hold no plaque image at all.

  A REGION is one plaque-assay image a measurement would have to separate from
  its neighbours, counted from the figure by eye, in two kinds:

    well    a whole round culture surface photographed whole: a well, a dish,
            a plate. This is what the detector was trained to find.
    crop    a plaque image that is not round: a stained monolayer field, a
            lawn strip, a dilution row.

  Whether plaques happen to be countable in a region does not change that it
  is one; a graph OF plaque counts is not a region. Every box was judged from
  a per-model overlay: tp = first box on a region, dup = a later box on a
  region already claimed, fp = anything else. Duplicates count against
  precision because a duplicate becomes a duplicated measurement row.

THE TABLE

  v1 = toxoplasma_well_detector_v1, yolo_welldetect_v3.pt, PLATE PHOTOGRAPHS
       ONLY. This is the one 424's question is about.
  v2 = toxoplasma_well_detector_v2, yolo_welldetect_v4.pt, the CURRENT zoo
       detector, trained with 939 PMC figures in it.
  Both at conf 0.25, imgsz 640, on the CPU with the GPU hidden; 182 figures
  x 2 models in 31 s, which is why this measurement never queued for a GPU.

  arm A -- 20 papers, any organism; 129 figures, 251 wells, 49 crops,
           114 figures with no plaque image

    model  boxes   TP  dup   FP  precision  wells found   crops found  macro
    v1       168  127   21   20      0.756  121/251 .482    6/49 .122   .631
    v2        36   15    9   12      0.417    9/251 .036    6/49 .122   .233

  arm B -- 8 Toxoplasma papers, none in v4's split; 53 figures, 8 wells,
           61 crops, 44 figures with no plaque image

    model  boxes   TP  dup   FP  precision  wells found   crops found  macro
    v1         8    4    0    4      0.500      4/8 .500    0/61 .000   .111
    v2         5    4    0    1      0.800      4/8 .500    0/61 .000   .111

  POOLED: wells 125/259 (48 %) for v1 and 13/259 (5 %) for v2; crops 6/110
  (5 %) for both; false boxes on the 158 figures with no plaque image, 23 for
  v1 and 13 for v2.

  TWO RECALLS ARE PRINTED AND NEITHER IS "THE" ONE. Micro pools every region,
  so ONE 96-well plate photograph (PMC13278376 Fig 8, 192 wells, of which v1
  boxed 88 and v2 none) carries 64 % of arm A's regions. Macro averages the
  per-figure recall, so every figure counts once. They disagree -- .423 and
  .631 for v1 in arm A -- and printing one alone would be choosing.

VERDICT: PARTIAL, AND THE PART THAT FAILS IS THE PART THIS PIPELINE NEEDS

  * It TRANSFERS to round wells and dishes printed in figures. v1 finds about
    half of them, across organisms it never trained on -- phage plates,
    thogotovirus and adenovirus crystal-violet wells, Listeria plaque
    monolayers. Cropped, rescaled and JPEG-compressed did not stop it.
  * It DOES NOT transfer to cropped rectangular plaque panels: 6 of 110, and
    all six of those are the round dilution spots of one phage strip figure.
    In Toxoplasma papers the number is 0 of 61.
  * That is the form Toxoplasma papers print. 61 of arm B's 69 regions were
    crops: 38 in one mSphere figure (TgPDE1-18, vehicle and auxin), 14 in a
    PLOS Pathogens glycolysis paper, 5 stained fields in a PLOS NTD paper,
    4 in an mBio figure. All of them measurable; none of them found.
  * PRECISION IS NOT FREE EITHER. v1's 23 false boxes landed on a pie chart,
    a cartoon virion, two clusters of scatter dots, a brightfield organoid,
    ten bacterial MOTILITY plates -- round dishes that are not plaque assays
    at all -- and a domain diagram. A pipeline that measures those and writes
    them to the database with a DOI attached is worse than one that finds
    less.

THE SURPRISE, AND IT MATTERS FOR THE ZOO

  THE OLDER DETECTOR BEAT THE CURRENT ONE ON EVERY FIGURE THAT WAS NOT
  TOXOPLASMA. v4 fired 36 times against v1's 168 in arm A and found 9 of 251
  wells against 121. It is not broken: on the Toxoplasma arm the two are level
  (4 of 8 wells each) and v4 is the cleaner of the two (1 false box against 4).
  v4 was trained with 614 confirmed no-well figures to stop it boxing
  everything, and what it appears to have learned with them is Toxoplasma
  plaque wells specifically. 437 records that v4 beats v3 on the shared test
  set; that test set is Toxoplasma. Both statements are true, and 424 needs
  the second one: THE ZOO'S CURRENT DETECTOR IS THE WRONG FRONT HALF FOR A
  CROSS-ORGANISM LITERATURE PIPELINE, and the deprecated-looking v3 row is
  the better one for it. Neither row says this; 437's "deliberately not
  removing the v3 row" turns out to have been righter than its reason.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT BUILDING 424

  Building on the detector alone would fetch a paper, find half its wells,
  miss every cropped panel, and add a handful of pie charts. The module needs
  a detector that has seen a cropped plaque panel. A training round is written
  up as a queued GPU job -- see below -- and its prerequisite is annotation,
  not GPU time.

A MEASUREMENT DEFECT FOUND AND FIXED MID-RUN, RECORDED BECAUSE IT WOULD HAVE
GONE UNNOTICED

  The figure fetcher kept one file per figure ranked BY FORMAT. PLOS ships
  `g001.webp` at 1600 px beside `g001.gif` at 200 px, and webp was not in the
  extension list at all, so six PLOS figures were handed to the detector as
  200-pixel thumbnails. Nothing failed; the detector simply found nothing, and
  "found nothing" is the result being measured. The rule is now SIZE FIRST
  with format only breaking ties, the figures were refetched, and the six were
  relabelled (they hold no plaque image either way; one extra false box
  appeared at full size). Every figure's pixel dimensions are now recorded
  beside its hash.

HOW TO REPRODUCE IT

    python tools/measure_plaque_detector_transfer.py --out <dir> \
        --stages sample,figures,detect --papers 20 --seed 424 \
        --main-figures-only --overlay-all \
        --exclude-pmcids <v4 repo training/split.csv>
    (look at overlays/<model>/*.png, write labels.json)
    python tools/measure_plaque_detector_transfer.py --out <dir> --stages score

  The labels, the boxes, the papers with their DOIs and licences, and both
  summaries are committed at
  features/data/424_detector_transfer_2026-09-19.json, so a new detector can
  be scored against the SAME hand labels rather than a new opinion.

WHAT IS LEFT, HONESTLY

  * ONE READER LABELLED 182 FIGURES, from the overlays, in one pass. Nobody
    has checked the labels against a second reader, and the boxes were visible
    while the regions were being counted, so a missed region next to a drawn
    box is the anchoring failure this design can have. The 96-well figure was
    counted from a zoomed crop and its duplicate boxes were found by IoU
    rather than by eye.
  * n IS SMALL WHERE IT MATTERS MOST. Arm B holds 8 round wells. 4 of 8 is a
    number with a confidence interval wider than the difference it is being
    used to describe; it is the crops (0 of 61) that carry the verdict.
  * ONLY THE FIRST OPEN QUESTION IS ANSWERED. Scale, the two condition
    strategies and their conflict flag, deduplication by DOI plus image hash
    (the harness hashes and flags duplicates but nothing consumes it), the
    database, and the PDF text layer are all untouched.
  * The harness calls spacr.plaque.detect_wells with min_axis_ratio=0, so
    nothing is dropped before a person sees it; each box records whether the
    shipped 0.7 filter would have kept it. It would have kept 173 of v1's 176
    boxes and 40 of v2's 41, so the filter is not what is losing the crops.
  * A GPU JOB IS QUEUED AND MUST NOT BE RUN YET:
    scratchpad/gpu_queue/424a-detector-transfer-1.md. It trains a two-class
    detector (well, plaque_panel) on v4's data plus 300-600 newly annotated
    figures, 30-45 min, ~6 GB VRAM, and its success check is this same
    182-figure set rescored against these same labels -- 6 of 110 crops today.
    Its prerequisite is the annotation, which is human work, and a training
    run needs the maintainer's go-ahead.
