Shape:     The plaque module takes a DOI, a PMID or a PDF. It finds the figures,
           finds the plaque images inside them with the trained YOLO detector,
           segments and measures the plaques, works out WHAT CONDITION each
           plaque image shows, and writes the lot to the database.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. INPUT: a DOI, a PMID, or a PDF the user already has.

  2. FETCH. Given a DOI or PMID, search the European database -- the same one
     that was scraped to build the plaque model's training set, so the
     retrieval path already exists in this project and should be reused rather
     than written again.

  3. FIND THE PLAQUE IMAGES. Run the trained YOLO detector over the downloaded
     files. It was trained to locate wells in plate images; here it is being
     asked to find plaque images inside FIGURES, which is a related but not
     identical problem -- see the open questions.

  4. MEASURE. Segment and measure the plaques in each detected image, and save
     to the database, the same measurements the plaque module already produces
     for images from a microscope.

  5. ATTRIBUTE THE CONDITION. Below. This is the part that makes the output
     worth anything: a plaque area with no condition attached is a number
     nobody can use.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATTRIBUTING THE CONDITION -- TWO STRATEGIES, ALWAYS BOTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both are ALWAYS applied, not one as a fallback for the other failing. Two
independent readings that agree is evidence; one reading is a guess.

  STRATEGY 1 -- TEXT AROUND THE IMAGE.
      Detect the text immediately around each plaque image -- the label above,
      beside or beneath it -- and annotate the image with it. This is the
      direct route: most figures label the panel with the condition.

  STRATEGY 2 -- PANEL LAYOUT PLUS FIGURE LEGEND.
      Detect which panel each plaque image sits in, and where each image sits
      RELATIVE TO THE OTHERS. Then read the figure legend and attribute each
      image from the panel letter, its position, and what the legend says about
      that panel. This is how a human reads a figure whose panels are labelled
      only A, B, C.

  WHEN BOTH FAIL.
      Annotate each plaque by the DOCUMENT it came from and its position
      relative to the other plaques in that document. That is weak, and it
      must be recorded AS weak: a row whose condition came from this route must
      be distinguishable in the database from one that was read off a label,
      or the whole dataset inherits the uncertainty of its worst rows.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTE -- THE MODELS THIS DEPENDS ON ARE ABOUT TO CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Many more images have been curated for a new training round of BOTH the YOLO
detector and the plaque segmentation model, and the plaque model is to move to
a cpsam_v2 backbone. That is expected in the near future.

So this module should not hard-code a checkpoint. It should ask the model zoo
for the current plaque detector and the current plaque segmenter, so the
retrain lands here without a code change -- and so a result can record WHICH
model version produced it, which matters more here than usual, because these
measurements come from other people's published figures.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPEN QUESTIONS -- ANSWER BEFORE BUILDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - The YOLO detector finds WELLS in plate photographs. Finding plaque images
    inside a published figure is a different distribution -- cropped, rescaled,
    JPEG-compressed, often greyscale. Does it transfer, or does it need its own
    training round? Measure this before building on it.
  - Scale. A plaque area in pixels is meaningless across papers with different
    magnifications and no scale bar. Is a scale bar detected, is a well
    diameter used as the ruler the way the plate pipeline does, or are only
    RATIOS within one figure reported?
  - Licensing and redistribution: which of these papers may have their figures
    downloaded, stored and republished as crops in a database?
  - What happens when the two strategies DISAGREE? Recording both with a
    conflict flag is more useful than silently preferring one.
  - PDF text extraction is unreliable on scanned and vector-mixed figures. Is
    OCR in scope, and which engine?
  - Deduplication: the same figure appears in a preprint and the published
    version, and the same plaque should not be counted twice.

READ THIS BEFORE THE TABLE

  This note was written to report PARTIAL -- round wells transfer, cropped
  panels do not. Review found the measurement itself broken, and the verdict
  is withdrawn. What follows is kept, with the numbers exactly as they were
  taken, because it is the record of a run that happened and because the
  corpus and the region counts in it are still good. It is not an answer to
  424's first question.

  WHAT WAS WRONG. Ultralytics decodes a file path with OpenCV, so it trains
  and infers in BGR; handed a numpy array it assumes the caller has already
  done the same. `_load_image` builds an RGB array because the overlays are
  drawn with Pillow, and that array went straight to
  `spacr.plaque.detect_wells`. Every one of the 182 figures was therefore
  shown to the detector with red and blue exchanged.

  HOW IT WAS ESTABLISHED, not inferred: over all 182 figures, passing the
  FILE PATH (ultralytics' own canonical input, and how every training image
  reached these models) and passing a BGR array give identical box counts --
  182 of 182 agree -- while RGB differs. Re-running the same
  `detect_wells` call over the same 182 files both ways reproduces the
  committed record exactly on the RGB side, which is what identifies the
  committed record as the RGB one:

    model, 640 px, conf 0.25     boxes on        boxes on       boxes on the
                                 the 14 figures  the 10 figures 158 figures
                                 holding wells   holding crops  holding neither

    v1  RGB  (what was measured)      136              17              23
    v1  BGR  (what it should be)      333              16              22
    v2  RGB  (what was measured)       13              15              13
    v2  BGR  (what it should be)       61              72              15

  v1's RGB row sums to 176 boxes and v2's to 41, which are exactly the box
  totals in the table below; that is the check that ties this to the record.

  WHY THIS VOIDS THE VERDICT AND NOT JUST THE PRECISION. The verdict rests on
  one claim -- that the detectors cannot see a cropped rectangular plaque
  panel (6 of 110, and 0 of 61 in Toxoplasma). With the channels the right way
  round the CURRENT zoo detector puts 72 boxes on the cropped-panel figures
  instead of 15, and puts at least one box on 7 of the 10 such figures instead
  of 1. Nobody has looked at whether those boxes land ON the panels, so this
  is not evidence that it does transfer either. It is evidence that the
  question was never asked. The false-box count barely moves (23 -> 22 and
  13 -> 15), so this is not the detector simply firing everywhere.

  WHAT SURVIVES: the 28 papers with their DOIs and licences, the 182 figures
  with their hashes and pixel sizes, the 369 REGION counts (those were counted
  from the figures, not from the boxes), the 670-article exclusion, the
  harness, and the scoring arithmetic with its guards. WHAT DOES NOT: every
  box, every box verdict, every `found` count, and therefore every precision,
  recall and verdict below.

  WHAT IT COSTS TO FIX: one `detect` stage rerun (about 30 s of CPU) and a
  fresh eye pass over the overlays. The region counts do not need redoing.
  The harness now converts to BGR and records `channel_order` in
  `detections.json`, so a result file says which question it answered.

  THE SAME SWAP IS IN THE SHIPPED PRODUCT, not only in this harness.
  `spacr/submodules.py` reads plate photographs with `cellpose.io.imread`,
  which returns RGB, and passes them to the same `detect_wells`, whose
  signature documents no channel order at all. That is a user-facing
  under-detection on the plate-splitting path and it is NOT fixed here,
  because it needs measuring on real plate photographs rather than on
  figures. It is written up as item 445.

WHAT WAS MEASURED, AND WHAT A REGION MEANS

  Two samples, drawn from Europe PMC's open-access subset, CC-BY only, with a
  fixed seed, and with EVERY PMC ARTICLE IN THE v4 DETECTOR'S PUBLISHED SPLIT
  (670 of them) refused at sampling time -- v4 trained on 939 reviewed PMC
  figures, so a paper it trained on measures nothing.

    arm A   20 papers, any organism, "plaque assay" and its variants.
            129 figures. Mostly virology and phage; that is what the
            open-access plaque literature is.
    arm B    8 papers matched by a Toxoplasma query, none of them in v4's
            split. 53 figures. The in-domain arm -- but only 6 of the 8 are
            actually Toxoplasma papers. The query ORs "plaque size"/"plaque
            number" against FULL TEXT and only needs Toxoplasma to appear
            somewhere in the article, so it also pulled in a von Willebrand
            factor scoping review (PMC13294430) and an ocular-microbiome /
            Alzheimer's review (PMC13564517). Those two contribute 5 of the
            53 figures and 0 of the 69 regions, so every rate below stands
            exactly as printed; what was wrong was the description of the
            arm, in the arm the verdict leans on. Read it as 6 Toxoplasma
            papers, 48 figures, 69 regions, plus 5 figures of ballast.

  Every figure of every sampled paper was kept -- blots, graphs, schematics,
  micrographs -- because precision measured only on plaque figures is not
  precision. 158 of the 182 figures hold no plaque image at all.

  A REGION is one plaque-assay image a measurement would have to separate from
  its neighbours, counted from the figure by eye, in two kinds:

    well    a whole round culture surface photographed whole: a well, a dish,
            a plate. This is what the detector was trained to find.
    crop    a plaque image that is not round: a stained monolayer field, a
            lawn strip, a dilution row.

  Whether plaques happen to be countable in a region does not change that it
  is one; a graph OF plaque counts is not a region. Every box was judged from
  a per-model overlay: tp = first box on a region, dup = a later box on a
  region already claimed, fp = anything else. Duplicates count against
  precision because a duplicate becomes a duplicated measurement row.

RESOLUTION: THE CONFOUND THIS RUN ARGUED ABOUT AND THEN DID NOT CONTROL

  The harness records every figure's pixel size, and its own docstring says a
  detector that finds nothing in a 200 px preview has not been asked the
  question this harness is asking. That principle was applied to thumbnails
  and not one level up, where it also applies.

  EVERY FIGURE IN THIS CORPUS THAT HOLDS A PLAQUE REGION IS SMALL. All 24 of
  them are at most 984 px on the long side; the median over all 182 is
  767 px. Of the 37 figures at 1000 px or more, and the 22 at 1200 px or
  more, NOT ONE holds a plaque region. So the entire measurement of recall
  was taken on the small end of the corpus.

  IT IS WORSE THAN THAT FOR THE HEADLINE NUMBER. 192 of arm A's 251 wells are
  in a single figure, PMC13278376 g008, which is 735 x 278 px -- about 30 px
  per well before the detector letterboxes it to 640. The 38-crop mSphere
  figure that carries most of arm B's crops is 753 x 984 px. "Finds half the
  wells" and "finds no crops" are both statements about ~30-90 px objects.

  THIS IS THE ROUTE'S CEILING, NOT A SELECTION BUG. The chooser now takes the
  biggest file in the bundle, and for the two figures that carry most of the
  regions the Europe PMC supplementaryFiles bundle holds nothing bigger --
  checked by listing them: PMC13278376 has g008.jpg at 130 KB and g008.gif at
  21 KB and no other copy; PMC8809380 has f004.jpg at 369 KB, f004.gif, and a
  .tif that belongs to a different figure (sf004, a supplement). Whether the
  publisher's own original is larger, through the NCBI OA package or the
  article page, is UNTESTED and worth ten minutes before anyone spends 35 GPU
  minutes.

  AND INFERENCE SIZE IS FREE TO VARY. A quick unjudged probe -- box counts
  only, nobody looked at where they landed -- over the same 182 files at
  640, 1280 and 1600 px showed it is not a neutral knob:

    v3: 13 of the 24 region-bearing figures get a box at 640, 16 at 1280 --
        but false boxes on the other 158 go 22 -> 103.
    v4: 17 at 640, 19 at 1280, and false boxes stay flat, 15 -> 12.

  Those are box counts, not recall, and they were taken with the corrected
  channels; they settle nothing. What they show is that imgsz, resolution and
  the channel swap are all tangled together in the one number this item was
  about to spend a training round on. Vary them before annotating anything.
