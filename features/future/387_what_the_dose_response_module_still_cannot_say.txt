--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE STATE IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE MODULE IS GOOD, AND THIS FILE IS NOT A CRITICISM OF IT. It fits a
four-parameter logistic (`four_parameter_logistic(x, bottom, top,
log10_ec50, hill)`), carries the direction in the sign of the Hill slope,
offers both a Wald interval and a profile-likelihood one (`CI_WALD`,
`CI_PROFILE`), runs off the GUI thread through `JobRunner`, and -- the part
most fitters get wrong -- will say `STATUS_REFUSED` or `STATUS_UNBOUNDED`
rather than invent an EC50 the experiment does not locate. Constrained fits
and the biphasic case are already handled.

WHAT IT CANNOT SAY, verified by grep on both files 2026-09-09:

  ABSENT: selectivity index, CC50, therapeutic index
  ABSENT: Bliss, Loewe, synergy, checkerboard
  ABSENT: normalisation to plate controls (percent inhibition)
  ABSENT: pooling across replicate plates (random effects, mixed model)
  ABSENT: five-parameter / asymmetric logistic
  ABSENT: hormesis (non-monotonic response)

Each of these is a sentence a dose experiment routinely needs and this
module currently cannot produce.

PRESENT ELSEWHERE, AND THE POINT IS THAT DOSE-RESPONSE DOES NOT USE IT:
`spacr/qt/widgets/control_chart.py` already computes a per-plate Z-factor
(`zprime_frame`, when a positive and a negative control are named) and charts
it (`zprime_chart`). The Dose-Response screen neither reads it nor mentions
it. So the plate-quality number exists, on a different screen, and a user can
fit a curve on a plate the package already knows is bad.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE FIRST ONE IS THE IMPORTANT ONE. For an anti-parasitic compound the
number that decides whether anybody cares is not the EC50, it is the ratio
of host toxicity to parasite killing -- CC50 over EC50, the selectivity
index. A compound that kills Toxoplasma at 1 uM and the HFF monolayer at
1.2 uM is not a hit, and today spaCR will report its EC50 with a clean
confidence interval and say nothing about that.

spaCR IS UNUSUALLY WELL PLACED TO FIX THIS, which is the argument for doing
it here rather than in a spreadsheet. The module already segments host cell
and pathogen as separate object types from the same image. Host viability
and parasite burden come off ONE plate, at the SAME doses, in the same run.
Most tools computing a selectivity index are dividing two numbers from two
different experiments and hoping the conditions matched. Here they cannot
fail to match.

The rest follow from how dose plates are actually read: raw response values
are not comparable across plates until they are normalised to the controls
on each plate, replicate plates want pooling rather than three EC50s and an
eyeball, and a plate whose Z'-factor is 0.1 should not be silently fitted at
all.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In value order. 1 and 2 are the ones worth doing; 3-5 are cheap once the
engine grows a notion of the plate.

1. SELECTIVITY INDEX. A paired fit: two response columns from the same
   `DoseResponseSpec`, one host, one pathogen, fitted independently and then
   divided, with the interval propagated rather than dropped. The ratio of
   two uncertain numbers is more uncertain than either, and a selectivity
   index quoted without its interval is the same failure this module already
   refuses to commit for EC50. If either fit is `STATUS_REFUSED` or
   `STATUS_UNBOUNDED` the index is one-sided or refused too, and says so --
   reuse the existing status vocabulary rather than inventing a second one.

2. SYNERGY, for two-compound checkerboards. Bliss independence and Loewe
   additivity over a dose grid, plus the interaction surface as a heatmap.
   New engine functions beside the 4PL; a new tab on the screen. Report the
   surface, not a single index: one number for a whole checkerboard hides
   exactly the concentration-dependent structure that makes synergy
   interesting.

3. PLATE NORMALISATION. Percent inhibition against the positive and negative
   control wells of each plate. `spacr/control_names.py` and
   `spacr/well_scope.py` already know which wells those are.

4. CONSUME THE Z'-FACTOR THAT ALREADY EXISTS -- do not rebuild it. Call
   `zprime_frame` from `spacr/qt/widgets/control_chart.py` and carry the
   result into the fit, so a plate that fails Z' reaches the fitting code
   knowing it. The engine already has the vocabulary for this: a fit on a
   failed plate should be `STATUS_REFUSED` with the Z' in its message, not a
   clean EC50 with a footnote nobody reads. Two screens computing Z' two ways
   would be worse than the gap it closes.

5. POOLED FITS ACROSS REPLICATE PLATES -- one EC50 with plate as a random
   effect, rather than three EC50s the user averages by hand.

NOT IN THIS ORDER BUT WORTH RECORDING: the five-parameter logistic (real
asymmetry exists and 4PL absorbs it into a wrong EC50) and hormesis
detection (a non-monotonic curve should be refused loudly, not fitted
monotonically). Both are engine-level and both are smaller than 1 or 2.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THIS MODULE'S OWN STANDARD APPLIES: the useful answer is sometimes "this
experiment does not support that number", and every addition here must be
able to say it.

  * Selectivity: a plate with a known-selective compound and a known-toxic
    one. The selective compound gets an index well above 1 with an interval
    that excludes 1; the toxic one gets an index near 1, or a refusal. A
    build that hands back a clean index for the toxic compound has failed
    even if the arithmetic is right.
  * Synergy: an additive control pair must come out additive. Bliss on two
    compounds that do not interact should sit at zero across the surface;
    if it does not, the normalisation is wrong, not the biology.
  * Z' and normalisation: a deliberately bad plate must fail visibly.
  * Pooled fits: pooling three replicates of the same compound must land
    inside the three individual intervals.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELIBERATELY NOT DONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A SINGLE SYNERGY SCORE PER COMPOUND PAIR as the headline. Rejected: it is
the number people quote and it is the number that misleads, because synergy
is usually concentration-dependent and collapsing the surface destroys the
only part worth seeing. The surface is the result; a summary may sit beside
it, never instead of it.

REPLACING THE 4PL DEFAULT WITH 5PL. Rejected: the extra parameter buys
asymmetry at the cost of stability on the short dilution series most plates
actually run. Offer it, do not default to it.

CHOU-TALALAY COMBINATION INDEX as the primary synergy method. Considered;
Bliss and Loewe are better understood, and CT's median-effect assumptions
are routinely violated by exactly the refused and unbounded curves this
module is careful about.

2026-09-15 -- THE SCREEN HALF, STEP 1 OF 4: EACH PLATE AGAINST ITS OWN CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MEASURED FIRST, BY NAME. Of the engine's 26 public names the Dose-Response
screen used six: `fit_frame`, the spec/set/result classes and the two column
pickers. Everything the five numbered tasks built -- `selectivity_index`,
`checkerboard_from_frame` with `bliss_surface` / `loewe_surface`,
`normalise_to_controls` / `plate_reports`, `pool_frame` -- was unreachable from
the screen. That is the "whatever the screen does not yet surface" this file's
status line names, so the screen half is four steps:

  1. plate normalisation and each plate's Z'        -- THIS STEP, BUILT
  2. pooled EC50 across replicate plates            -- next
  3. selectivity index (host over pathogen)
  4. checkerboard synergy, Bliss and Loewe

WHAT STEP 1 DOES. A Plates row: a plate column, a control column, and the
control column's levels as the positive and negative controls, pre-picked by
name ("pos", "neg", "vehicle", "dmso"). While either column picker reads
"(none)" the fit is exactly the raw-response fit it always was. With both
chosen, Fit normalises every plate to its own controls with
`normalise_to_controls` and fits `percent_inhibition`. The report pane leads
with one line per plate -- usable or refused, its Z', and the engine's reason
for a refusal -- because a refused plate is not in the curve and a reader
should meet that before the EC50.

NO NEW STRING FOR A TRANSLATOR. Every caption on the row -- "Plate",
"Controls", "Positive control wells", "Negative control wells", "(none)" --
already exists in the English catalog, checked against `en.UI_SOURCES` before
the row was written. The generated-catalog caption test therefore does not
move, and nine languages owe nothing for this step. The new methods are
private, so the public API surface does not move either (10,536 before and
after).

HOW IT WAS CHECKED. `tests/qt/test_dose_response_normalises_to_each_plate.py`
builds three plates of one compound at three absolute scales, one of them with
no vehicle wells. Five tests; all 48 existing dose-response screen tests still
pass. Each mutation reddens tests that can only fail for that reason:

      the pickers never become a plate spec    3 red
      the report never carries the verdicts    2 red
      the control column offers no levels      4 red

A MISTAKE CAUGHT BY THE TESTS, recorded because it is an easy one:
`PlateReport.usable` is a PROPERTY. Calling it raised inside the table's
selection slot, the report pane stayed at "fitting…", and only the tests that
selected a row could see it.

2026-09-15 -- STEP 2 OF 4: ONE EC50 PER GROUP, POOLED ACROSS REPLICATE PLATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES. With a plate column chosen, every group's EC50 is also pooled
across the plates it was fitted on, with `pool_frame` -- log10 scale, plate as
a random effect. The report pane gives one line per group after the plate
verdicts: the pooled EC50 and its interval, how many plates were used of how
many, I-squared, "plates disagree" when the engine says the replicates are not
reproducible, and the engine's note naming any plate that could not be fitted.

TWO DECISIONS, recorded so they can be reversed deliberately:

  1. POOLING NEEDS ONLY THE PLATE COLUMN, not the controls. Each plate is
     fitted on its own scale, so an EC50 is comparable across plates even when
     their raw signals are not. With controls chosen as well, the pool runs on
     the normalised table, and a refused plate is named rather than pooled.
  2. A GROUP SEEN ON ONE PLATE IS NOT POOLED. `pool_frame` fits every row of a
     plate as one curve, so the screen splits by group first; a group on a
     single plate would yield a "pooled" number over one replicate, claiming a
     reproducibility nobody measured.

STILL NO NEW STRING FOR A TRANSLATOR: the pooled lines are built from the
engine's numbers and notes. The public API surface is unchanged at 10,536.

HOW IT WAS CHECKED. `tests/qt/test_dose_response_pools_replicate_plates.py`
reuses the step-1 plates, so both features are measured on the same data.
Five tests; with steps 1 and 2 and the 48 existing screen tests, 58 pass.

      nothing is ever pooled                     3 red
      the report leaves the pooled lines out     2 red
      a group on one plate is pooled anyway      1 red

STEPS 3 AND 4 NEED NEW CAPTIONS, which steps 1 and 2 did not. Selectivity needs
a second response picker for the host readout, and synergy a second dose
picker; the English catalog has neither "Host", "Toxicity", "Viability",
"Selectivity" nor a second-agent label. New runtime captions are the catalog
lane's, so those two steps wait on that lane rather than landing a red caption
test on nightly.
