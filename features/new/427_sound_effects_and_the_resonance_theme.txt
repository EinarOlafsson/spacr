--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. INTERFACE FEEDBACK.
   A small sound on click and on hover, in the spirit of the iPhone click:
   short, quiet, unobtrusive, felt more than heard. The point is confirmation
   that a control registered, not decoration.

2. RUN SOUNDS.
   A distinct sound when a run STARTS and another when it STOPS. These earn
   their place: a spaCR run is long, the user is usually in another window, and
   an audible "it finished" is the one sound in this list with a real job. The
   stop sound should differ for finished-successfully and stopped-with-an-error
   -- a user who hears "done" and finds a traceback will not trust the sound
   again.

3. BACKGROUND MUSIC.
   A separate option from the feedback sounds, with its own switch: someone who
   wants click feedback while working in silence must not get music too.
   Decided 2026-09-19: no streaming service and no bundled recordings. The
   music is a looping bed SYNTHESIZED by spaCR in the theme's key (so nothing
   carries a licence question), or a local audio file the user picks.

4. THE RESONANCE THEME.
   A new theme whose background is a visualised FLOOR with particles on it. The
   particles flow, gather into shapes and bounce in response to the sound's
   resonance -- amplitude and pitch -- like a digital Chladni plate. Chladni
   figures are the right reference: real resonance patterns on a vibrating
   plate are exactly particles settling at the nodes, so the physical analogy
   gives a principled rule for where particles should go rather than an
   arbitrary one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PART THAT DECIDES WHETHER THIS IS ANY GOOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

EVERYTHING OFF BY DEFAULT, AND IT MUST STAY THAT WAY. A scientific tool that
makes noise the first time it is opened, in a shared office or during a talk,
is a tool people learn to distrust. Off is the default, and the tab is last
because it is the least important thing in Preferences.

HOVER SOUNDS ARE THE RISKY ONE. A sound on every hover, on a dense settings
panel, becomes a stream of clicks as the pointer crosses the window. If it is
built: debounce it hard, only on interactive controls, never on a control that
is disabled, and give it its own switch separate from click feedback so it can
be turned off alone.

AUDIO MUST NOT TOUCH THE RUN. Sound belongs on the GUI thread's own timeline
and must never block, delay or fail a pipeline. A missing audio device, a
busy PulseAudio, or no sound stack at all -- headless, a cluster, SSH -- must
degrade to silence without a warning dialog and without an exception.

THE VISUALISER NEEDS A FRAME BUDGET. A particle field reacting to audio is a
per-frame loop competing with a GUI that is already doing image work on the
same machine, sometimes while the GPU is training. It needs a hard cap, a
setting to reduce or disable it, and it must yield: if frames are being
dropped, the visualiser is what gets cheaper, not the image canvas.

OPEN QUESTIONS -- ANSWER BEFORE BUILDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ALL ANSWERED 2026-09-19 -- see "Decided:" at the end. In short:

  - What plays the sound? Qt Multimedia's QSoundEffect, on its own thread,
    falling back to silence when Qt Multimedia or a device is missing.
  - Where do the sounds come from? Synthesized in code with numpy, so there
    is no licence question at all.
  - What feeds the visualiser? spaCR's own sounds, or a local audio file the
    user picks (part B).
  - Does the Resonance theme have to work with no audio at all? Yes: it
    idles beautifully in silence (part B).

2026-09-19 — PART A AFTER REVIEW: FIVE NITS, ALL FIXED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The review found no correctness or safety problem and five nits. All five
are fixed; four were real and the fifth turned out to be a measurement this
file had got wrong.

  1. "Nothing is imported while sound is off" was true at launch and not
     afterwards. notify.announce_pipeline_finished imported spacr.qt.sound
     unconditionally, so the first run that ENDED in any session paid 20.4
     ms on the GUI thread for a user who had never asked for sound, and
     SoundPage did the same on every Preferences close. Both now ask
     sys.modules first, the way apply_preferences_to_app already did.
     Nothing is lost: without an engine both calls return at once, and the
     engine is only ever built by apply_sound_preferences.

  2. THE "every later start: 5.3 ms, ready after 0.26 s" ROW WAS THE WRONG
     CASE. Re-measured four times in fresh interpreters against a warm
     cache: the first switch-on in a process costs about 30 ms (30.0, 30.2,
     31.6 and 9.5 ms) and is ready after 0.22-0.32 s. 5.3 ms is what a
     SECOND switch-on inside one process costs. The reviewer was right and
     the original probe measured through the Preferences dialog, where
     Save's own 390-460 ms of unrelated work runs with no event-loop tick
     inside it and hides the audio thread's connection entirely. The row
     and the notes now carry both cases and say which is which.

  3. Pressing a Preview button played the click sound as well as the
     previewed one -- on the Click row, the same pluck twice. The Preview
     buttons now carry the Qt property sound.SILENT_PRESS_PROPERTY
     ("spacrSilentPress"), which the input filter reads on a left press on
     a pressable widget and nowhere else.

  4. Two more things the music bed's Preview got wrong. Switching the
     master off in the dialog only greyed the rows and left nine seconds of
     bed playing underneath a switch that said sound was off. And a
     Preview taken while the STORED bed was playing silenced it after the
     fade until the dialog closed, because the fade's last step and the
     bed's stop were the same stop. The last fade step now emits
     bed_faded and the engine answers it by putting the stored settings
     back on the bed.

  5. tests/conftest.py sandboxes every other resolver that writes under the
     real ~/.spacr -- the run journal, the plate queue -- but not
     sound_synth.sound_cache_root, whose fallback is Path.home(). The three
     sound test files each point the cache somewhere of their own, so
     nothing had been written there; the first test that built a
     SoundEngine without saying where would have. SPACR_SOUND_CACHE is now
     set in pytest_configure, before collection, which also reaches the
     subprocess tests.

Tests: 5 new ones, all mutation-checked (each guard removed on its own
turned its own test red). tests/qt/test_sound_is_silent_until_asked.py 44,
tests/qt/test_the_sound_tab_is_last_and_off.py 14,
tests/qt/test_the_sounds_are_melodic_space_house.py 38.

Still open, and recorded above rather than decided here: nobody has
listened to "Orbit"; the run-START sound of section 2; the frozen bundle's
size with Qt Multimedia in it; Windows and macOS.

2026-09-19 -- part B reviewed before landing; six findings, all six fixed

Decided: "The theme should sound like melodic space house" -- the maintainer,
2026-09-19, answering what the sound sets and the bed should be.

The review found no defect in the code that makes sound or pictures. Five of
the six were documentation or user-facing copy that part B had left behind
its own change, and the sixth was a preference that did not reach half of the
new engine.

  * THE MODULE SAID SIX THEMES. spacr/qt/widgets/ambient.py's docstring --
    including the doc-comment on the line the change edited -- still said six
    animations, a seventh engine called fractal and five buffered themes,
    while docs/notes said seven. Corrected, and the docstring's theme list
    now has a resonance entry like the other six.
  * THE COPY DID NOT MENTION THE DRUMS. ORBIT.description and the Music bed
    row's tooltip both still described the version-1 bed -- pads, arpeggio,
    sub -- under a bed that now has a kick at 0.5 and a shaker at 0.34. Both
    say so now. There is still no per-user drum level: kick_level and
    shaker_level are theme fields, which is what makes part C a table of
    numbers, and a user's controls are the bed's volume and the bed's
    switch.
  * THREE DOCSTRINGS SAID SIXTEENTHS. The shaker plays eighths -- range(8)
    over a four-beat bar, 208 hits over 26 sounding bars -- and part C is
    meant to be written from those :param: docs.
  * THE COST TABLE HAD NO RESONANCE ROW. It has one now for the idle column,
    which reproduces the existing rows exactly (blobs 0.246 against the
    table's 0.240, cells 0.521 against 0.538); resonance is 1.005 to 1.072 ms
    at 1080p. The contended column is a stated range rather than a number:
    four nine-round repeats gave medians of 10, 174, 286 and 407 ms, because
    this is the one theme whose shading is dozens of small numpy calls each
    giving the interpreter lock back and queueing for it again. The cost is
    paid on the producer thread, so it is a repeated frame and not a slow
    interface, and a running module holds gil_priority.BUSY_INTERVAL where
    the same measurement is 6 to 24 ms. Nobody has watched it under a real
    Python worker.
  * RESONANCE WAS IN ONE ANIMATION MENU AND NOT THE OTHER. preferences.
    POPUP_BACKDROPS, the backdrop behind a settings window, was never
    extended. Decided that this setting is curated by being its own setting
    and not by offering fewer animations: it is now 'off' plus every name in
    AMBIENT_THEMES alphabetically, with a test asserting that equality.
  * THE SPEED PREFERENCE DID NOT REACH THE MUSIC. advance() scales the clock
    by speed, which is the whole of the preference for every theme whose
    motion is a function of the clock. Half of this one is a function of the
    music: at speed 0.1 the breath, the mode walk and the wander crawled at a
    tenth while a 122 BPM kick still threw the sand 6 % of the plate twice a
    second, for somebody who had moved the slider to its minimum to stop
    exactly that. ResonanceEngine.answering() now scales the moment by
    min(1, speed) before it is stored; above 1.0 it is not scaled up, because
    every field of a Moment is already 0 to 1 against the loop's own loudest.
