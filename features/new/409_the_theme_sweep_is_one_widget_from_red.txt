================================================================================
THE REAL-WINDOW THEME SWEEP IS ONE WIDGET AWAY FROM GOING RED AGAIN
================================================================================

Status:    OPEN, filed 2026-09-15. NOT A REGRESSION AND NOT URGENT -- it is
           identical at 7efb9b1a1 and at HEAD. It is filed because the next
           person to hit it will spend a day on it, as one was just spent.
Found by:  an adversarial review of the 2026-09-15 combo-probe fix, which
           quantified a margin the fix itself did not need.
Owner:     unassigned. `tests/qt/test_every_widget_wears_the_current_theme.py`
           (`test_the_real_window_wears_one_theme_everywhere`) and
           `spacr/qt/app.py:4309` `MainWindow.stylesheet_roots`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MARGIN, WHICH IS ONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`test_the_real_window_wears_one_theme_everywhere` samples the window's widgets
with a stride -- `hosts[::len(hosts) // 200]` -- and asks each sampled widget
whether the theme reached it.

TWO WIDGETS IN THE WINDOW ARE GENUINELY NOT REACHED, by design:

    QWidget#CentralRow      index 6 in window.findChildren(QWidget)
    QStackedWidget          index 9

and the sample is currently `hosts[::10]`. INDEX 6 AND INDEX 9 AGAINST A
STRIDE OF 10. One extra chrome widget anywhere ahead of them in the traversal
puts the stack into the sample and the test fails with

    ['QStackedWidget', 'QWidget']

which is the SAME SHAPE as the `['QComboBox']` failure that was just debugged
for a day: a test reporting "the theme did not reach this" when the theme is
working exactly as designed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THOSE TWO ARE UNREACHED ON PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`MainWindow.stylesheet_roots` deliberately excludes the central widget and the
stack -- it nominates the chrome and the page that is actually showing, and
the pages that are not showing are MARKED instead and sheeted on their own
`showEvent`. That is the per-screen sheeting design and it is right.

But the central row and the stack are not marked either, so the test's
`deferred()` branch does not cover them. They are legitimately outside the
sweep and legitimately outside the exemption, which is exactly the gap a
stride can fall into.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE TWO WAYS TO FIX IT, AND WHY NEITHER IS A REVIEW FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * MARK THEM PRODUCT-SIDE with `_spacr_is_a_sheet_target`, so `deferred()`
    covers them and the sample may contain them harmlessly. This changes
    product behaviour -- they would then be sheeted on their own show -- and
    that is a theme decision, not a test decision.
  * GIVE THE TEST A NAMED, GUARDED EXEMPTION: these two, by objectName and
    class, with an assertion that there are exactly two and that each is the
    widget the exemption names. A bare skip-list would rot into a blindfold,
    which is the failure mode this whole test family exists to prevent.

THE SECOND IS PROBABLY RIGHT and it is still bigger than it looks, because the
exemption has to fail loudly when the design changes rather than quietly
covering a third widget that should have been swept.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT NOT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DO NOT WIDEN THE STRIDE OR SAMPLE FEWER WIDGETS. That hides the margin instead
of removing it and makes the next failure rarer and therefore harder to
diagnose. The sample size is not the defect; the uncovered pair is.

DO NOT ASSUME A `['QStackedWidget', 'QWidget']` FAILURE IS A REGRESSION. Check
it against the WHOLE population first -- that is the lesson the combo day
bought, and this file exists so it is bought once.
