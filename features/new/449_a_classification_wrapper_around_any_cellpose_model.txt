WHAT THIS IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ONE SEAM, TWO HEADS. The seam is a wrapper that turns "segment" into
"segment, then say what each object is". It has two ways in, and the request
names both:

  1. MODEL IN: a Cellpose-SAM model and its options go in, the wrapper
     segments, then classifies every object of the mask it just made.
  2. MASK IN: a mask Cellpose already produced goes in and is classified as
     it stands. Nothing is re-segmented.

The heads are the classifiers that sit on that seam. Two are asked for:

  A. PARASITES PER VACUOLE. Each PV is cut out and put in one of
     1, 2, 4, 8, 16, >16. This is the replication read-out -- Toxoplasma
     divides by binary fission inside the vacuole, so those classes are the
     series that division produces rather than an arbitrary binning.
  B. WHAT THE OBJECT IS: cell, nucleus, cut cell, cut nucleus, artifact.
     With two options on top: try to merge the two halves of something cut
     in half, and remove what the head calls an artifact from the mask.

THE TWO MODELS THE FIRST HEAD SITS BEHIND ALREADY EXIST and are in the Model
Zoo: `cpsam_v2_toxo_r6` (PV segmentation) and the two cross-channel models,
`toxoplasma_from_cellmask_pv` and `toxoplasma_from_hoechst_pv`. This item
adds the SECOND model -- the classifier -- and the wrapper that runs the
pair as one operation.

WHAT ALREADY EXISTS AND SHOULD BE USED RATHER THAN REBUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * `spacr/crop_source.py::crop_object(array, mask, label, ...)` cuts one
    object out of a merged stack, either as its bounding box or with
    everything outside the object zeroed, and resizes to a fixed size. That
    IS "cut out each PV"; the classifier's input tensor is a batch of these.
  * `spacr/deep_spacr.py::train_model` trains a torch classifier on crops,
    and `spacr/classify.py`, `classifier_quality.py` and
    `classifier_evaluation.py` already score one. A new architecture is not
    needed for six classes of small crops.
  * `spacr/_segmentation_backends.py` is where a model becomes something
    with an `eval`. The wrapper should take THAT object rather than a
    Cellpose class, so it wraps Cellpose-SAM, Cellpose 3, DINOCell and
    SAMCell without knowing which it has -- items 404, 405, 423 and 446 made
    them all answer the same call.
  * `skimage.segmentation.clear_border` is already used in `utils.py` to
    drop objects touching the edge, and `measure.py`'s
    `merge_edge_pathogen_cells` already resolves a parasite straddling two
    cells. Both are prior art for the "cut" and "merge the halves" questions
    below, and whoever builds this should read them before inventing a third
    rule.

WHAT TO BUILD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. THE WRAPPER. One module. Given (a) a segmentation backend and its
     options plus an image, or (b) an image and a mask, it produces the
     mask, the per-object crops, a class and a probability for every label,
     and a table keyed on label id. It must not change the mask unless
     asked: removing artifacts is an option, not the default, and the
     original labels are kept so nothing downstream loses object identity
     (`canonical_labels` in `mask_engine.py` says why that matters).
  2. THE TWO HEADS, as separate trained models behind one interface, so a
     third head can be added without touching the seam.
  3. ARTIFACT REMOVAL, as an option that returns a NEW mask and says how
     many objects it dropped, rather than editing in place silently.
  4. MERGING TWO HALVES, as an option, with the rule written down and a
     measurement of how often it is right. See the open question.
  5. TRAINING DATA. Neither head can be trained without labelled crops.
     What produces them, and who labels them, is the fourth question below.

HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * The same image, segmented then classified, and the resulting mask
    classified on its own, give identical per-object classes.
  * A run with artifact removal off leaves the mask byte-identical to the
    one the backend produced.
  * Object ids survive: a mask that went in with ids 1, 5, 9 comes out with
    1, 5, 9 and a class for each, not renumbered.
  * The PV head is scored against a held-out set of vacuoles that were
    counted by hand, reported per class -- a classifier that is right about
    1s and wrong about 16s is not "accurate", and the class sizes are
    wildly uneven in any real field.
  * The object head is scored the same way, with the artifact class
    reported separately: a false artifact is a deleted object.

OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE MAINTAINER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. WHAT DOES A VACUOLE OF THREE DO? The classes are the binary-fission
     series, but real fields hold vacuoles of 3, 5, 6 and 7 -- parasites
     divide asynchronously and some die. Nearest class, an "other" class,
     or a count regression rounded into these bins?
  2. WHAT DOES THE PV HEAD LOOK AT? The vacuole crop in one channel, or
     several? The nuclei it is counting are Hoechst; the vacuole boundary
     is not. If it needs both, the wrapper has to carry the channel map
     through, which is a different signature.
  3. WHAT IS A "CUT" OBJECT? Touching the image border, or split into two
     labels by the segmenter? And if halves are to be merged, are they two
     halves in the SAME field, or the same object across two fields --
     which needs the stitcher, not this.
  4. WHERE DOES THE TRAINING DATA COME FROM? Hand-annotated crops through
     the Annotate module, the curation queue, or a set you label outside
     spaCR? And how many per class -- the >16 class is rare, and a model
     trained on what a normal plate contains will hardly ever see one.

RELATED. Item 421 already cuts objects and measures them from Make Masks;
item 386 wants a label-free embedding for every object, which is the
unsupervised cousin of this; item 99 explains CV models and resolves hits
back to cells. The replication assay this head serves is the module that
would consume it.

2026-09-20 -- THE FOUR QUESTIONS ANSWERED, AND WHAT THE DATA ACTUALLY IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE ANSWERS, through the question prompt.

1. A VACUOLE OF THREE: "Predict the count, bin for display." The head
   predicts how many parasites it sees and 1/2/4/8/16/>16 is how the answer
   is REPORTED, not how it is trained. A 3 stays a 3 in the model and is
   shown in whichever bin the report asks for. Nothing is taught that a
   wrong answer is right, and the binning can change later without
   retraining.

2. WHAT IT LOOKS AT -- and the maintainer replaced the question with a
   design: "there should be different modes, count from hoechst, cell mask,
   or toxo staining (ds red or antibody). so we need to generate a dataset
   where we generate pngs with hoechst, parasite stainin, cell mask as the
   channels. annotate the pngs as the classes, then train the classifier on
   each channel individually so it can count based on any of the 3
   channels."

   So it is THREE HEADS, not one with three inputs: one trained on Hoechst,
   one on the parasite stain, one on CellMask, and the caller picks the mode
   its plate can serve. A plate with no CellMask still counts from Hoechst.
   The crops are generated once, as PNGs carrying all three channels, and
   annotated once; each head then trains on its own channel of the same
   annotated set, so the three heads share their labels and differ only in
   what they are shown.

3. WHAT "CUT" MEANS: both, told apart. An object touching the image border
   and an object the segmenter split into two labels are different classes,
   because only the second can be merged where it stands -- the first needs
   the neighbouring field. The merge option acts only on the second.

4. TRAINING DATA: the Annotate module on crops.

WHAT THE PV DATA IS, MEASURED 2026-09-20 RATHER THAN ASSUMED. The
maintainer asked whether the Toxo PV dataset carries those channels. It
does not.

  * `einarolafsson/toxoplasma-pv-segmentation-dataset` holds 556 image/mask
    pairs -- the set PV rounds 5 and 6 were trained on. One image was
    downloaded and read: shape (1998, 1998), uint16. ONE CHANNEL. The repo
    is 556 images, 556 masks, and three CSVs (`fields.csv`, `folds.csv`,
    `split.csv`); there is no second or third channel anywhere in it.
  * `fields.csv` keys every field by a stem of the form
    `4hMEFRNF213_20250117_122515__plate1_A03_6_1` -- experiment, plate,
    well, field and a trailing channel index. THE STEMS ARE THE WAY BACK:
    the other channels of the same fields exist in the original
    acquisitions, if those are still somewhere.
  * THEY ARE NOT ON THIS MACHINE. Every local mount (/mnt/firecuda2,
    /mnt/einar_12TB, /mnt/wd12tb, /mnt/wd4tb, /mnt/samsung) was searched
    five levels deep for `*4hMEFRNF*` and `*20250117*`: no acquisition
    folder.
  * WHAT IS LOCAL is `/mnt/wd4tb/toxo_channel_check/`, twelve PNGs from
    2026-09-02 -- four fields, each as Hoechst, PV and CellMask. That is a
    channel check, not a training set, but it is proof the three channels
    were acquired for this experiment.
  * THE NAS COULD NOT BE CHECKED. `/nas_mnt` answers a `stat` on its mount
    point and then `ls` returns "No such device": a stale NFS mount. This
    is exactly what hung a session earlier today; `tools/nas_guard.sh`
    returned instead of hanging, which is what it was written for. Whether
    the source plates are on the NAS is unknown until it is remounted.

WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THIS ITEM. The wrapper, the object head and the merge
and artifact options need none of this and can be built now. THE PV COUNT
HEADS CANNOT BE TRAINED until the three-channel crops exist, and producing
them needs the original acquisitions of those 556 fields -- or a fresh set
of fields acquired with all three stains. Everything else about the PV head
-- the count-not-class decision, the three modes, the per-channel training
-- is settled and written above.
