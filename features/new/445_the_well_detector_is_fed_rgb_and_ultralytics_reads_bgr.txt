================================================================================
THE WELL DETECTOR IS FED RGB AND ULTRALYTICS READS IT AS BGR
================================================================================

Status:    DONE 2026-09-20. Measured against published ground truth, fixed at
           detect_wells' own door, pinned by a test, and the sibling caller
           that was already converting was corrected in the same pass. FOUND
           2026-09-19 by unit 424a, which hit the same bug in its own harness,
           fixed it there, and measured the size of it.
           READ THE TRAILING NOTE: the premise that the v4 test split is
           plate photographs turned out to be false, and that changes what
           the measurement below is worth, not the verdict.
Found by:  424 (features/future/424_plaques_from_published_papers.txt), whose
           transfer measurement was voided by it.
Owner:     unassigned

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ultralytics decodes a file path with OpenCV, so it trains and infers in BGR.
Handed an ``H x W x 3`` numpy array instead, it does NOT convert: it assumes
the caller has already put the channels in BGR order. Every image these
detectors were trained on reached them through the file-path route, so BGR is
the order they learned.

spacr feeds them RGB.

  spacr/submodules.py, the plate-splitting pass:

      image = cellpose.io.imread(path)          # RGB for a colour image
      wells = detect_wells(image, weights, ...)

  spacr/plaque.py, detect_wells:

      :param image: the field, as an array the detector can read.

  -- which is the whole of what the signature says about channel order.

So every plate photograph spaCR splits into wells is shown to the detector
with red and blue exchanged. Crystal violet is violet; the swap is not
harmless.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW BIG IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Measured 2026-09-19 on 424a's corpus of 182 published figures, calling the
real ``spacr.plaque.detect_wells`` both ways over the same files, CPU, conf
0.25, imgsz 640, min_axis_ratio 0:

                                boxes on the    boxes on the    boxes on the
                                14 figures      10 figures      158 figures
                                holding wells   holding crops   holding neither

  v1 (yolo_welldetect_v3) RGB        136              17              23
  v1                      BGR        333              16              22
  v2 (yolo_welldetect_v4) RGB         13              15              13
  v2                      BGR         61              72              15

The detector finds two to five times as much with the channels the right way
round, and the false-box count barely moves -- so this is recall being thrown
away, not a threshold being loosened.

THAT BGR IS THE RIGHT ORDER WAS CHECKED, NOT ASSUMED. Over all 182 figures,
passing the FILE PATH to ultralytics and passing a BGR array give identical
box counts -- 182 of 182 agree -- and RGB differs. The file path is how every
training image reached these models.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The measurement above is on PUBLISHED FIGURES: cropped, rescaled, JPEG'd, and
often not even photographs. The shipped path runs on plate photographs taken
by the user, which is the distribution the detector was trained on and where
a channel swap may matter more or less. NOBODY HAS MEASURED THIS BUG ON A
REAL PLATE PHOTOGRAPH. That is the first thing to do, and it is why 424a did
not simply change the line.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MEASURE IT ON PLATE PHOTOGRAPHS. The v4 test split is 129 images with 297
   boxes and is published
   (einarolafsson/toxoplasma-plaque-well-detector-yolo26, training/split.csv).
   Score detect_wells over it both ways. If BGR wins there too, the fix is
   worth making; the size of the win is what the release note has to say.

2. DECIDE WHERE THE CONVERSION LIVES. Two defensible answers:
   * detect_wells takes the array in BGR and SAYS SO in its signature, and
     every caller converts. Honest, and it pushes the problem onto callers.
   * detect_wells takes RGB, says so, and converts internally. Fewer places to
     get it wrong, and it matches what every other spaCR entry point expects,
     since cellpose.io.imread is the house reader.
   The second is probably right for this codebase, but it is a public
   signature change either way and needs the docstring to state the order.
   Whichever is chosen, ``:param image:`` must name a channel order: the
   current wording is what let this sit unnoticed.

3. WARN ABOUT EXISTING RESULTS. Anyone who has split plates with spaCR has
   done it through the swap, and THE DANGEROUS CASE IS PARTIAL DETECTION, not
   total failure. An image where nothing is found is at least loud: it logs
   "no wells detected in %s" and is skipped. An image where the swap costs
   four wells out of twelve is silent -- the plate is split into eight crops,
   eight rows are written, and nothing says the other four existed. On the
   figure corpus the swap cost v1 roughly 60 % of its boxes, so partial is the
   normal case, not the edge one. This needs a release note, not a silent fix.

   (While in there: the "no wells detected" branch logs "it is passed through
   whole" and then ``continue``s, so the image is NOT passed through -- it
   contributes no crop at all unless every image fails and the function falls
   back to ``src``. Separate bug, same function, worth fixing in the same
   pass.)

4. CHECK THE OTHER DETECTORS. Anything else in spaCR that hands ultralytics a
   numpy array has the same bug. detect_wells is the only caller found on
   2026-09-19, but the question is about the pattern, not the function.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT 424a ALREADY DID
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

tools/measure_plaque_detector_transfer.py converts to BGR before calling
detect_wells (``_detector_input``), records ``channel_order`` in its
detections file, and has a test pinning the conversion. Its own published
measurement was withdrawn; see 424's trailing note. Nothing under spacr/ was
changed.

================================================================================
2026-09-20 -- MEASURED AGAINST GROUND TRUTH, AND FIXED
================================================================================

THE PREMISE OF THIS FILE WAS WRONG, AND IT IS WORTH SAYING FIRST. The plan
above was to close the distribution gap by scoring the v4 test split, "129
images with 297 boxes", on the grounds that it is plate photographs and the
182 published figures were not. It is not. `split.csv` in
einarolafsson/toxoplasma-plaque-well-detector-dataset labels every test row
`pmc_accept` (45 images, 297 boxes) or `pmc_negative` (84 images, no box):
the test split is 129 PUBLISHED FIGURES.

Nor is the rest of it plate photographs. All 1,453 rows -- train, val and
test, including the 518 carried over from v3's own corpus under the source
`detector_v3` -- carry published-figure filenames (`..._Fig4_HTML.png`,
`ppat.1009.g003.png`, `neg__PMC7182120__f005-...jpg`); exactly two of the
1,453 are not obviously figure-shaped, and both are journal artifacts as
well. THERE IS NO LABELLED PLATE PHOTOGRAPH ANYWHERE IN THE PUBLISHED DATA,
and none on this machine. The model zoo row for v1 describes its training set
as "whole-plate and multi-well crystal violet images", which is what these
figures DEPICT, and that wording is what made this file expect photographs.

So the gap this file names cannot be closed by measuring; it is closed by
noticing that it is not the gap it looked like. Published figures are not a
second distribution the detector must transfer to -- THEY ARE THE
DISTRIBUTION IT WAS TRAINED ON. The measurement below is therefore in-domain,
which is the strongest place to establish which channel order a model learned,
and the remaining question about real plate photographs is about the SIZE of
the loss, not its direction. Its direction does not depend on the pixels at
all: which order ultralytics reads an array in is a property of its loader.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`spacr.plaque.detect_wells` scored over the published test split -- 129
images, 297 ground-truth boxes -- three ways per model. CPU, conf floor
0.001, imgsz 640, min_axis_ratio 0, matching at IoU 0.5, P/R quoted at the
shipped confidence default of 0.25. Boxes are the detections surviving 0.25.

                              mAP50   mAP50-95       P       R      F1  boxes
  v1 (yolo_welldetect_v3)
    FILE PATH (reference)    0.8888     0.7696  0.6366  0.9024  0.7465    421
    BGR array                0.8888     0.7696  0.6366  0.9024  0.7465    421
    RGB array (what shipped) 0.5877     0.5199  0.5815  0.6128  0.5967    313
  v2 (yolo_welldetect_v4)
    FILE PATH (reference)    0.9480     0.8443  0.8367  0.9663  0.8969    343
    BGR array                0.9480     0.8443  0.8367  0.9663  0.8969    343
    RGB array (what shipped) 0.6972     0.6260  0.6984  0.2963  0.4161    126

BGR WINS, AND NOT NARROWLY. The file-path route and the BGR array agree to
four decimal places on every measure for both models; RGB does not. v2 keeps
29.6% of the wells in RGB against 96.6% -- it loses TWO THIRDS OF EVERY
PLATE -- and v1 loses just under a third of its recall. 424a's figure-corpus
finding that "this is recall being thrown away, not a threshold being
loosened" holds against ground truth: precision barely moves for v1
(0.6366 -> 0.5815) and IMPROVES for v2 while recall collapses, which is the
signature of finding less, not of finding worse.

THE REFERENCE ROUTE IS TIED TO THE TRAINING ROUTE RATHER THAN ASSUMED. The
file-path numbers above reproduce the detector repository's own published
qc/test_metrics.json -- v2 0.9457 / 0.8341 and v1 0.8838 / 0.7630 against
0.9480 / 0.8443 and 0.8888 / 0.7696 here, the small differences being this
harness's greedy matcher and its conf floor. Those published metrics were
computed by ultralytics' own validator over file paths. So the route this
file calls the reference IS the route the models were trained and scored in.

AND THE FIX WAS CONFIRMED END TO END on the same 129 images: after the change
below, handing detect_wells an RGB array -- which is exactly what split_wells
does -- scores 0.9480 / 0.8443 for v2 and 0.8888 / 0.7696 for v1, identical
to the file path, and handing it a pre-swapped BGR array is now the degraded
one. The two rows changed places, which is the fix doing precisely one thing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE CONVERSION IS AT detect_wells' OWN DOOR, the second of the two options
above. `spacr/plaque.py` gains `_to_detector_channel_order`, called on the
way into `model.predict`, and `:param image:` now NAMES A CHANNEL ORDER --
RGB, what cellpose.io.imread returns -- which the old wording, "an array the
detector can read", did not. Only an H x W x 3 array is converted: a 2-D
greyscale array has no order to get wrong, reversing the last axis of an
H x W x 4 array would move alpha to the front, and a file path is
ultralytics' own input. `spacr/submodules.py` needed no change, which was
the point of putting it there.

EVERY CALLER WAS CHECKED. `model.predict` in detect_wells is the ONLY call
into ultralytics in the package -- every other `.predict` under spacr/ is
scikit-learn -- so there is one door and it is now right. Two callers hand it
an array: `spacr/submodules.py` (split_wells), which was the bug and is now
correct unchanged; and `tools/measure_plaque_detector_transfer.py`, which
was ALREADY converting in its own `_detector_input` and would have converted
twice, putting 424a's original bug back with nothing visible to say so. Its
conversion is removed, its module docstring says why it must not come back,
and its test now FAILS if a swap reappears in that file.

THE SIBLING BUG IN split_wells IS FIXED TOO. The "no wells detected" branch
logged "it is passed through whole" and then `continue`d. Nothing was passed
through: when some images in a folder split and others did not, the others
produced no crop, no row and no further mention. They are now copied into the
wells folder whole, with no geometry entry, so they are analysed and simply
carry no physical scale -- which `_plaque_scale_for` already returns None for.
The all-or-nothing case is unchanged: with no geometry at all the function
still falls back to `src`.

TESTS THAT PIN IT. `tests/test_plaque_wells_and_scale.py` gains three: the
detector is handed BGR and the caller's array is not mutated; greyscale,
four-channel and file-path inputs pass through untouched; and the file-path
route reaches ultralytics unchanged. The first was checked to FAIL when the
conversion is removed. `tests/test_submodules_plaque_well_integration.py`
gains two: split_wells hands both detect_wells and crop_well the reader's RGB
and converts nothing itself, and an image with no detected well still reaches
the analysis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT USERS OF EARLIER VERSIONS NEED TO BE TOLD -- RELEASE NOTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANYONE WHO SPLIT PLATES WITH `well_detection` ENABLED BEFORE THIS FIX RAN THE
DETECTOR WITH RED AND BLUE EXCHANGED, and their well crops are probably
missing wells. THE DANGEROUS CASE IS PARTIAL DETECTION, not total failure. A
plate where nothing was found was at least loud; a plate where four wells out
of twelve were lost was silent -- eight crops, eight rows, and nothing saying
the other four existed. On the ground-truth split above that is the normal
case: v2 recovered 29.6% of boxes and v1 61.3%, so a run made with the
default detector should be assumed to have lost most of its wells rather than
a few.

WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT: re-run the split. It is cheap -- the detector is a 5 MB
yolo26n and 129 images take seconds on a CPU -- and nothing downstream needs
redoing except what that run produced. A previous run cannot be repaired from
its outputs, because a well that was never detected left no trace to repair.

TWO OTHER THINGS CHANGED FOR USERS, both making a run report more rather than
less: an image the detector finds nothing in now appears in the wells folder
whole instead of vanishing, so a folder that used to yield eight rows may now
yield nine; and the summary line names how many images were passed through
that way.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS STILL NOT MEASURED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

REAL PLATE PHOTOGRAPHS, and they now look like the more interesting question
rather than a loose end of this one. The detectors have apparently never been
trained on or scored against a photograph of a plate taken by a user -- only
on figures of such plates, which are cropped, rescaled and JPEG'd. That is a
transfer question about the DETECTOR, not about this bug, and it is worth its
own item: what these weights do on the images spaCR is actually pointed at is
unknown, in either channel order. What is settled is that whatever they do,
they now do it in the order they were trained in.
