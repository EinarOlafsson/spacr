--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MEASUREMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`SETTING_API_TARGETS` maps a setting key to the module and symbol that reads
it, and the Qt panels use it to point a setting's help at the right API page.
Of its rows, 77 name a key that is NOT in `expected_types`:

    has a tooltip but no type      21   blue_channel, columnID,
                                        control_sgrnas, csv
    known local-scope family       17   hit_bootstrap, hit_direction,
                                        hit_effect, hit_fdr
    bare word                       4   inputs, layout, measure, overwrite
    other                          35   barcode_qc, cellpose_all,
                                        cellpose_masks, collision_max_distance

THE SEVENTEEN ARE PROBABLY FINE AND ARE THE KEY TO READING THE REST.
`build_setting_consumer_map.py` documents its own reason for unioning the EN
i18n catalog into its vocabulary: `hit_investigation_default_settings` builds
a `tips` dict INSIDE the function body, so seventeen `hit_*` settings appear in
a panel and in no module-scope table. They are real settings that
`expected_types` cannot see. So "absent from expected_types" does NOT mean
"does not exist", and anyone auditing this list by deletion will remove real
settings.

THE TWENTY-ONE ARE THE INTERESTING ONES. A key with a TOOLTIP but no
`expected_types` entry is half-declared: it has prose a user can read and no
type the loader can check. Either the type is missing or the tooltip outlived
the setting, and the two are opposite defects with opposite fixes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS, AND WHY IT IS NOT URGENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A row naming a dead key is a settings-help link pointing at a page for
something that is not there. It is not a crash and no user has reported it.
But 364's whole finding was that a rename strands every generated table keyed
by setting name, and four settings went missing from the API exactly that way
-- so a standing population of 77 unexplained rows is the same class of thing,
sitting unexamined.

  IT IS ALSO A BLIND SPOT IN A CHECK. Anyone measuring "did my change strand a
  row?" has to subtract this 77 first, which is what happened on 391: the
  count went 90 -> 77 and the useful signal was the 13, not the total. A
  baseline nobody has explained makes every future measurement of that kind
  harder to read.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * SPLIT THE 77 BY CAUSE before fixing anything. Declared-elsewhere (the
    `hit_*` shape) is not a defect. Tooltip-without-type is. A key nothing
    reads is a third thing.
  * FOR THE TOOLTIP-WITHOUT-TYPE TWENTY-ONE, decide per key whether the type
    is missing or the tooltip is stale -- and note that adding a type is the
    safer error, since `check_settings` then validates what a user typed.
  * THEN PIN THE REMAINDER. Whatever cannot be explained should be a named,
    committed list with a reason each, so the next person measuring stranded
    rows can subtract a set rather than a number.

DO NOT SIMPLY DELETE ROWS TO MAKE THE COUNT ZERO. The seventeen `hit_*` rows
are the proof that the count is not the defect.

2026-09-13 -- SPLIT BY CAUSE, AND THE ANSWER IS THAT 76 OF THE 77 ARE REAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The item asks for the 77 to be split by cause before anything is fixed. Done,
and the split does not look like the one guessed above.

THE GUESS WAS WRONG ABOUT THE TOOLTIPS. This file predicts "21 have a tooltip
but no type" and calls those the interesting ones. In fact ALL 77 have a
tooltip -- and on reflection that is forced rather than surprising:
`build_setting_consumer_map.py` unions the EN catalog's `SETTING_TOOLTIPS`
into its vocabulary, so a row can only exist if a tooltip exists. Tooltip
presence cannot discriminate anything here. The 21/17/4/35 breakdown above was
a guess at the shape and should not be used.

THE DISCRIMINATOR THAT WORKS is whether anything reads the key. Measured over
558 files, EXCLUDING the three tables that define these keys by construction
(`setting_api_targets.py`, `setting_consumers.json`, the catalogs) -- without
that exclusion every key trivially matches itself and the answer comes back
77/77, which is how I first measured it and it was meaningless:

    read elsewhere in spacr/      76
    not read anywhere              1     umap.reduction_method

So the 77 is NOT a population of rows naming dead settings. It is 76 real
settings that are declared somewhere `expected_types` cannot see -- the
`hit_*` shape generalised -- plus one anomaly.

THE ONE ANOMALY, and it is benign. `umap.reduction_method` is the only dotted
key among all 836 rows. The dot is not damage: `umap.metric` and
`umap.n_neighbors` sit beside it in the EN catalog, so screen-scoped dotted
keys are a real convention. What is odd is that those two siblings have NO row
in `SETTING_API_TARGETS` and this one does, and that its row is degenerate --
`('spacr.validate', '', False)`: empty symbol, not rendered. Meanwhile the bare
`reduction_method` is declared, is read, and has its own correct row pointing
at `spacr.core.generate_image_umap`. So the dotted row is a duplicate that
points nowhere and renders nothing.

TWO MORE ARE WORTH A SECOND LOOK but are not claimed as defects. Of the ten
bare single-word keys, `layout` and `measure` are read as a setting
(`settings[...]`, `.get`, `.setdefault`) in ZERO files, where the other eight
are read that way in at least one. That test is deliberately narrow and only
recognises four container names, so a key read some other way would look dead
to it. `measure` in particular appears as a plain string in 44 files, which is
exactly the noise this item warns about.

WHAT THIS CHANGES. The "subtract a set rather than a number" advice above is
still right, but the set is smaller and duller than expected: 76 explained,
1 duplicate, 2 to confirm. Nobody auditing a future stranded-row count needs
to chase 77 unexplained rows, because 77 was never the number of unexplained
rows -- it was the number of rows whose declaration `expected_types` cannot
see, and that is a property of the measurement, not of the table.

NOTHING WAS DELETED, per the warning at the head of this file. The duplicate
row is recorded here rather than removed, because removing it is a change to a
generated table and belongs with the generator's next pass.

2026-09-13 -- THIS IS HANDOFF TRAP 3c, COUNTED FROM THE OTHER END
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`HANDOFF.md` trap 3c says "`spacr.settings.tooltips` is NOT complete on
import": six pipelines call `register_defaults` from their own module body, so
a key is undocumented until that module is imported. Measured 2026-09-13,
thirteen keys arrive late:

    collision_max_distance  exclude_starved_wells  min_reads_per_well
    on_error  on_error_attempts  on_error_backoff  position_effect_ratio
    qc_data  starved_read_fraction  sweep_points  sweep_span
    target_grnas_per_well  target_statistic

`collision_max_distance` and `exclude_starved_wells` are in THIS item's 77.

So the two are one fact: a setting registered from inside a module body is
invisible to any tool that reads a module-scope table. Trap 3c is that fact
seen by a tool reading the tooltip dict; the 77 is the same fact seen by a
tool reading `expected_types`. They were filed days apart as separate things.

THAT STRENGTHENS THE CONCLUSION ABOVE rather than changing it. The 77 is a
property of the measurement, and here is the mechanism spelled out in a second
place: not a population of dead rows, a population of live settings declared
where the comparison vocabulary cannot see them. Anyone tempted to "clean up"
this list should read trap 3c first.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The entry above left `layout` and `measure` as "worth a second look but not
claimed as defects", on the grounds that the test which called them unread

    layout    spacr/convert.py:2176         resolved.get('layout')

THE CONTAINER IS CALLED `resolved`, NOT `settings`. Each of those functions

so the dictionary being read IS the settings dictionary; it simply has a

greps for `settings[...]`, `settings.get(...)` and `settings.setdefault(...)`
sees none of it.

HOW BIG THE BLIND SPOT IS, measured rather than guessed:

    modules using `resolved = default_settings(...)`      4
        spacr/convert.py  spacr/external_masks.py
        spacr/align.py    spacr/foreign.py
    reads of the form `resolved.get('<key>')`            84
    DISTINCT SETTING KEYS read only that way             50

  FIFTY KEYS, NOT TWO. Any audit of the form "which settings does nothing
  read?" run against those four container names will report all fifty as
  dead, and four of spaCR's pipeline entry points -- convert, align, foreign
  import and external masks -- are exactly the modules such an audit would be
  run to check.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE ITEM. It is the same lesson as trap 3c and as the 77
itself, arriving for the third time from a third direction: THE MEASUREMENT
VOCABULARY IS THE DEFECT, NOT THE TABLE. First it was `expected_types` not
seeing keys registered inside a function body; then the tooltip dict not
seeing keys registered from a module body; now a grep not seeing keys read out
of a differently-named local. Three instruments, three blind spots, and in all
three cases the first reading was "these settings do not exist".

  THE RULE THAT FALLS OUT OF IT: before reporting that a setting is unread,
  say which containers the search recognised. A count of unread settings
  without that sentence attached is not a measurement, it is a property of
  the grep.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A sharper instance of this item's subject, and an attempt that FAILED, written
down so the next one starts further along.

`z_handling` is declared once, by `external_masks.register_defaults`, and read
by TWO modules with different contracts:

    spacr.convert.convert_folder   accepts keep / max / first, DEFAULT 'keep'
    spacr.external_masks           rejects 'keep',             DEFAULT 'max'

`register_defaults` raises if a second module registers differing text for one
key, so the single shared tooltip is whatever the first registrar wrote -- and
it describes External Masks alone. A reader of Format Convert is told the
default is 'max' when it is 'keep', and is never told 'keep' exists.

  IT IS THE 77's SHAPE ONE STEP ON. This item is about rows whose declaration
  `expected_types` cannot see. Here the declaration is visible and SINGULAR
  where the truth is PLURAL: one string cannot be the help for two contracts,
  and the table has no way to say so.

is the obvious fix and was attempted three times:

    5 sentences, 6 quoted literals   zh_CN, hi, is refused   ("lack target script")
    6 sentences, 5 quoted literals   zh_CN, hi, is refused
    3 sentences, 3 quoted literals   zh_CN took it; hi, is refused

Three nine-language repair passes, several GPU-hours, and the third version has
the SAME literal density as the original that translates cleanly -- so length
and quoting are not the variable. The hypothesis that "Format Convert" and
"External Masks" are protected product tokens was tested and is false; neither
is in `_PROTECTED_TERMS`.

IT ALSO INVALIDATES REVIEWED WORK. Three human-reviewed records are pinned to
the old source hash -- ko and is in `2026-08-27-external-masks-tooltips.json`,
hi inside `2026-09-08-release-gaps.json` -- and a review of the previous
sentence is not a review of a new one. Any wording change retires all three.

REVERTED. The original text is restored byte-for-byte, confirmed by recomputing
its sha256 as `3ed5e5258...`, which is exactly what the reviewed records carry.
All nine locales translate it. The three reviewed records are back.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY FIX IT: a per-app tooltip override, so a module can
register help scoped to its own app key rather than to the setting name. That
is a mechanism -- it touches `register_defaults`, the settings model and the Qt
panels -- and it is the honest size of this problem. Every attempt to solve it
with words is an attempt to make one sentence true of two different contracts.

  DO NOT RE-ATTEMPT THE PROSE. The measurements above are the evidence that it
