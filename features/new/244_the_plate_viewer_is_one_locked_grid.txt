================================================================================
THE PLATE VIEWER: ONE LOCKED GRID, SQUARE WELLS, AND DRAG TO SELECT
================================================================================

Status:    done, 2026-08-25. All three asks and the fourth defect are
           built and measured; a fifth, found while driving the gesture,
           is fixed with them. The one thing left open -- a drag at the
           scroll area's edge did not scroll -- is built 2026-09-25 (last
           entry). Reported again 2026-08-25, having been
           asked for once before and never built -- "a long time ago i
           told you that ... none of this is true".
Owner:     spacr/qt/widgets/plate_map_picker.py, and every screen that
           offers a plate map (the regression control-well pickers are
           where it was met). Every one of those screens opens the same
           dialog through `AppScreen.pick_wells_for`, so the widget is
           the only place any of this had to be built.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THREE ASKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. EVERY PART OF THE PLATE IS LOCKED TO EVERY OTHER PART.

   "all elements of the plate locked to each other (wells and column and
   row axis labels)."

   The row letters, the column numbers and the wells are one grid, not
   three things that happen to be near each other. Resize the window and
   they move together; scroll and they scroll together; a column label
   sits over its column at every size, and a row letter beside its row.

   The failure this is aimed at is the ordinary one: labels laid out in
   their own strip drift out of register with the wells as soon as the
   two are given different amounts of space.

2. A WELL IS A SQUARE.

   "the wells should be squares not rectangles."

   A plate well is round in life and square on a map. Stretched into a
   rectangle it reads as a grid of something else, and the eye stops
   being able to count across it. The square survives every window size:
   the grid takes the space it needs to keep them square and leaves the
   rest, rather than dividing whatever it is given.

3. CLICK AND DRAG SELECTS A RECTANGLE.

   "the user should be able to click and drag to select not only click."

   Press on a well, move, release: every well the pointer swept is
   selected. Selecting a block of a plate is the common case -- a whole
   column of controls, a quadrant, four wells in a corner -- and clicking
   ninety-six times is not a way to do it.

   NOTE, from the coverage pass: the drag gesture was WRITTEN and is
   dead. `_Well._picker` walks exactly two parents to find the picker,
   and `area.setWidget(self._holder)` reparents the holder into the
   scroll area's VIEWPORT, so the walk lands on the viewport instead.
   Every `hasattr(picker, "begin_drag")` guard is then False and a press
   selects nothing. Pinned by
   `tests/qt/test_cov_w2_6_plate_map_picker.py`. The walk has to find the
   PlateMapPicker ancestor rather than counting steps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A FOURTH THING, FOUND WITH THEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`PlateMapPicker.set_selection` blocks the well's signals around
`setChecked`, so the `toggled -> _paint` connection never fires: a
programmatically chosen well -- which includes the picker's own starting
value and everything kept across a layout change -- is checked and still
painted EMPTY. It has to repaint after setting the state.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Driven, not eyeballed, at more than one window size:

* a column label's centre x equals its column's centre x, and a row
  label's centre y equals its row's centre y, at 700 px wide and at
  1400 px wide;
* every well's width equals its height, at both sizes;
* press on B2, move to D5, release -> exactly the twelve wells of that
  rectangle are selected, and the ones outside it are not;
* `set_selection(["A01"])` leaves A01 painted as selected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS TRUE NOW, MEASURED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The wells, the row letters and the column numbers are items of ONE
`QGridLayout`, each centred in its cell, carried by one holder inside the
scroll area. The wells are a fixed 22 px square, and the spare space goes
to an empty trailing row and column rather than being shared out among the
cells -- which is what used to pull the labels off the wells they name.

Driven at 700 px and at 1400 px on a 96-well plate, geometry read back off
the widgets:

* every column label's centre x equals its column's centre x, and every
  row letter's centre y equals its row's centre y, at both widths, and the
  numbers are IDENTICAL at the two sizes (column 1 at x=55, column 2 at
  x=79, ... row A at y=78, row B at y=102): 700 px of extra window moves
  nothing;
* every well measures 22 x 22 at both widths -- and at all six layouts
  (6, 12, 24, 96, 384, 1536) across widths from 400 px to 2000 px, with no
  label off its well anywhere in that sweep;
* press on B2, move, release on D5 selects exactly B02..D05, twelve wells,
  with nothing outside the rectangle checked, at both widths;
* `set_selection(["A01"])` leaves A01 both checked and painted CHOSEN.

The drag reaches the dialog because `_Well._picker` walks its ANCESTRY for
a `PlateMapPicker` instead of counting two parents; a scroll area reparents
the holder into its own viewport, so the fixed walk landed there and every
`hasattr(picker, "begin_drag")` guard read False.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A FIFTH THING, FOUND DRIVING THE GESTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A CLICK THAT WOBBLED LANDED NOTHING. No hand holds still, so a real click
delivers a move event or two before the release. `drag_to` treated any
move as a drag: it cleared the selection, drew the one-well rectangle on
the pressed well, and then -- because the pointer had not left that well --
reported no drag, so the release fell through to the button's own toggle
and turned the well straight back off. One pixel of travel therefore
emptied the whole plate and chose nothing. A move that never leaves the
well it was pressed on is no longer a drag, and once the pointer HAS left
that well the gesture stays a drag, so sweeping out and back to the anchor
ends with the anchor chosen rather than clicked off again.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STILL OPEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nothing that blocks the ask. One thing was noticed and not built: a drag
that reaches the edge of the scroll area does not scroll the plate, so on
a 1536-well map in a narrow window the rectangle can only span the wells
already in view. Selecting past the edge means scrolling first.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-09-25 -- A DRAG THAT REACHES THE EDGE SCROLLS THE PLATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The "still open" item above. `PlateMapPicker` now keeps its scroll area and
a 30 ms QTimer. `drag_to` works out, from the pointer's global position, how
deep it is into a one-well-wide band inside each viewport edge (or past the
edge): the step grows with that depth, 2 px at the band's inner line up to
AUTOSCROLL_MAX_STEP (40 px) far past the edge, and is zero in a direction
the scroll bar cannot move. A non-zero step starts the timer; each tick
scrolls and redraws the rectangle to the well now at the edge -- because a
pointer held still sends no move events, which is exactly when the scroll
has to keep going. Moving back inside the band, releasing, or reaching the
end of the scroll range stops it.

ALSO FIXED ON THE WAY: past the edge the pointer is over wells the viewport
clips, and `well_at` found them anyway, so a fast drag out of a narrow
window selected wells nobody could see. The lookup is now clamped to the
visible viewport (only while the dialog is on screen), stepping inward over
the 2 px spacing between wells, so the rectangle ends on the last visible
well and only the scroll extends it.

Tests (tests/qt/test_the_plate_viewer_is_one_locked_grid.py, 8 new, on a
1536-well map in a 500x400 window): holding past the right edge scrolls and
extends the columns while staying on one row; below the bottom scrolls rows;
the rectangle stops at the last well on screen; a drag away from the edges,
moving back inside, and no press all leave the timer off; the real timer
scrolls with the pointer held still; a plate that fits never scrolls. File
44 passed; with test_the_plate_map_is_squares_you_drag_across,
test_cov_w2_6_plate_map_picker and test_both_plates_are_locked_and_keep_
what_was_chosen, all pass.
