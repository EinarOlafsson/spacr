which calls `_on_invert` (6540) and applies `mask_engine.invert_mask`:

    out = np.where(mask > 0, 0, 1).astype(mask.dtype)

So it is foreground/background flipped and relabelled. On an ordinary field

definition and it is almost certainly NOT what the maintainer means by
invert, which is the first thing to settle. A single field-sized label can
also look like nothing happened at all: it has no visible outline of its own

draws it as one flat region.

WHAT WAS BUILT IS THE COMPLEMENT, NOT THE RECIPROCAL, AND HERE IS WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------

`mask_engine.invert_intensity` returns `dtype_max - value` (and, for float
data, `min + max - value` over the array's own range, which is stated in the
docstring because a float image has no dtype maximum worth speaking of).

The reciprocal was CONSIDERED AND NOT BUILT. It is written down here so that
nobody derives it again from the same sentence:

  * `dtype_max - value` is what an image viewer means by Invert, so the
    picture that comes back is the one he is picturing when he asks for it.
  * It is EXACTLY REVERSIBLE on every integer and boolean dtype -- pressing
    Invert twice returns the identical array. That is what makes it safe to
    leave switched on while curating, and it is asserted by comparing
    arrays, not by eye:
    tests/qt/test_make_masks_inverts_the_picture_and_otsu_grew_options.py
    ::test_inverting_twice_returns_the_identical_array, over uint8, uint16,
    int16 and bool, and ::test_invert_image_changes_the_picture_and_puts_it
    _back, which compares the CANVAS PIXMAP's bytes before and after two
    presses. A float field rounds twice and comes back within one unit in
    the last place of `min + max` -- measured at 0.002 in float32 and 4e-12
    in float64 over 0..65535, against an interval of one count -- which the
    docstring says rather than claiming an exactness the arithmetic does not
    have. Every field this editor opens is an integer dtype.
  * `1/value` divides by zero on every background pixel, and plenty of
    fields have a true zero background. It also squashes the bright end
    non-linearly, so two objects a thousand counts apart come back
    indistinguishable while the background explodes.

HE CAN STILL HAVE THE RECIPROCAL AS A SECOND MODE if he wants a log-like
lift of the dim end; it is a reasonable thing to want and it is not this one.
It would go in as a choice beside the switch, not in place of it.

WHAT INVERT DOES NOW
--------------------

  * "Invert image" is a Toggle in the DISPLAY category, beside the two
    contrast percentiles, because that is what it is. On, the canvas paints
    the photographic complement of the field: what was dark is bright.
  * IT IS A VIEW AND ONLY A VIEW. The complement is computed in
    `_MaskCanvas.displayed_source` and nowhere else, so `canvas.image` still
    holds the pixels that were read off disk. Five paths are DRIVEN with
    Invert on and asserted to have seen the original numbers, because a view
    inversion that leaked into what is measured would be worse than the
    defect it fixes -- a curator would be filtering on numbers that are not
    the data:
      - Otsu detect: the same mask either way, and a spy on
        `engine._otsu_instances` catches the array it was handed;
      - the corner readout: the same intensity and the same object mean;
      - the object filter: the same objects dropped;
      - the live magnifier: `build_request().crop` equals the slice of
        `canvas.image`;
      - and `canvas.image` itself is unchanged, asserted array-wise.
  * The status line says so on the way in: "Showing the image inverted --
    dark is bright. Detection, filtering and saving still use the original
    pixels."
  * The complement is cached against the identity of the image, because
    `refresh` runs on every point of a brush stroke.

THE MASK FLIP STAYS, UNDER THE NAME THAT DESCRIBES IT. This file asked for a
deliberate decision and the decision is KEEP: outlining the space between the
cells and wanting the cells is a real thing to do. "Invert mask" is now
"Swap object and background", its tooltip says what it gives you on an
ordinary field AND where the picture invert is, and it reports its own result
-- because the thing that made it look broken is that its result is
invisible: one field-sized object with holes reads as one flat wash. Nothing
on the panel called "Invert" flips the mask any more. `engine.invert_mask`
keeps its name and its behaviour; only its docstring grew.

WHAT 435 ADDED TO OTSU
----------------------

Four controls, each with a tooltip that says what it does TO THE PICTURE, and
each driven by a test that shows the mask change:

  1. CLASSES (2-6). Multi-level Otsu
     (`skimage.filters.threshold_multiotsu`), so a field holding background,
     a dim halo and bright nuclei is cut at the boundary that matters
     instead of at the one compromise level between all three.
  2. FOREGROUND CLASS (0 = dimmest). Which band becomes the objects.
     EXACTLY that band is taken, so choosing a middle one gives the halo
     without the nuclei inside it, which is the point of asking for more
     than two classes -- the three bands of a 3-class split are asserted to
     share no pixel. With more than two classes "Objects are brighter than
     background" is not read: the class number already says which side is
     meant, and the box is greyed out to say so.
  3. LOCAL THRESHOLD, with its LOCAL WINDOW in pixels. Otsu's level found
     separately in a square around every pixel
     (`skimage.filters.rank.otsu`), so a corner the lamp has fallen away
     from is judged against its own corner. The test builds a field with an
     illumination ramp and asserts the dim disc is LOST by one global level
     and FOUND by the local one, and that the window size changes the result.
     An even window is rounded up so the square has a centre.
  4. SHOW HISTOGRAM AND LEVEL. A modeless window with the field's intensity
     histogram and a line at every level the settings cut it at. THE MARKER
     IS THE BUTTON'S OWN LEVEL, not a second opinion: the preview and
     `_otsu_instances` both read `mask_engine._otsu_levels`, and a test
     reproduces the detection's binary image from the marked number on four
     bright/dark/correction combinations. The dark-side level marked is the
     MIRRORED one the detector actually compares against, which is a
     different number from Otsu's own. With the local threshold on the
     caption says the level varies window by window and that the line is the
     whole-field level for reference.

The three whole-field ones are THE DETECT BUTTON'S ONLY, on the precedent
419 part B set with "Drop objects the image border cuts". A 64 px magnifier
box rarely holds three populations, and a window the size of the box is the
box's own threshold, so offering either there would be offering a control
that does nothing -- which is the defect this item exists for.
`_classical_region_labels`, the magnifier's own routine, is untouched, and
`_MODEL_SETTING_FIELDS` did not move, so no cached request key changed
meaning.

NOBODY'S DEFAULTS MOVED. The two-class non-local path was rewritten to read

2026-09-20 (later) -- THE TWO INVERTS BECAME ONE, AND THE ARITHMETIC CHANGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Requested by the maintainer, in these words:

  "in make masks the swap objects and background button should be called
   invert image and the image show should be inverted. invert should
   normalize between 0 and 1 then take 1- pixel value for all pixels. this
   will generate an inverted image hat otsu can work on if there are black
   objects on an image then inverting allows the user to use otsu and
   magnigier. that is tre reason for this buton."

ASKED BEFORE BUILDING, because the screen already had THREE things with
"invert" in them and the request named the wrong one of them. "Swap object
and background" flips a LABEL image; renaming it "Invert image" would have
left two controls with that name and removed the only way to flip a mask.
The maintainer was shown the three and chose: ONE control named "Invert
image" that normalises 0..1, takes 1-v, and drives the display AND the
detectors; "Invert for detection" retired into it; "Swap object and
background" left alone under its own name.

WHY THERE WERE TWO, AND WHY THEY HAD TO STOP BEING TWO. This item's Invert
drew a negative and promised detection was untouched. 419 point 9's "Invert
for detection" inverted what the detectors read and drew nothing. A curator
could hold either one without the other, and this item's own status line had
to carry a branch that contradicted the other one's banner when both were on
-- that branch is gone. The reason given for the button IS the argument for
merging them: a curator who inverts the picture to see dark objects means
Otsu and the magnifier to see them too.

WHAT IS BUILT.

  mask_engine.invert_normalized    new. 1 - (v - min) / (max - min), refitted
                                   to the input dtype's full range (0..1 for
                                   a float input, because the display hands
                                   the result to normalize_uint16, which
                                   reads np.iinfo and raises on a float).
                                   A flat field inverts to a flat bright one.
  make_masks._MaskCanvas.displayed_source   now calls it, not invert_intensity.
  make_masks._detector_image and the magnifier's region_for
                                   now call it, not invert_for_detection.
  make_masks._cp_invert            IS self._invert_display -- the same widget
                                   object under its old name, so the eleven
                                   places that read _cp_invert keep working
                                   and no second Toggle can disagree with it.
                                   The Object detection card now carries a
                                   line saying where the switch went.
  invert_intensity, invert_for_detection
                                   kept, unused by this screen, for callers
                                   outside it.

WHY NORMALISING FIRST IS THE POINT. Item 417's Otsu correction is a
MULTIPLIER on an absolute level. The dtype complement moved a 12-bit field
into the top sixteenth of uint16, where a correction of 0.8 asks for a cut
below every pixel present and 1.3 for one above them all -- the measurement
is in invert_for_detection's docstring. Normalising to the field's own range
first puts every field on the same span before the multiplier lands, so one
correction value means the same thing on the next image.

WHAT IS GIVEN UP, said rather than discovered later: this is no longer
exactly reversible on the original numbers. The dtype complement was, and
that was one of the three reasons this item chose it. It does not matter
here because the canvas keeps the untouched array and re-derives the negative
-- nothing measured, filtered or saved ever sees it -- but a caller that
inverts and keeps only the result has lost where the field sat.

2026-09-20 -- REOPENED BY THE MAINTAINER: THE READOUT DID NOT FOLLOW THE PICTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reported in his own words, against a file that said DONE:

    "i told you how i wanted the invert button to be called invert and for
    the inversion to actually invert the image so black objects become
    white and withe become black. i.e. high intensity becomes low and low
    becomes high. i can see that this is not the case because the hover
    allows me th see the intensities and they dont seem to change at all
    when i press invert, so it is not done"

HE WAS RIGHT, AND THE FILE SAID SO ON PURPOSE. `_on_invert_display`'s own
docstring listed "the hover readout" among the things that still read the
loaded pixels. That was a deliberate decision, and it was the wrong one --
not because the reasoning was bad but because of what the readout IS. It is
the only instrument on the screen that reports a number per pixel, so it is
what a curator checks an inversion WITH. A readout that does not move under
an inverted picture does not read as "the raw value, helpfully preserved";
it reads as a switch that did nothing.

WHAT THE PICTURE WAS ALREADY DOING, checked before changing anything: the
canvas drew `invert_normalized(image)` and did invert. The defect was
entirely in what the readout reported.

THE FIX, AND WHY IT IS A SPLIT RATHER THAN A SWITCH. Two numbers are shown
and they serve different masters:

    the pixel intensity    follows the PICTURE      says "(inverted)"
    the object mean        follows the FILTER       says "(as loaded)"

The mean is documented as the number a Filter bound is typed from, and the
Filter reads the loaded pixels; inverting it as well would have broken that
silently, which is a second wrong answer rather than a fix. Each now says
which it is, so the two cannot be mistaken for each other.

UNCHANGED, and re-asserted by a test beside the change: `canvas.image` is
never touched, so what is measured and what is saved are still the numbers
the microscope wrote.

TWO TESTS THAT ASSERTED THE OLD DECISION WERE REWRITTEN RATHER THAN DELETED,
each carrying why:

  * `test_the_readout_reports_the_intensity_on_disk_while_invert_is_on` is
    now `test_the_readout_splits_the_pixel_from_the_object_mean_while_
    inverted`. Its old reason -- "a number read off the picture is typed
    into the filter boxes" -- is true of the mean and was wrong about the
    pixel.
  * Item 419 point 9's `test_the_readout_and_the_filter_keep_the_fields_
    real_values` froze the WHOLE readout; it is narrowed to the filter,
    which is what point 9 actually needs.
