WHAT WAS ADDED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`_thresholds` and `_aggregation`.

THE FRACTION THRESHOLD IS THE ONE THAT QUIETLY DELETES A SCREEN, and it is a
constant by default. A constant cannot know how crowded a well is: with four
guides per well a 2% cut removes nothing, and with four hundred it removes
almost everything, because the typical guide is then a quarter of one per
cent.

So the advice is measured against the screen's OWN fraction distribution --
which `read_the_counts` computed and threw away -- and where the usual
default would discard most of the library it says so WITH THE NUMBER:

    "the usual 0.02 would keep only 0.8% of the guide-in-well pairs here,
     because the typical guide is 0.023% of its well"

AND IT SAYS WHAT IT CANNOT DO. A threshold is a noise floor, and nothing in
a count table alone measures noise. Control wells of known composition do,
directly, and `spacr.read_background` computes it from them (instruction
207). The advice names that as the real answer and its own proposal as a
value chosen to keep the library rather than to remove noise.

`min_n` comes from the measured wells per guide; `min_cell_count` from the
objects per well; `agg_type` from the measured SKEW of the response, because
a mean over cells is dragged by the tail and on a classification score the
tail is where the interesting cells are.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* All six are proposed, each with a reason that names a measured quantity.
* The fraction advice states what the default would cost THIS screen.
* It names the control-well measurement as the better route rather than
  presenting its own proposal as noise-derived.
* Nothing is proposed that the run would refuse -- `advise_that_runs` still
  filters the whole proposal.
