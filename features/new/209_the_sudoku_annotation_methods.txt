--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. SUDOKU -- WHAT WAS ASKED FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "we come up with a sofisticated exclude, include criterion across wells,
     where we anchor the approach to the highest scoring cells in the
     annotated wells (the user may choose several high scoring guides), then
     use the fractions, clssification scores, and cell measurements for a
     suduku like process of elimination and affirmation where cells get a
     score that mapps if they are likely to not be one of the chosen top
     scoring guides, and a seperate score for if they are likely to be one of
     the top scoring guides."

The essential parts, none of which may be quietly dropped:

  * ANCHORS. The highest scoring cells in the annotated wells are taken as
    near-certain examples. The user chooses which guides anchor it, and may
    choose several.
  * ACROSS WELLS, not within one. This is what makes it sudoku rather than
    the per-well posterior that already exists: what a guide's cells look
    like is learned from every well the guide is in, and applied in each.
  * THREE SOURCES OF EVIDENCE: the sequencing fractions, the classification
    scores, and the cell measurements.
  * ELIMINATION AND AFFIRMATION, and these are SEPARATE. "cells get a score
    that mapps if they are likely to not be one of the chosen top scoring
    guides, and a seperate score for if they are likely to be one".

TWO SCORES, NOT ONE, and this is the part most likely to be lost in
implementation because one number is easier. It must not be: "probably not
this guide" and "probably this guide" are different claims and a cell can be
low on both. A cell that is unlike the anchors AND unlike everything else is
exactly the cell a single score hides, and it is the interesting one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. SUDOKU ALL -- THE SEQUENTIAL FORM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "the method starts with the highest scoring + p value guide and performs
     the suduku method across all wells that have the guide, and then excludes
     the cells annotated with that guide and goes to the next guide with the
     now highest cloassification score + p value, and so on untill the method
     reaches a reasonable threshold of unsertainty where there is no way to
     know if cell is one gRNA or another, where it is a coin flip."

Greedy, in confidence order, removing what it has claimed as it goes, and
STOPPING at a stated uncertainty -- not running to exhaustion. The stopping
rule is a first-class part of the method, not an afterthought: "where it is a
coin flip" has to become a number, and that number has to be defensible.

THE ORDER IS THE RISK, and it must be said in the explanation rather than
discovered later: a greedy method that removes cells commits early. The first
guide's mistakes are permanent and every later guide inherits them. Whatever
is built must be able to say how much the answer would change if the order
changed, or the confidence it reports is not confidence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE GRAPH QUESTION, ANSWERED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    "is there a way to apply network theory and or graph learning to solv
     this problem of annotation?"

YES, AND IT IS THE SAME ALGORITHM. The third premise of the sudoku
description -- cells that look alike carry alike, wherever they sit -- IS a
graph statement. Written as one it becomes label propagation on a
k-nearest-neighbour graph (Zhu & Ghahramani 2003, Zhou et al. 2004), and the
well constraint becomes the class-mass normalisation step from that same
literature.

Two lines of reasoning that arrived independently turned out to be one
method, which is the best evidence available that it is the right one.

The other graph reading -- cells and `(well, guide)` slots as a bipartite
min-cost flow -- is what `assign_well` already does per well. It is kept as
the PROJECTION rather than the whole method because a hard assignment cannot
abstain, and abstention is a requirement.

A GRAPH NEURAL NETWORK WAS CONSIDERED AND REJECTED: the labels are noisy
pseudo-labels from anchors, the sample is thousands not millions, and the
result would be uninterpretable in a way that matters for a method deciding
what every downstream number means. Label propagation has no trained
weights, is convex, and its answer traces back to the anchors that made it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`spacr/sudoku.py`

  * `anchors_for` -- anchors from wells where the guide is a large share,
    because a top-scoring cell in a 70% well is probably that guide and in a
    5% well is probably not.
  * `similarity_graph` -- MUTUAL kNN with a self-tuning heat kernel. Mutual
    so an outlier can end up with no edges at all, which is the honest
    answer; self-tuning because a screen's cells do not have one density.
  * `propagate` -- Zhou's iteration, returning UNNORMALISED mass so that a
    cell which received almost nothing stays visibly so.
  * `constrain_to_fractions` -- the sudoku step, IPF onto the counts.
  * `sudoku` / `sudoku_all` -- with both scores kept apart and abstention on
    two distinct grounds: unlike every anchor, or a coin flip.

THE MITIGATIONS ARE IN THE CODE, not in a comment about future work. The
score is excluded from the graph by default and the report says so when it
is not, which is the circularity mitigation. Order sensitivity is measured
rather than argued about. Abstention is on two grounds because one number
cannot express both.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND THE VALIDATION, WHICH FOUND TWO REAL BUGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`spacr/annotation_validation.py` scores every strategy on the same screens,
against two baselines that use no cell measurement at all, and against a
permutation null.

IT EARNED ITS PLACE IMMEDIATELY, twice:

  1. The FIRST version of the generator emitted each guide's cells as a
     contiguous block, so the row order carried the answer. It was caught by
     the `no_effect` scenario -- features containing nothing -- reporting 85%
     precision, which is impossible. Every earlier number was worthless.
  2. `sudoku_all` scored at the null: gain +0.00. Running the per-round call
     with ONE guide made the well constraint normalise each row over a
     single column, so every posterior was exactly 1.0 and the first guide
     claimed the whole screen. A posterior is a comparison, and comparing a
     guide against nothing returns the prior.

Measured after both fixes, precision on annotated cells:

    scenario      majority baseline    sudoku      sudoku_all
    clean               46%             70%           71%
    no_effect           44%             47%           48%
    realistic           33%             46%           45%

The `no_effect` row is the one that matters most: with no signal in the
features, sudoku does NOT beat the fractions. A method that did would be
reading its own anchors.
