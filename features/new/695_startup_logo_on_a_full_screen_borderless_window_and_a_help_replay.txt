--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE STATE IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

694 shows the photomosaic centred on the loading cover, holds it at least
0.6 s after the first paint (longer while loading), then blows it away in
about 1.85 s while the live window appears under the front. There is no way
to watch it again after start-up, and its timing follows loading, not a
fixed schedule.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The maintainer wants the start to read as a deliberate title sequence on the
whole screen, at a fixed and calm pace, and to be able to show it on demand
(for talks, demos and tutorials) without restarting spaCR.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Splash surface. Show the start-up sequence on a frameless window
   (Qt.FramelessWindowHint) sized to the available geometry of the screen the
   app opens on, NOT in full-screen mode (no showFullScreen, no exclusive
   full-screen; the task bar or dock may stay). The spaCR logo photomosaic
   ("the spacr icon") sits in its centre.
2. Fixed timeline, measured from the first painted frame of the logo:
   - 0.0 - 2.0 s: the logo holds still in the centre.
   - 2.0 - 6.0 s: the blow-away runs for 4.0 s (stretch the 694 motion: wind
     front, lift, flip, spin, fly, fade, from about 1.85 s to 4.0 s).
   - 3.0 s (1 s into the blow-away): the spaCR window starts to become visible
     under the tiles.
   - 5.0 s (3 s into the blow-away): the spaCR window is fully visible
     (its fade-in runs 3.0 -> 5.0 s; choose the easing, e.g. smoothstep).
   - 6.0 s (4 s into the blow-away): the last tile has gone; the splash
     window closes.
   If loading is not finished at 2.0 s, keep the logo holding until it is,
   then run the same 4 s sequence (the window must not appear half-built).
   A click or a key still skips to the end.
3. Help menu: add "Show startup animation" (wording to confirm) to the Help
   dropdown. It replays the same 6 s sequence over the current window on a
   borderless full-screen-size surface, then returns the user exactly where
   they were (focus, open dialogs untouched). It plays even when the
   Startup animation preference is off, but Reduce motion shows the logo
   still for 2 s and then a plain 1 s cross-fade instead (or ask the
   maintainer).
4. Keep the existing switches: Preferences > Animation > Startup animation,
   Reduce motion, SPACR_NO_SPLASH=1 and offscreen disable the start-up run.
5. Strings through tr(); new public docstrings into an API arrival contract.
   WS translates and registers them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO KNOW IT WORKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- A test drives the timeline with a fake clock and asserts: the splash
  window is frameless and its geometry equals the screen available geometry;
  the window is not full-screen (windowState has no WindowFullScreen); no tile
  moves before 2.0 s; window opacity is 0 at 3.0 s, rises monotonically, is
  1.0 at 5.0 s; no tile is drawn after 6.0 s and the splash window is closed.
- A test for the Help action: it exists in the Help menu, replays the
  sequence, and leaves the main window state unchanged afterwards.
- A real launch on WS, recorded on the GNOME capture host with SPACR_NO_SPLASH
  unset, measured with the capture gate: hold 2.0 s +/- 0.1, tiles gone at
  6.0 s +/- 0.1, distinct frames >= 28 fps through the sequence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELIBERATELY NOT DONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Whether the MAIN window itself should also open borderless and screen-sized
  after the splash (rather than at its remembered geometry) is not decided;
  the request reads as the splash surface. Ask the maintainer before changing
  the main window.
