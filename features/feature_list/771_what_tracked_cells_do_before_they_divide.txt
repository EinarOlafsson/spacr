WHAT TRACKED CELLS DO BEFORE THEY DIVIDE: DIVISION-ALIGNED TRAJECTORIES WITH CONTROLS
===================================================================================
Lifecycle: future
Status: IN PROGRESS (alpha) 2026-11-04 — library module spacr/division_analysis.py and its tests are on branch division-analysis; no GUI surface, setting or screen yet, so nothing is registered with the alpha gate.
Requested: maintainer, 2026-11-04 (BMC Bioinformatics paper, Fig 7 "Timepose applied: what happens before cells divide"; analysis approved on gold tracks, no model).
Owner: Home (division-analysis worktree).
Related: 426 timeflows (the tracker whose output this reads), 535 cell-cycle phase, 539 bleach correction, lineage trees.

WHAT THE STATE IS
spacr/division_analysis.py turns a tracked label stack (label value = track id) plus a lineage table
(label, t_start, t_end, parent; CTC man_track semantics) into a controlled answer to "does this
measurement change before division more than in cells that are not about to divide?":
- measure_label_stack, add_motion_features: area, major/minor axis, aspect ratio, eccentricity,
  circularity, solidity, mean intensity normalised to the frame median; speed, displacement, turning.
- division_events, median_cycle_frames: fission (parent ends, two daughters next frame) and budding
  (mother continues, each bud one event); t_div = last frame before a daughter is seen.
- control_slots, match_controls: same-movie, time-overlapping, size-matched (+/-25%) tracks that do
  not divide in the window or the horizon after their pseudo-division time.
- shuffled_alignment: the dividing tracks aligned on a random time away from division (null control).
- align_units, unit_changes: rel_t trajectories (daughters after 0) and late-vs-baseline change
  (log ratio for positive measures, difference otherwise).
- effect_size (Hedges g, bootstrap 95% CI, large-sample variance), random_effects_meta
  (DerSimonian-Laird, tau2, Q, I2).
- cell_cycles, size_control_fits: birth vs division size, added size; sizer/adder/timer slopes.
tests/test_division_analysis.py builds synthetic lineages with a planted pre-division change and
checks that the pipeline finds it while the shuffled control does not, plus the meta-analysis and
size-control slopes against hand-computed cases.

The first use is the Fig 7 reanalysis in
/nas_mnt/data/sync/papers/BMCspacr/reanalysis/division_biology/ (REPORT.md there, driver code in code/).

WHY IT MATTERS
Tracking is only worth its cost when the tracks answer a biological question. This is the question
almost every time-lapse user asks first (what changes before a cell divides), and without matched and
shuffled controls the usual answer is a growth curve mistaken for a pre-division signal.
