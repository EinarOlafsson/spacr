--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY EVERY ROW REPAINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The backdrop (AmbientWidget) is the bottom child of each screen and the page
panels above it are translucent (pane opacity 60 % by default). Qt's raster
backing store is one flat image: when the backdrop calls update() on its whole
rectangle, every widget that overlaps that rectangle and is not opaque has to
be painted again on top, so each backdrop frame re-runs the paint of every
label, button, switch and tile on screen. Earlier work (_QuietBackgrounds,
_CardTints) made each of those paints cheaper; none of it stopped them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DESIGN DECISIONS (made 2026-10-26 while the maintainer was asleep)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1. Held rows: the backdrop repaints only where nothing is drawn over it.
    Once the window is calm, the backdrop works out which parts of its area
    have screen content drawn over them (cards, panels, rows, labels, buttons,
    switches, tiles: every widget that paints something, taken whole) and
    which parts show the backdrop alone (margins, gaps between cards, the
    page around them). Each frame it updates only the second region, so Qt
    repaints nothing that sits on the first. Under the content the backdrop
    shows the frame that was on screen when the hold began, and paints that
    same frame whenever a row repaints for its own reasons (hover, typing, a
    value change), so the pixels under every row stay consistent.

    Why this design and not the others listed:
    - A cached static BLUR behind the panels (the wording of the option) would
      change how every translucent row looks: the rows would sit over a
      blurred picture instead of the sharp backdrop they sit over today, and
      the screenshot-equivalence check would fail by design. The held frame
      is the exact picture that was under the rows, so at the moment the hold
      begins the window is pixel-identical to the per-frame paint (tested).
      A blur can be added later as a look preference if wanted.
    - Cached row LAYERS (render each panel once into an image and reuse it)
      need Qt to tell us when anything inside a panel changes. For widgets
      Qt does not: QGraphicsEffect on a widget re-renders its source on every
      paint (probed: 1,211 child paints in 2 s with an effect against 1,231
      without), and a label's setText() raises no event a filter can see. A
      stale row is a correctness bug; a held backdrop is at worst a still
      patch of background.
    - A low-rate snapshot UNDER the panels (for example 2-4 fps) costs about
      200-800 row paints a second and makes the backdrop visibly step behind
      the rows. It was kept as the fallback if the held frame looked wrong.
    - Exact live animation under translucent rows without repainting them
      needs the backdrop on a GPU texture layer composited by Qt
      (QOpenGLWidget/QRhiWidget). That changes how the whole window is
      flushed on every platform and cannot be verified on the Mac or Windows
      from Home, so it is out of scope here.

D2. What counts as drawn over. Walked from the backdrop's siblings in paint
    order, clipped by their parents: a widget is taken whole when it fills
    its background, claims opaque painting or has a mask; when its style
    sheet background paints a pixel (probed at up to 64x64 and cached per
    widget and size); when its class overrides paintEvent in Python; when it
    is a leaf (no visible widget children) other than a plain QWidget; or
    when it is a container that is not a plain layout container. Plain
    containers (QWidget, QFrame, QScrollArea, QAbstractScrollArea,
    QStackedWidget, QSplitter) are walked into, and only a QFrame's frame
    ring is taken. Getting this wrong cannot make a pixel wrong: a painted
    widget left out of the region is simply repainted each frame as before,
    and a blank area put in it shows a still patch of backdrop.

D3. When the hold is used. Only with a shading thread, square corners, card
    compositing (_CardTints) off, the backdrop running, and the window calm:
    1.5 s with no pointer influence on the field, no click ripple or field
    grab, no popup wave and no pending impulse, and 1.5 s after the field
    (re)started. Any of those releases the hold at once so the interactive
    field shows everywhere while it is being used; the hold comes back 1.5 s
    after the last one. A field whose timer is found stopped (stop(), or a
    caller stopping the timer directly) drops the hold and paints classically,
    so the stopped backdrop's still and partial-frame cache are unchanged. The covered region is
    walked again once a second; if it changed (a scroll, a section folded, a
    screen switched, a resize) the hold is released and re-armed on the new
    region.

D4. No pop when the hold ends. Releasing cross-fades the held frame into the
    live one over 0.25 s under the content (a layer drawn at rising opacity),
    so the backdrop under the rows never jumps. Starting a hold is seamless
    by construction (the held frame is the frame just painted).

D5. Pause on battery, every OS. On macOS and Windows (and other platforms)
    psutil.sensors_battery(), where on battery means power_plugged is False.
    On Linux the kernel's /sys/class/power_supply is read directly instead,
    skipping supplies whose scope is "Device": psutil on Linux takes the first
    supply named like a battery, and on Home's workstation it reported a
    Bluetooth mouse ("65 %, power_plugged=False"), which would have stopped
    the backdrop on every desktop with a wireless mouse or keyboard. With a
    system battery present, any Mains/USB supply online means AC; with none
    reported, the battery's own status decides (Discharging). Read at most
    every 5 s, joined to the existing macOS Low Power Mode / Reduce Motion
    check (_system_asks_for_stillness), so the backdrop's run check and each
    tick consult both; a held backdrop asks again every 5 s and resumes on AC.
    No battery, an unknown plug state or any error answers "not on battery".
    The spaCR Reduce motion preference is untouched. SPACR_BATTERY_PAUSE=0 in
    the environment keeps the animation on battery; tests/qt/conftest.py sets
    it for every Qt test, so a developer's laptop on battery does not stop
    every backdrop a test expects to run, and the battery tests remove it and
    mock the sensor.

D6. Pacing while held. Measured after D1, the remaining idle cost was the
    field itself, not the rows: at 60 fps the shading thread took 12-20 % of
    a core and the GUI thread 12-13 % (Linux, 1600x1000). While the rows are
    held the field is capped at _HOLD_FPS = 20 frames per second (a third of
    60 Hz), the way a run already caps it at 30; a stir restores the full
    rate on the next tick. What still moves is the page around the cards.
    30 fps was measured too (Home 21.5 %, Mask 13.8 %, Measure 17.7 %) and
    misses the 10 % target; 20 fps was the highest rate that met it on Mask
    and Measure.

D7. The measuring tool is tools/measure_idle_repaint.py: the real MainWindow
    with settings in a throwaway folder and the tours marked seen, Home, Mask
    and Measure, paints per second by class, backdrop frames, and process
    CPU (user + system over wall). Offscreen, because under Xvfb without a
    window manager the window is never exposed and the backdrop never ticks.

D8. Second round (2026-10-26, coordinator: Home and Measure clearly under
    10 %, attack the backdrop's own cost, cap stays at 20 fps). Profiling
    the held field showed the cost was not the shading (about 1 ms a frame
    on the shading thread, 1.5-2 % of a core) but the GUI drawing the
    uncovered page: the data-art frame is shaded at the Detail preference's
    resolution (1038x616 for a 1600x949 window here) and upscaled with
    bilinear filtering on every paint, and Qt's raster engine split that
    large blend across its thread pool (eight "Thread (pooled)" threads at
    0.8 % each on Home). Done:
    - _blit_by_rectangle: with a flat page and the buffered blit, the live
      region is painted in stripes of at most 64 rows, each clipped and drawn
      with the same whole-widget transform, so the pool is not woken and
      every pixel equals the region-wide paint (tested exactly). Home's live
      region: 2.17 ms of CPU a frame through the region, 2.00 ms a rectangle
      at a time, 1.17 ms in 64-row stripes (best of six interleaved rounds).
    - The covered region is snapped outward to an 8 px grid: the few-pixel
      gaps between neighbouring rows and tiles are held with them, and the
      live region falls from 160 to 71 rectangles on Home and from 83 to 22
      on Measure; fewer layout containers are crossed (Mask 13.1 -> 6.6,
      Measure 14.1 -> 10.3 paints per frame).
    Evaluated and not done, with the numbers:
    - Shading only the uncovered region: the whole shade is about 1 ms a
      frame (fill 0.2 ms, dot stamping the rest), so culling saves well under
      1 % of a core, and the frame is also the still a paused field shows.
    - Skipping frames whose uncovered pixels are unchanged: in the default
      field 3 % of the pixels but 85 % of the 16 px tiles change every frame
      (the lens moves every dot a little), so almost nothing is skipped.
    - A lower internal resolution: the frame is already shaded below the
      window size and upscaled; the upscale is the cost, so going lower
      would not remove it.

D9. A paused field no longer paints. test_a_run_still_pauses_the_backdrop
    failed once under load because, when a held field stopped, the release
    asked for a repaint (so a run pausing the backdrop cost one frame), and
    under load the window could reach the hold before the pause. Now a field
    that stops while held (stop(), or its timer found stopped) settles into
    the still without a repaint: the held frame becomes the frame the
    stopped field shows (its still and partial-frame cache), so the rows keep
    exactly the pixels under them and nothing is painted. Pinned by
    test_pausing_a_held_field_paints_nothing_more (a mutation that repaints
    on the pause fails it). The hold also waits 1.5 s after the field starts.

D10. GUI stalls during the impulse lens (third round, 2026-10-26; WS tutorial
    lane, spacr/resources/AI/HANDOFF.md item 4: 0.13-0.18 s GUI stalls with
    the paint rate dipping in lessons 13, 34, 60, 68 and 70, the other thread
    always in _shade -> _frame_impulse_lens, field density 0.1).
    - The CPU path does not hold the interpreter lock for long. Its grain
      kernels are numba nogil; with a sampler thread timing every 1 ms wait
      for the lock while impulse-lens frames shade with impulses and ripples
      firing, the longest wait was 2.0 ms with the kernel and 4.2 ms on the
      NumPy fallback used before the kernel compiles (1038x616 and 2560x1440
      requests). At density 0.1 a whole frame shades in about 1 ms.
    - The GPU path does. At density 0.1 Automatic sends the impulse lens to
      the GPU (cost table: 6.06 ms CPU against 0.82 ms GPU at 1920x1080,
      density 0.25), and a GPU frame holds the lock from submission to the
      framebuffer readback, because moderngl does not release it. On a card
      that is busy elsewhere -- the capture host records and encodes the
      screen -- the readback waits, and the GUI thread waits with it. That
      matches the dumps: the shading thread inside _frame_impulse_lens, the
      GUI stalled for one frame's readback at a time.
    - Fix: _graphics_frame times each GPU frame; one that holds the lock
      longer than 20 ms (about one 60 Hz frame; the RTX 3090 Ti audit's
      slowest impulse-lens frame was 9.2 ms at 3840x2160) sends the engine
      to its CPU path for 30 s, then the GPU is tried again. The first frame
      of a renderer compiles its shaders and is not judged. No GPU was used
      at Home: the GPU path was exercised with a stand-in renderer whose
      frame holds the lock for 150 ms (a C-level loop), which reproduces the
      reported shape.
    - Tried and dropped: lowering the interpreter switch interval to 1 ms
      while a frame shades (a "shading turn" in gil_priority). It made no
      difference on the CPU path (stalls the same within noise), because
      that path is not where the lock is held.
    - Also measured: with a pure-Python worker holding the responsive-GUI
      claim (a stand-in for a Python-bound run) and the pointer clicking and
      moving over Home at 2560x1440 every 0.4 s, the GUI stalls up to
      160 ms with the backdrop on (p95 66, p99 80 ms) against 13 ms with it
      off. That is the GUI's own per-frame Python waiting for the lock
      behind the worker, not the lens. Keeping the hold through a run and
      repainting only the live region on field input were tried: p95 fell
      to about 40 ms but the count of gaps over 100 ms rose, so neither was
      kept. Kept: the hold's once-a-second walk is not repeated while a run
      paces the field. This contention is left for 641/gil_priority work.

    GUI-thread heartbeat (2 ms QTimer) gaps, offscreen, Home's workstation,
    impulse lens with impulses firing:

    Case                                                   p95     p99     max     gaps>60 ms
    -----------------------------------------------------  ------  ------  ------  ----------
    GPU stand-in holding the lock 150 ms/frame, no backoff 164.1   171.7   188.1   51 of 138
    same, with the backoff (this change)                     3.4     4.6    50.4    0 of 3,773
    CPU path, 2560x1440 window, clicks every 0.4 s          14.1    17.5    32.9    0
    control: backdrop off, same clicks                       2.1     2.1     5.4    0
    (1600x949 host for the stand-in rows, 8 s; 20 s for the window rows;
    times in ms.) Without the backoff the field painted 51 frames in 8 s;
    with it, 519.

    Pinned by test_a_slow_gpu_frame_sends_the_lens_to_the_cpu and
    test_the_gui_never_waits_long_on_the_lens_during_impulses (stand-in GPU
    at 150 ms a frame, impulses every 0.2 s, 3 s: at most the first two
    frames reach 100 ms; both tests fail with the backoff disabled), in
    tests/qt/test_a_slow_gpu_frame_sends_the_lens_to_the_cpu.py. The
    WS capture host should re-record lessons 13, 34, 60, 68 and 70 on this

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1. Idle, animation on, default dark theme: Home, Mask and Measure each run
    under 10 % of a CPU core on Linux offscreen with tools/measure_idle_
    repaint.py, and widget paints per backdrop frame drop from ~160 to a

    non-backdrop widget paints per backdrop frame.
A4. A visual-equivalence test: at the moment the hold begins, a translucent
    row and its card are pixel-identical (within a tight tolerance) to the
    per-frame paint of the same frame; and a row repainting for its own
    reasons shows the held frame under it, not a newer one.
A5. Battery: the backdrop stops on battery and resumes on AC, tested with a
    mocked sensor; the Reduce motion preference stays as the user set it.
A6. The ambient, backdrop, theme and accessibility cohorts and the Home, Mask
    and Measure layout tests pass, with no test, budget or ratchet weakened.
A7. Screenshots light and dark, before and after, checked by eye.
A8. The Mac lane re-measures natively (Retina 2x) and records the numbers
    here; target under 10 % of a core on Home, Mask and Measure.
